Q1

A 72-year-old man is evaluated before elective open colectomy for colon adenocarcinoma. He has hypertension, type 2 diabetes mellitus, and a history of myocardial infarction 4 years ago with placement of a drug-eluting coronary stent. He walks 3 miles daily without dyspnea or chest pain. Blood pressure is 132/78 mm Hg and pulse is 68/min. ECG shows normal sinus rhythm with old Q waves in the inferior leads. Serum creatinine is 0.9 mg/dL.

What is the most appropriate next step in preoperative cardiac evaluation?

A. Coronary angiography

B. Exercise stress testing

C. No additional cardiac testing

D. Transthoracic echocardiography

E. Dobutamine stress echocardiography

Answer: C

Explanation:
This patient has good functional capacity (>4 METs), stable cardiac disease, and no active cardiac symptoms. According to perioperative evaluation principles, additional cardiac testing is unnecessary because it is unlikely to alter management.

Why not the others?

A. Coronary angiography is reserved for standard cardiology indications, not routine surgical clearance.

B. Stress testing is unnecessary in asymptomatic patients with adequate functional capacity.

D. Echocardiography is indicated only if valvular disease or heart failure is suspected.

E. Dobutamine stress testing is used for patients unable to exercise and only when results would change management.

High-Yield Pearl:
Patients with >4 METs and no active cardiac symptoms generally proceed directly to surgery.

Difficulty: Moderate

Q2

A 68-year-old woman is scheduled for elective total hip arthroplasty. She has chronic atrial fibrillation treated with apixaban. Renal function is normal. She has no history of stroke within the past year.

What is the most appropriate perioperative anticoagulation management?

A. Continue apixaban through surgery

B. Stop apixaban 48 hours before surgery

C. Stop apixaban 7 days before surgery

D. Bridge with therapeutic heparin

E. Switch to aspirin only

Answer: B

Explanation:
Major orthopedic surgery carries a high bleeding risk. Direct oral anticoagulants such as apixaban are generally discontinued approximately 48 hours before surgery in patients with normal renal function. Bridging is not routinely recommended for most patients taking DOACs.

Why not the others?

A. Continuing anticoagulation markedly increases surgical bleeding.

C. Seven days is unnecessarily long for normal renal function.

D. Bridging increases bleeding without reducing thromboembolic events in most DOAC-treated patients.

E. Aspirin does not provide equivalent stroke prevention for atrial fibrillation.

High-Yield Pearl:
DOACs usually require temporary interruption rather than bridging.

Difficulty: Moderate

Q3

C. Anastomotic leak

D. Pulmonary embolism

E. Acute cholangitis

Early postoperative fever (<24 hours) is most often due to atelectasis rather than infection.

Q7

A 63-year-old woman is scheduled for an elective right hemicolectomy for colon cancer. She has hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and type 2 diabetes mellitus controlled with metformin. She reports walking 5–6 blocks daily and climbing two flights of stairs without chest pain or dyspnea. Blood pressure is 128/76 mm Hg, pulse is 72/min, and oxygen saturation is 98% on room air. Which of the following best describes her functional capacity?

A. <2 METs

B. 2–3 METs

C. ≥4 METs

D. ≥10 METs

E. Functional capacity cannot be determined

Answer: C

Explanation

Functional capacity is estimated using metabolic equivalents (METs). A patient able to climb two flights of stairs or walk briskly without symptoms has at least 4 METs, indicating adequate cardiopulmonary reserve. Patients with ≥4 METs and no active cardiac symptoms generally do not require further cardiac testing before intermediate-risk surgery.

Why the Others Are Incorrect

A. <2 METs indicates inability to perform basic activities such as dressing.

B. 2–3 METs corresponds to light household activities.

D. ≥10 METs includes strenuous sports such as swimming or running.

E. Functional capacity can clearly be estimated from her activity level.

High-Yield Pearl

Climbing two flights of stairs = approximately 4 METs, a classic Step 2 CK benchmark.

Difficulty: Easy

Q13

A 67-year-old man undergoes elective right hemicolectomy for colon adenocarcinoma. His postoperative course is uncomplicated. On postoperative day 1, he remains hemodynamically stable with adequate pain control. He asks why the nurses are encouraging him to get out of bed despite his discomfort.

Which postoperative complication is most effectively reduced by early ambulation?

A. Anastomotic leak

B. Deep venous thrombosis

C. Wound dehiscence

D. Acute kidney injury

E. Surgical site infection

Answer: B

Explanation

Early ambulation is a cornerstone of Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS) protocols. It improves venous return, reduces venous stasis, enhances pulmonary expansion, stimulates bowel motility, and decreases muscle deconditioning.

One of its greatest benefits is the reduction in deep venous thrombosis (DVT) and pulmonary embolism.

Why the Others Are Incorrect

A. Anastomotic leaks are related to tissue perfusion, tension, and surgical technique.

C. Ambulation does not increase or decrease wound dehiscence when performed appropriately.

D. AKI is primarily prevented by maintaining adequate perfusion and avoiding nephrotoxins.

E. Surgical site infections are reduced by proper sterile technique and antibiotic prophylaxis.

High-Yield Pearl

Early ambulation is one of the highest-yield ERAS interventions, reducing DVT, pulmonary complications, and postoperative ileus.

Difficulty: Easy
