Example:


The Question
A 64-year-old male is brought to the Emergency Department by his wife due to "confusion and acting strange" for the past 24 hours. The patient has a medical history of Type 2 Diabetes, Hypertension, and Stage 3 Chronic Kidney Disease. He was recently treated with a course of trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole for a urinary tract infection.


Physical Examination:

Vitals: Temp: 100.2°F | HR: 104 bpm | RR: 18/min | BP: 108/62 mmHg.

Mental Status: Disoriented to time and place; follows simple commands but is slow to respond.

Physical: Mucous membranes are dry. Skin turgor is poor. No focal neurological deficits are noted. Cardiac exam shows a soft systolic murmur. Lungs are clear to auscultation.

Laboratory Results:

Serum Sodium: 158 mEq/L (Normal: 135–145)

Serum Potassium: 4.1 mEq/L (Normal: 3.5–5.0)

Serum Glucose: 142 mg/dL (Normal: 70–100)

BUN / Creatinine: 52 mg/dL / 2.1 mg/dL (Baseline Cr: 1.4)

Urine Osmolality: 550 mOsm/kg



Question 1: Diagnosis & Initial Management
Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in the management of this patient’s hypernatremia?

A. Administration of IV 0.45% (half-normal) saline.
B. Administration of IV 0.9% (normal) saline.
C. Administration of IV 3% (hypertonic) saline.
D. Rapid correction with 5% Dextrose in water (D5W).
E. Desmopressin (dDAVP) administration.

Q1 Answer: B (Administration of IV 0.9% normal saline)
Reasoning: This is a classic Step 2 CK "trap." While the patient is hypernatremic (which usually requires free water or hypotonic fluids), the patient is hemodynamically unstable (tachycardic, borderline hypotensive, dry membranes).



Question 2: Pathophysiology (Follow-up)
The patient’s disorientation and elevated sodium levels are most likely the result of which mechanism?

A. Central Diabetes Insipidus.
B. Osmotic diuresis from hyperglycemia.
C. Impaired thirst mechanism and inadequate free water intake.
D. Syndrome of Inappropriate Antidiuretic Hormone (SIADH).
E. Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus.


Q2 Answer: C (Impaired thirst/Inadequate free water intake)Reasoning: The patient is elderly and has been ill (UTI). In the setting of high serum sodium ($>145$) and appropriately concentrated urine ($>300\text{--}600 \text{ mOsm/kg}$), the kidneys are working correctly to conserve water. The problem is a lack of intake.
