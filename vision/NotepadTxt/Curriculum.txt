1st year Year Topics:



Gross Anatomy & Embryology: Biochemistry & Genetics: Focuses on metabolic pathways (like the Krebs cycle), DNA replication, and inherited single-gene disorders.

Cell Biology & Histology: The microscopic study of tissues. You learn to identify "normal" under a microscope so you can eventually recognize "pathological" in M2.

Physiology: The study of how the body maintains homeostasis. This is the "physics" of medicine (e.g., pressure, flow, and resistance).

Musculoskeletal (MSK) & Integumentary: Bones, muscles, joints, and the skin.

Cardiovascular: The mechanics of the heart, blood vessels, and hemodynamics.

Respiratory: Mechanics of breathing, gas exchange, and acid-base balance.

Renal/Urinary: How the kidneys filter blood and regulate electrolytes.

Gastrointestinal (GI): Digestion, absorption, and the function of the liver and pancreas.

Reproductive & Endocrine: Hormonal regulation and the male/female reproductive tracts.

Neuroscience: The structure and function of the brain, spinal cord, and cranial nerves.

Ethics & Behavioral Science: Understanding patient-physician relationships, medical law, and basic psychology.

Introduction to Clinical Skills (Physical Exam): Learning how to use a stethoscope, perform a neurological exam, and take a patient history.

Medical Genetics: You will dive into the molecular basis of inheritance, focusing on things like Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium and the specific mutations behind diseases like Cystic Fibrosis.

Microbiology (Introduction): While the deep study of bacteria/viruses often happens in M2, M1 usually introduces the "Classification of Microbes" and basic immunology (how your T-cells and B-cells develop).

Pharmacology (Principles): You won't learn every drug yet, but you will learn Pharmacokinetics (how the body moves drugs) and Pharmacodynamics (how drugs affect the body).

Epidemiology & Biostatistics: This is a major part of the NBME exams. You must learn how to calculate Sensitivity, Specificity, and Odds Ratios.
