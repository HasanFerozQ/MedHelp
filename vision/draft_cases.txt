CASE 01: Hyponatremia & Hyperglycemia
Scenario: A 65-year-old male with Type 2 DM presents with confusion. Labs: Na 128 mEq/L, Glucose 900 mg/dL, K 4.2 mEq/L.
Q1: What is the corrected sodium level? (Formula: Na + 1.6 * (Glucose - 100) / 100)
A. 128
B. 140.8
C. 135
D. 145
Answer: B (128 + 1.6 * 8 = 128 + 12.8 = 140.8)
Q2: This condition is known as:
A. SIADH
B. Pseudohyponatremia
C. Hypertonic hyponatremia
D. Hypovolemic hyponatremia
Answer: C
Q3: What is the primary initial treatment for the confusion?
A. 3% Saline
B. Fluid restriction
C. Insulin and normal saline
D. Tolvaptan
Answer: C

CASE 05: NAGMA & Urine Anion Gap
Scenario: A patient has a pH of 7.30, HCO3 15, Cl 115, Na 140. Urine labs: Na 40, K 20, Cl 20.
Q1: Calculate the serum anion gap.
A. 5
B. 10
C. 15
D. 20
Answer: B (140 - (115+15) = 10. Normal gap.)
Q2: Calculate the Urine Anion Gap (UAG = Na + K - Cl).
A. -20
B. 0
C. +40
D. +60
Answer: C (40 + 20 - 20 = 40)
Q3: A positive UAG in the setting of NAGMA suggests:
A. Diarrhea
B. Renal Tubular Acidosis (RTA)
C. Addison's disease
D. Lactic acidosis
Answer: B (Negative UAG = Diarrhea; Positive UAG = RTA)
