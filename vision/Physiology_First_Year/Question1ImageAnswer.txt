Vignette:

A 62-year-old man with long-standing hypertension undergoes cardiac catheterization. During the procedure, a left ventricular pressure-volume loop is obtained to evaluate his cardiac function.

The Question:

Which point on the loop represents the closure of the mitral (bicuspid) valve?

A. Point A

B. Point B

C. Point C

D. Point D

Phase 3: Explanation & Answer Key

Correct Answer: A

Reasoning:

The mitral valve closes when left ventricular pressure exceeds left atrial pressure at the end of diastole. This occurs at the End-Diastolic Volume (EDV), which is the maximum volume the ventricle reaches (the rightmost point of the loop). This mechanical event produces the first heart sound ($S_1$) and marks the beginning of isovolumetric contraction.

Incorrect Answer Breakdown:

Point B (Aortic Valve Opens): At this point, ventricular pressure has climbed to meet aortic pressure. The aortic valve opens, and the ejection phase begins.

Point C (Aortic Valve Closes): This occurs at the End-Systolic Volume (ESV). The closure of the aortic valve produces the second heart sound ($S_2$) and marks the start of isovolumetric relaxation.

Point D (Mitral Valve Opens): As the ventricle relaxes, its pressure eventually falls below atrial pressure. The mitral valve opens here, allowing the ventricular filling phase (moving from D back to A) to begin.
