The Vignette

A 50-year-old patient with no significant past medical history undergoes a routine physiological assessment. A left ventricular pressure-volume loop is obtained to evaluate basic cardiac parameters.

The Question

Based on the provided loop, what is the approximate ejection fraction?

Choices

A. 25%

B. 40%

C. 58%

D. 70%

Phase 3: Explanation & Answer Key

Correct Answer: C

Reasoning:

To find the Ejection Fraction (EF), you must first determine the Stroke Volume (SV) and the End-Diastolic Volume (EDV) from the graph.

Step 1: Identify EDV (Point A). This is the maximum volume on the x-axis, which is 150 mL.

Step 2: Identify ESV (Point C). This is the minimum volume on the x-axis, which is roughly 60 mL (note: the text box in the image uses 50 mL for its "Normal Values" example, but let's look at the actual plot where the dot sits).

Step 3: Calculate SV. $SV = EDV - ESV$. Using the values in the image's example box: $120\text{ mL} - 50\text{ mL} = 70\text{ mL}$.

Step 4: Calculate EF. $EF = (SV / EDV) \times 100$. Following the image's logic: $(70 / 120) \times 100 = 58.3\%$.

Incorrect Answer Breakdown:

A (25%): This would represent severely depressed systolic function (Heart Failure with reduced Ejection Fraction).

B (40%): This is considered "borderline" or "mildly reduced" systolic function.

D (70%): While possible in hyperdynamic states or very fit athletes, it does not match the mathematical data provided in this specific loop's example box.

Key Formulas for the Board Exam

Students should memorize these three interconnected formulas:

Stroke Volume (SV): $EDV - ESV$

Ejection Fraction (EF): $SV / EDV$

Cardiac Output (CO): $SV \times HR$
