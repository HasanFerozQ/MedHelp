II. Physiology & Mechanics

Front: Definition of Residual Volume (RV)?

Back: Air remaining in lungs after maximal expiration.

Front: Formula for Functional Residual Capacity (FRC)?

Back: $FRC = ERV + RV$.

Front: Changes in FRC in a patient with emphysema?

Back: FRC increases due to loss of elastic recoil.

Front: Definition of physiologic dead space?

Back: Total volume of gas not participating in gas exchange (Anatomic + Alveolar).

Front: Effect of exercise on the hemoglobin-oxygen dissociation curve?

Back: Right shift (increased $H^+$, $CO_2$, temperature, and 2,3-BPG).

Front: Form in which the majority of $CO_2$ is transported in the blood?

Back: Bicarbonate ($HCO_3^-$).

Front: Enzyme that converts $CO_2$ and $H_2O$ to $H_2CO_3$ in RBCs?

Back: Carbonic anhydrase.

Front: Definition of the Haldane effect?

Back: Deoxygenation of blood increases its ability to carry $CO_2$.

Front: High altitude compensation: Erythropoietin levels?

Back: Increased (leads to polycythemia).

Front: Perfusion vs. Ventilation at the apex of the lung?

Back: Both are lower than the base, but $V/Q$ is higher (approx. 3.0).

Front: Response of pulmonary vasculature to hypoxia?

Back: Hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction (shunts blood to ventilated areas).

VIII. Critical Care & Miscellaneous

Front: Pathogenesis of ARDS?

Back: Diffuse alveolar damage (DAD) leading to hyaline membrane formation.

Front: Characteristic ABG finding in early Pulmonary Embolism?

Back: Respiratory alkalosis (due to hyperventilation).

Front: Definition of a Tension Pneumothorax?

Back: Air enters pleural space but cannot exit, causing mediastinal shift.

Front: Direction of tracheal deviation in Tension Pneumothorax?

Back: Away from the side of the lesion.

Front: Definition of Chylothorax?

Back: Pleural effusion containing lymph (milky appearance), usually from thoracic duct injury.

Front: Light’s Criteria: Protein ratio for exudative effusion?

Back: Pleural fluid protein / Serum protein > 0.5.

Front: Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA) is associated with?

Back: Obesity, loud snoring, daytime somnolence, and systemic hypertension.

Front: First-line treatment for OSA?

Back: CPAP (Continuous Positive Airway Pressure).

Front: Effect of Carbon Monoxide (CO) on the oxygen dissociation curve?

Back: Left shift (inhibits oxygen unloading).

Front: Physical exam finding in CO poisoning?

Back: "Cherry-red" skin discoloration (rarely seen in living patients).

Front: Treatment for Cyanide poisoning?

Back: Nitrites (to induce methemoglobin), Thiosulfate, or Hydroxocobalamin.

Front: Difference between Hypoxemia and Hypoxia?

Back: Hypoxemia is low $PaO_2$; Hypoxia is low $O_2$ delivery to tissues.

Front: Formula for A-a gradient?

Back: $PAO_2 - PaO_2$.

Front: Causes of hypoxemia with a NORMAL A-a gradient?

Back: Hypoventilation and High Altitude.

Front: Mechanism of action of Albuterol?

Back: $\beta_2$-agonist (bronchodilation).

Front: Mechanism of action of Tiotropium?

Back: Long-acting Muscarinic Antagonist (LAMA).

Front: Mechanism of action of Montelukast?

Back: Leukotriene receptor antagonist.

Front: Major side effect of inhaled corticosteroids?

Back: Oral candidiasis (thrush).

Front: Drug of choice for immediate relief of acute asthma symptoms?

Back: Short-acting $\beta_2$-agonist (SABA).

Front: First-line long-term therapy for persistent asthma?

Back: Inhaled corticosteroids (ICS).

Front: Definition of Compliance?

Back: Change in volume divided by change in pressure ($\Delta V / \Delta P$).

Front: Condition characterized by pathologically increased lung compliance?

Back: Emphysema.

Front: Condition characterized by pathologically decreased lung compliance?

Back: Pulmonary fibrosis.

Front: Primary factor determining airway resistance?

Back: Airway radius (Resistance $\propto 1/r^4$).

Front: Part of the tracheobronchial tree with the highest individual airway resistance?

Back: Medium-sized bronchi (generations 2–5).

Front: Part of the tracheobronchial tree with the lowest total resistance?

Back: Terminal bronchioles (due to massive parallel arrangement).

Front: Equal Pressure Point (EPP) in healthy lungs during forced expiration?

Back: Occurs in cartilaginous airways to prevent collapse.

Front: Shift of the Equal Pressure Point in emphysema?

Back: Moves distally toward smaller, non-cartilaginous airways (leading to airway collapse).

Front: Primary determinant of $PAO_2$ (Alveolar Oxygen)?

Back: The Alveolar Gas Equation: $PAO_2 = FiO_2(P_{atm} - PH_2O) - (PaCO_2 / R)$.

Front: Normal value for the A-a gradient in a young adult?

Back: 5–15 mmHg.

Front: Calculation for the age-adjusted upper limit of normal A-a gradient?

Back: $(Age / 4) + 4$.

Front: Only cause of hypoxemia that cannot be corrected with 100% $O_2$?

Back: Right-to-Left Shunt.

Front: Oxygen content formula ($CaO_2$)?

Back: $(1.34 \times Hb \times SaO_2) + (0.003 \times PaO_2)$.

XVI. West's Respiratory Physiology: Gas Exchange & Transport

Front: Definition of Diffusing Capacity ($DL_{CO}$)?

Back: Measurement of the lung's ability to transfer gas from inspired air to RBCs using Carbon Monoxide.

Front: Condition that increases $DL_{CO}$?

Back: Alveolar hemorrhage (e.g., Goodpasture) or polycythemia.

Front: Conditions that decrease $DL_{CO}$?

Back: Emphysema, Pulmonary Fibrosis, and Pulmonary Embolism.

Front: Oxygen transport: Definition of Methemoglobinemia?

Back: Iron in hemoglobin is in the $Fe^{3+}$ (ferric) state instead of $Fe^{2+}$ (ferrous).

Front: Classic clinical sign of Methemoglobinemia?

Back: Cyanosis unresponsive to supplemental oxygen and "chocolate-colored" blood.

Front: Treatment for Methemoglobinemia?

Back: Methylene blue or Vitamin C.

Front: Definition of the Bohr Effect?

Back: Increased $CO_2$ and decreased pH lower hemoglobin's affinity for $O_2$.

Front: Effect of 2,3-BPG on the Oxygen-Hemoglobin dissociation curve?

Back: Stabilizes the T-state (deoxygenated) and shifts the curve to the right.

Front: Respiratory center responsible for the rhythm of breathing?

Back: Pre-Bötzinger complex in the medulla.

Front: Primary stimulus for central chemoreceptors?

Back: $H^+$ concentration in the brain extracellular fluid (derived from arterial $CO_2$).

Front: Primary stimulus for peripheral chemoreceptors (Carotid/Aortic bodies)?

Back: Decreased $PaO_2$ (< 60 mmHg).

XXII. Advanced Physiology & Gas Laws

Front: Meaning of a "Left Shift" in the P50 of hemoglobin?

Back: Hemoglobin has a higher affinity for $O_2$ (holds on tighter).

Front: Boyle’s Law application in the lungs?

Back: Pressure and volume are inversely proportional; used to measure FRC via plethysmography.

Front: Definition of "Closing Volume"?

Back: The volume at which the airways in the dependent (lower) parts of the lung begin to close.

Front: Effect of aging on Closing Volume?

Back: Closing volume increases with age.

Front: Definition of Physiological Shunt ($Q_s/Q_t$)?

Back: The fraction of the total cardiac output that traverses the systemic circulation without being oxygenated.

XXVIII. Advanced Clinical Pathophysiology

Front: Definition of the "Shunt Equation"?

Back: $Qs/Qt = (CcO_2 - CaO_2) / (CcO_2 - CvO_2)$.

Front: Primary cause of hypoxemia in a patient with a massive pulmonary embolism?

Back: $V/Q$ mismatch (increased dead space).

Front: The "Haldane Effect" describes $CO_2$ release in which organ?

Back: The lungs (binding of $O_2$ to Hb promotes $CO_2$ dissociation).

Front: Definition of "Orthodeoxia"?

Back: Hypoxemia that worsens when moving from a supine to an upright position (seen in Hepatopulmonary Syndrome).

Front: Definition of "Platypnea"?

Back: Dyspnea that worsens when upright.

XXIX. Specific Histopathology & Cytology

Front: Cytological finding in Bronchoalveolar Lavage (BAL) of a patient with Asbestosis?

Back: Ferruginous bodies (asbestos fibers coated with iron).

Front: Histological finding in Silicosis?

Back: Whorled collagenous nodules with peripheral "eggshell" calcification.

Front: Appearance of Coal Worker’s Pneumoconiosis (CWP) on histology?

Back: Carbon-laden macrophages (coal macules) around bronchioles.

Front: Most common location for "Caplan Syndrome" nodules?

Back: Peripheral lung (seen in patients with both Rheumatoid Arthritis and Pneumoconiosis).

XXX. Pulmonary Pharmacology: Toxicity & Interactions

Front: Drug-induced interstitial lung disease: Mechanism of Amiodarone?

Back: Phospholipidosis (accumulation of foamy macrophages in the alveoli).

Front: Respiratory side effect of Methotrexate?

Back: Hypersensitivity pneumonitis (granulomatous reaction).

Front: Mechanism of Nitrofurantoin-induced lung injury?

Back: Direct oxidative damage to lung parenchyma (can be acute or chronic).

Front: Why is the use of 100% Oxygen avoided in patients with Paraquat poisoning?

Back: Oxygen exacerbates the oxidative lung injury caused by Paraquat.

XXXI. High-Yield Pediatric Respiratory Facts

Front: Most common cause of bronchiolitis in infants?

Back: Respiratory Syncytial Virus (RSV).

Front: Common complication of Neonatal Respiratory Distress Syndrome (NRDS) treatment?

Back: Bronchopulmonary Dysplasia (due to oxygen toxicity and barotrauma).

Front: Most common cause of Stridor in a neonate?

Back: Laryngomalacia (collapsible supraglottic structures).

Front: Character of the cough in Bordetella pertussis?

Back: Paroxysmal cough followed by an inspiratory "whoop."

XXXII. Final "Board-Style" Rapid Fire

Front: Most likely diagnosis: Smoker, hypercalcemia, central lung mass?

Back: Squamous cell carcinoma.

Front: Most likely diagnosis: Non-smoker, female, peripheral lung mass?

Back: Adenocarcinoma.

Front: Most likely diagnosis: Weight loss, night sweats, apical cavitary lesion?

Back: Reactivation (Secondary) Tuberculosis.

Front: Most likely diagnosis: Sudden pleuritic chest pain, tall thin male, hyperresonance?

Back: Spontaneous Pneumothorax.

Front: Most likely diagnosis: 24 hours post-femur fracture, confusion, petechiae?

Back: Fat Embolism Syndrome.
