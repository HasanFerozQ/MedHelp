Section 29: Pharmacological Targets

Front: Mechanism of "Sulfonamides"?

Back: PABA antimetabolites; inhibit Dihydropteroate synthase.

Front: Mechanism of "Trimethoprim"?

Back: Inhibits Dihydrofolate reductase.

Front: Side effect of "Ethambutol"?

Back: Optic neuritis (red-green color blindness).

Front: Mechanism of "Amphotericin B"?

Back: Binds ergosterol and forms pores in the fungal cell membrane.

Front: Mechanism of "Caspo-fungin"?

Back: Inhibits synthesis of $\beta$-glucan in the fungal cell wall.

Front: Definition of Bacterial "Transformation"?

Back: Uptake of naked DNA from the environment (notably S. pneumoniae, H. influenzae, Neisseria).

Front: Mechanism of Specialized Transduction?

Back: Lysogenic phage packages specific bacterial genes (e.g., Shiga toxin, Diphtheria toxin).

Front: Function of the bacterial "Sex Pilus"?

Back: Mediates conjugation (transfer of plasmids from $F^+$ to $F^-$ cells).

Front: Difference between "Exotoxin" and "Endotoxin"?

Back: Exotoxins are secreted proteins (high toxicity); Endotoxin is Lipid A of the cell wall (triggers cytokines).

Section 31: Specific Gram-Positive Distinctions

Front: Most common cause of "Prosthetic Valve" Endocarditis?

Back: Staphylococcus epidermidis.

Front: Identification of Streptococcus agalactiae (GBS)?

Back: CAMP test positive (enlarges area of hemolysis formed by S. aureus).

Front: Complication of S. pyogenes that follows only pharyngitis?

Back: Rheumatic Fever (PSGN can follow both pharyngitis and impetigo).

Front: Bug associated with "Gas Gangrene" and "Myonecrosis"?

Back: Clostridium perfringens (Alpha toxin/Lecithinase).

Section 32: Enteric & Gram-Negative Nuances

Front: Distinction between Salmonella and Shigella?

Back: Salmonella is motile and produces $H_2S$ (black on Hektoen); Shigella is non-motile and no $H_2S$.

Front: Main virulence factor of E. coli causing neonatal meningitis?

Back: K1 capsular antigen.

Front: "Comma-shaped" rod that grows at $42^\circ\text{C}$?

Back: Campylobacter jejuni.

Front: Mechanism of Pseudomonas Exotoxin A?

Back: Inactivates Elongation Factor-2 (EF-2) (same mechanism as Diphtheria toxin).

Front: Bug causing "Cat Scratch Disease" vs. "Bacillary Angiomatosis"?

Back: Both caused by Bartonella henselae (Angiomatosis occurs in immunocompromised).

Section 33: Specialized Virology (Hepatitis & HIV)

Front: Which Hepatitis virus requires HBV for coinfection?

Back: Hepatitis D (Deltavirus).

Front: Mode of transmission for Hepatitis E?

Back: Fecal-oral (Enteric); high mortality in pregnant women.

Front: Key structural protein used to diagnose early HIV infection?

Back: p24 capsid protein.

Front: Which HIV gene encodes for Reverse Transcriptase, Protease, and Integrase?

Back: pol gene.

Front: Target of the "Maraviroc" drug in HIV?

Back: CCR5 co-receptor on the host cell.

Section 34: Systemic Mycology "Look-Alikes"

Front: Fungus with "Broad-based budding" found in the Great Lakes region?

Back: Blastomyces dermatitidis.

Front: "Cigar-shaped" yeast associated with rose bushes/gardening?

Back: Sporothrix schenckii.

Front: Distinguishing feature of Mucor vs. Aspergillus?

Back: Mucor has non-septate hyphae with wide-angle ($90^\circ$) branching.

Front: Soap bubble lesions in the brain (Virchow-Robin spaces)?

Back: Cryptococcus neoformans.

Section 35: High-Yield Parasitology Vignettes

Front: Parasite associated with "Squamous Cell Carcinoma of the Bladder"?

Back: Schistosoma haematobium.

Front: Treatment for Toxoplasma gondii?

Back: Sulfadiazine + Pyrimethamine.

Front: "Anchovy paste" liver abscess?

Back: Entamoeba histolytica.

Front: Periorbital edema and muscle pain after eating "undercooked wild game"?

Back: Trichinella spiralis.

Section 36: Antimicrobial Side Effects (Final Round)

Front: Antibiotic that causes "Disulfiram-like reaction" with alcohol?

Back: Metronidazole (also some Cephalosporins).

Front: Classic side effect of "Dapsone"?

Back: Hemolysis in G6PD deficiency.

Front: Unique side effect of "Rifampin"?

Back: Orange-colored body fluids (tears, urine, sweat).

Front: Side effect of "Tetracyclines" involving the skin?

Back: Photosensitivity.

Front: Which antibiotic class causes "Fanconi Syndrome" if expired?

Back: Tetracyclines.

Section 37: Laboratory Diagnostics (Rapid Fire)

Front: "Quellung Reaction" positive?

Back: S. pneumoniae, H. influenzae, N. meningitidis, Klebsiella (Encapsulated bugs).

Front: "Nagler Reaction" identifies?

Back: Clostridium perfringens (Alpha toxin).

Front: "Weil-Felix Reaction" used for?

Back: Rickettsial infections (cross-reacts with Proteus antigens).

Front: "Cold Agglutinin" titer is used for?

Back: Mycoplasma pneumoniae and EBV.
