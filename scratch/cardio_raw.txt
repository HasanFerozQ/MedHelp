Front: Embryologic origin of the ascending aorta and pulmonary trunk?

Back: Truncus arteriosus.

Front: Embryologic origin of the smooth parts (outflow tracts) of the left and right ventricles?

Back: Bulbus cordis.

Front: Adult remnant of the ductus arteriosus?

Back: Ligamentum arteriosum.

Front: Structure that divides the atrium into right and left halves?

Back: Septum primum and septum secundum.

Front: Most common site of a Ventricular Septal Defect (VSD)?

Back: Membranous septum.

Front: Vessel that carries oxygenated blood from the placenta to the fetus?

Back: Umbilical vein.

Front: Fetal shunt that allows blood to bypass the liver?

Back: Ductus venosus.

Front: Fetal shunt that allows blood to bypass the lungs by entering the left atrium?

Back: Foramen ovale.

Front: Mechanism of Foramen Ovale closure at birth?

Back: Increased left atrial pressure pushes septum primum against septum secundum.

Front: Artery that usually supplies the SA and AV nodes?

Back: Right Coronary Artery (RCA).

Front: Coronary artery that determines "cardiac dominance"?

Back: Posterior Descending Artery (PDA).

Part 2: Physiology & Hemodynamics

Front: Definition of Cardiac Output (CO)?

Back: Stroke Volume × Heart Rate.

Front: Formula for Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP)?

Back: $2/3 \text{ Diastolic Pressure} + 1/3 \text{ Systolic Pressure}$.

Front: Variable representing the "stretch" on the heart before contraction?

Back: Preload.

Front: Effect of Nitroglycerin on preload and afterload?

Back: Primarily decreases preload via venodilation.

Front: The Frank-Starling Law relationship?

Back: Increased end-diastolic volume leads to increased stroke volume.

Front: Primary factor determining resistance in Poiseuille’s Law?

Back: Vessel radius (to the 4th power).

Front: Effect of Sympathetic stimulation on the Starling curve?

Back: Shifts the curve upward and to the left (increased contractility).

Front: Phase 0 of the ventricular action potential?

Back: Rapid depolarization via voltage-gated $Na^+$ channels.

Front: Phase 2 (plateau) of the ventricular action potential?

Back: Influx of $Ca^{2+}$ balanced by efflux of $K^+$.

Front: Unique ion current responsible for the SA node "pacemaker" potential?

Back: $I_f$ (funny current) sodium channels.

Front: Normal PR interval duration?

Back: 120 to 200 milliseconds.

Front: Effect of carotid sinus massage on the heart?

Back: Increases baroreceptor firing, increasing vagal tone to slow the HR.

Part 3: Pathology - Valvular & Structural

Front: Classic triad of Aortic Stenosis symptoms?

Back: Syncope, Angina, and Dyspnea (SAD).

Front: Murmur description for Aortic Stenosis?

Back: Crescendo-decrescendo systolic ejection murmur.

Front: Murmur associated with Mitral Regurgitation?

Back: Holosystolic, high-pitched "blowing" murmur at the apex.

Front: Most common cause of Mitral Stenosis?

Back: Chronic Rheumatic Heart Disease.

Front: Murmur associated with an Atrial Septal Defect (ASD)?

Back: Mid-systolic murmur with a wide, fixed split S2.

Front: Physical exam finding in Coarctation of the Aorta?

Back: Brachial-femoral pulse delay and rib notching on X-ray.

Front: Four components of Tetralogy of Fallot?

Back: Pulmonary stenosis, RVH, Overriding aorta, VSD.

Front: Pathognomonic histological finding in Rheumatic Heart Disease?

Back: Aschoff bodies (containing Anitschkow cells).

Part 4: Ischemic Heart Disease & Heart Failure

Front: Definition of Unstable Angina?

Back: Angina at rest or increasing in frequency/severity without enzyme elevation.

Front: EKG finding in Prinzmetal (variant) angina?

Back: Transient ST-segment elevation.

Front: Gold standard biomarker for myocardial infarction (MI)?

Back: Cardiac Troponin I or T.

Front: MI biomarker that returns to normal first (useful for re-infarction)?

Back: CK-MB.

Front: Most common cause of death in the first 24 hours post-MI?

Back: Arrhythmia (usually Ventricular Fibrillation).

Front: Timing of free wall rupture or papillary muscle rupture post-MI?

Back: 3 to 14 days.

Front: Name of the friction rub-associated pericarditis occurring weeks after an MI?

Back: Dressler Syndrome (Autoimmune).

Front: Definition of Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction (HFpEF)?

Back: Diastolic dysfunction (impaired filling) with EF $\geq$ 50%.

Front: Key compensatory mechanism in Chronic Heart Failure?

Back: Activation of the Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System (RAAS).

Front: Interpretation of an elevated B-type Natriuretic Peptide (BNP)?

Back: Indicates ventricular wall stretch/volume overload.

Part 5: Cardiomyopathies & Inflammatory Disease

Front: Most common cause of Dilated Cardiomyopathy?

Back: Idiopathic (or Alcohol, Beriberi, Coxsackie B, Chagas).

Front: Inheritance pattern of Hypertrophic Obstructive Cardiomyopathy (HOCM)?

Back: Autosomal Dominant (Sarcomere protein mutations).

Front: Beck's Triad for Cardiac Tamponade?

Back: Hypotension, Distended neck veins, Muffled heart sounds.

Front: Classic EKG finding in Cardiac Tamponade?

Back: Electrical alternans.

Front: Kussmaul sign?

Back: Increase in JVP on inspiration (seen in constrictive pericarditis).

Front: Most common organism in Subacute Bacterial Endocarditis?

Back: Viridans group streptococci.

Front: Most common organism in Acute Endocarditis (IV drug users)?

Back: Staphylococcus aureus.

Front: Physical signs of infective endocarditis on the hands/eyes?

Back: Osler nodes, Janeway lesions, and Roth spots.

Part 6: Vascular & Hypertension

Front: Definition of Hypertensive Emergency?

Back: BP >180/120 mmHg with evidence of end-organ damage.

Front: Characteristic of Monckeberg Sclerosis?

Back: Medial calcific sclerosis that does not obstruct blood flow.

Front: Histological appearance of malignant hypertension in vessels?

Back: Hyperplastic arteriolosclerosis ("onion skinning").

Front: Classic presentation of Aortic Dissection?

Back: Sudden onset "tearing" chest pain radiating to the back.

Front: Most important risk factor for Aortic Dissection?

Back: Hypertension.

Front: Giant Cell Arteritis is associated with which systemic condition?

Back: Polymyalgia Rheumatica.

Front: Classic triad of Wegener’s (Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis)?

Back: Nasopharynx, Lung, and Kidney involvement.

Front: Vascular disease associated with "String of Beads" on angiography?

Back: Fibromuscular Dysplasia.

Part 7: Pharmacology

Front: Mechanism of action of Statins?

Back: HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors.

Front: Side effects of ACE Inhibitors?

Back: Cough, Angioedema, Hyperkalemia (CATCH).

Front: Preferred drug for hypertensive emergency in pregnancy?

Back: Hydralazine or Labetalol.

Front: Mechanism of Class I Antiarrhythmics?

Back: Sodium ($Na^+$) channel blockers.

Front: Mechanism of Class III Antiarrhythmics (e.g., Amiodarone)?

Back: Potassium ($K^+$) channel blockers (prolong repolarization).

Front: Common side effect of Dihydropyridine Calcium Channel Blockers (e.g., Amlodipine)?

Back: Peripheral edema.

Front: Reversal agent for Heparin?

Back: Protamine sulfate.

Front: Reversal agent for Warfarin?

Back: Vitamin K (delayed) or Fresh Frozen Plasma (rapid).

Front: Clinical use of Digoxin?

Back: Heart failure and Atrial Fibrillation (slows AV conduction).

Front: Characteristic EKG change of Digoxin toxicity?

Back: "Scooped" ST segments.

Front: Drug of choice for paroxysmal supraventricular tachycardia (PSVT)?

Back: Adenosine.

Part 8: Clinical Skills & Diagnostics

Front: Significance of a Fourth Heart Sound (S4)?

Back: Atrial kick into a stiff ventricle (e.g., hypertrophy).

Front: Significance of a Third Heart Sound (S3)?

Back: Rapid ventricular filling in a dilated ventricle (e.g., HF).

Front: EKG criteria for Left Ventricular Hypertrophy?

Back: Deep S in V1 + Tall R in V5 > 35mm.

Front: Appearance of "Water-bottle" heart on Chest X-ray?

Back: Large pericardial effusion.

Front: Diagnosis for "Boot-shaped heart" on X-ray?

Back: Tetralogy of Fallot.

Front: Meaning of "Pulsus Paradoxus"?

Back: Drop in systolic BP > 10 mmHg during inspiration.

Part 9: Advanced Topics (Braunwald/Lilly Focus)

Front: Primary mechanism of Prinzmetal Angina?

Back: Coronary artery vasospasm.

Front: Pathophysiology of "Stunned Myocardium"?

Back: Reversible contractile dysfunction after temporary ischemia.

Front: Characteristic of "Hibernating Myocardium"?

Back: Chronic ischemia leading to downregulated function, reversible with revascularization.

Front: Key hemodynamic difference between restrictive cardiomyopathy and constrictive pericarditis?

Back: Restrictive CM usually has higher BNP and pulmonary pressures.

Front: EKG finding in Wolff-Parkinson-White (WPW) syndrome?

Back: Short PR interval and Delta wave (slurred upstroke of QRS).

Front: Major criteria for Jones criteria (Rheumatic Fever)?

Back: Joints (polyarthritis), Carditis, Nodules, Erythema marginatum, Sydenham chorea.

Front: Most common primary cardiac tumor in adults?

Back: Atrial Myxoma (usually left atrium).

Front: Most common primary cardiac tumor in children?

Back: Rhabdomyoma (associated with Tuberous Sclerosis).

Front: Mechanism of Nitrate tolerance?

Back: Depletion of sulfhydryl groups required for NO release.

Front: Most common cause of sudden cardiac death in young athletes?

Back: Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy.

Part 10: Summary & Integration

Front: Effect of inspiration on Right-sided murmurs?

Back: Increases intensity (due to increased venous return).

Front: Effect of Valsalva maneuver on HOCM murmur?

Back: Increases intensity (decreased preload).

Front: Effect of Squatting on HOCM murmur?

Back: Decreases intensity (increased preload and afterload).

Front: First-line treatment for stable chronic angina?

Back: Beta-blockers.

Front: Drug used to close a Patent Ductus Arteriosus (PDA)?

Back: Indomethacin (NSAID).

Front: Drug used to keep a PDA open?

Back: Prostaglandin E1.

Front: Definitive treatment for Third-degree (Complete) Heart Block?

Back: Permanent pacemaker.

Front: "Thumbprint" sign on abdominal X-ray suggests?

Back: Ischemic colitis (often secondary to low CO or vascular disease).

Front: Most common site of Myocardial Infarction?

Back: Left Anterior Descending (LAD) artery ("Widowmaker").

Front: Complication of high-flow oxygen in a patient with COPD and cor pulmonale?

Back: CO2 retention and respiratory acidosis (loss of hypoxic drive).

Front: Definition of Brugada Syndrome?

Back: Autosomal dominant disorder with pseudo-right bundle branch block and ST elevations in V1-V3; risk of V-fib.

Front: Distinguishing feature of Mobitz Type I (Wenckebach) AV block?

Back: Progressive PR interval lengthening until a QRS is dropped.

Front: Distinguishing feature of Mobitz Type II AV block?

Back: Dropped QRS complexes without PR interval lengthening; usually involves His-Purkinje system.

Front: Treatment of choice for Torsades de Pointes?

Back: Intravenous Magnesium Sulfate.

Front: Most common cause of Long QT Syndrome?

Back: Congenital mutations in $K^+$ or $Na^+$ channels (e.g., Romano-Ward or Jervell and Lange-Nielsen).

Front: EKG finding in Multifocal Atrial Tachycardia (MAT)?

Back: At least 3 different P-wave morphologies; often seen in COPD patients.

Front: Definition of the "Ashman Phenomenon"?

Back: A long R-R interval followed by a short R-R interval, often causing an aberrant QRS (usually RBBB) in A-fib.

Part 12: Congenital Heart Disease (Detailed)

Front: Direction of shunt in Eisenmenger Syndrome?

Back: Right-to-Left (reversal of a chronic Left-to-Right shunt).

Front: Common cardiac defect in Down Syndrome?

Back: Atrioventricular (AV) canal defect.

Front: Cardiac defect associated with Turner Syndrome?

Back: Bicuspid aortic valve or Preductal Coarctation of the aorta.

Front: Neonatal presentation of Transposition of the Great Vessels?

Back: Early cyanosis (blue baby) that is not corrected by supplemental oxygen.

Front: Pathophysiology of Ebstein Anomaly?

Back: Displaced tricuspid valve leaflets into the right ventricle ("atrialization" of the RV).

Front: Maternal drug exposure associated with Ebstein Anomaly?

Back: Lithium.

Front: X-ray finding in Total Anomalous Pulmonary Venous Return (TAPVR)?

Back: "Snowman" sign (dilated vertical vein and SVC).

Part 13: Clinical Management & Guidelines

Front: First-line treatment for acute pericarditis?

Back: NSAIDs and Colchicine.

Front: Recommended door-to-balloon time for STEMI?

Back: Within 90 minutes.

Front: Contraindication for Nitrate use in MI?

Back: Right Ventricular infarction or recent Phosphodiesterase inhibitor (Viagra) use.

Front: Goal LDL for a patient with established ASCVD (Very High Risk)?

Back: Less than 55 mg/dL.

Front: Indication for SGLT2 inhibitors in Cardiology?

Back: Heart failure (both HFrEF and HFpEF), regardless of diabetes status.

Front: Class of drugs that improve mortality in HFrEF?

Back: ACEi/ARNI, Beta-blockers, Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (MRA), and SGLT2 inhibitors.

Front: Major side effect of Hydralazine?

Back: Drug-induced Lupus Erythematosus.

Part 14: Rare & High-Yield Pathologies

Front: Typical patient demographic for Takotsubo Cardiomyopathy?

Back: Post-menopausal women following extreme emotional or physical stress.

Front: Cardiac complication of Kawasaki Disease?

Back: Coronary artery aneurysms.

Front: Diagnostic test for Syphilitic Aneurysm?

Back: Tree-barking of the aorta (endarteritis of vasa vasorum).

Front: Common cause of Myocarditis in South America?

Back: Trypanosoma cruzi (Chagas Disease).

Front: Histological finding in Giant Cell Myocarditis?

Back: Extensive necrosis with multinucleated giant cells and lymphocytic infiltrate.

Front: Classic EKG finding in Pulmonary Embolism?

Back: S1Q3T3 (Deep S in I, Q wave in III, inverted T in III).

Part 15: Pressure-Volume Loops & Curves

Front: Effect of increased Afterload on the Pressure-Volume loop?

Back: Increased peak systolic pressure and decreased Stroke Volume (narrower loop).

Front: Effect of increased Inotropy on the Pressure-Volume loop?

Back: Increased Stroke Volume and decreased End-Systolic Volume (loop shifts left).

Front: Point on the PV loop where the Mitral Valve opens?

Back: Bottom left corner (following isovolumetric relaxation).

Front: Point on the PV loop where the Aortic Valve closes?

Back: Top left corner.

Part 16: Physical Exam Nuances (Chizner/Lilly)

Front: Heart sound heard in Mitral Stenosis?

Back: Opening Snap (OS) followed by a mid-diastolic rumble.

Front: Significance of a shortened A2-OS interval?

Back: Indicates more severe Mitral Stenosis.

Front: Murmur of Pulmonic Regurgitation?

Back: Graham Steell murmur (high-pitched, decrescendo diastolic murmur).

Front: Character of the pulse in Aortic Regurgitation?

Back: Water-hammer pulse (Corrigan pulse) or bounding pulses.

Front: Pulsus Alternans is a sign of?

Back: Severe Left Ventricular systolic failure.

Front: Pulsus Bisferiens is seen in which conditions?

Back: Aortic Regurgitation or Hypertrophic Obstructive Cardiomyopathy.

Front: Effect of Handgrip on Aortic Stenosis murmur?

Back: Decreases intensity (increased afterload decreases flow across the valve).

Front: Effect of Handgrip on Mitral Regurgitation murmur?

Back: Increases intensity (increased afterload increases backward flow).

Part 17: Summary Statistics & Trivia

Front: Most common cause of heart failure worldwide?

Back: Coronary Artery Disease.

Front: Most common cause of constrictive pericarditis in developing countries?

Back: Tuberculosis.

Front: Percentage of people with a Patent Foramen Ovale (PFO)?

Back: Approximately 25%.

Front: Most common site of an Aortic Aneurysm?

Back: Abdominal Aorta (infrarenal).

Front: Major risk factor for Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm (AAA)?

Back: Tobacco smoking.

Front: Standard screening for AAA?

Back: One-time ultrasound in men aged 65–75 who have ever smoked.

Front: Classic EKG progression in STEMI?

Back: Hyperacute T-waves $\rightarrow$ ST elevation $\rightarrow$ Q waves $\rightarrow$ T-wave inversion.

Front: Most common complication of Mitral Valve Prolapse?

Back: Mitral Regurgitation.

Front: "Early peaking" systolic murmur suggests?

Back: Mild Aortic Stenosis.

Front: "Late peaking" systolic murmur suggests?

Back: Severe Aortic Stenosis.

Front: Apoprotein that mediates extrahepatic uptake of LDL?

Back: Apo B-100.

Front: Primary function of Apo A-I?

Back: Activates LCAT to catalyze cholesterol esterification in HDL (Reverse cholesterol transport).

Front: Deficiency in Familial Hypercholesterolemia (Type IIa)?

Back: Absent or defective LDL receptors.

Front: Physical finding pathognomonic for Hypertriglyceridemia?

Back: Eruptive xanthomas.

Front: Physical finding pathognomonic for Type II Hyperlipidemia?

Back: Tendon xanthomas (especially Achilles).

Front: Key enzyme inhibited by Fibrates?

Back: They activate PPAR-$\alpha$ to increase LPL activity.

Front: Primary side effect of Niacin treatment?

Back: Cutaneous flushing (mediated by prostaglandins).

Part 19: Metabolic & Endocrine Effects on the Heart

Front: Cardiac effect of Hyperthyroidism?

Back: Increased B1-receptor expression leading to tachycardia and high-output heart failure.

Front: Most common cardiac manifestation of Sarcoidosis?

Back: Restrictive cardiomyopathy or sudden cardiac death due to AV block.

Front: "Orphan Annie eye" nuclei in a thyroid met are unrelated, but what is the cardiac "Orphan" sign?

Back: There is none; however, "Soldier’s Heart" refers to Autonomic Dystonia (Da Costa's Syndrome).

Front: Effect of Hypocalcemia on the EKG?

Back: QT interval prolongation.

Front: Effect of Hyperkalemia on the EKG?

Back: Peaked T-waves, dropped P-waves, and widened QRS (sine wave).

Front: Cardiovascular complication of Acromegaly?

Back: Biventricular hypertrophy and cardiomyopathy.

Part 20: Advanced Pathology & Morphology (Robbins Focus)

Front: Definition of a "Stary" lesion in atherosclerosis?

Back: A fatty streak (the earliest visible lesion).

Front: Most common site of a Syphilitic (Luetic) Aneurysm?

Back: Ascending Aorta and Aortic Arch.

Front: Histological term for debris-filled plaque in a coronary artery?

Back: Atheroma with a necrotic core (cholesterol clefts).

Front: Difference between a "True" and "False" (Pseudo) aneurysm?

Back: A true aneurysm involves all three layers of the arterial wall.

Front: Primary location of a "Nutmeg Liver"?

Back: Central veins (due to Right Heart Failure/Passive Congestion).

Front: Pathophysiology of Cardiac Amyloidosis?

Back: Deposition of misfolded proteins (AL or TTR) in the myocardium.

Front: Cardiac finding in Carcinoid Syndrome?

Back: Endocardial fibrosis of the Right Heart (Tricuspid/Pulmonic) valves.

Part 21: High-Yield Physics & Formulas

Front: Formula for Stroke Volume?

Back: $EDV - ESV$.

Front: Formula for Ejection Fraction?

Back: $(SV / EDV) \times 100$.

Front: Relationship between Wall Stress and Pressure (Law of Laplace)?

Back: $\text{Wall Stress} = (\text{Pressure} \times \text{Radius}) / (2 \times \text{Wall Thickness})$.

Front: Effect of increased radius on wall stress?

Back: Increases wall stress (why dilated hearts fail).

Front: Factor that determines the "Velocity of Shortening" in muscle?

Back: Afterload (inversely proportional).

Front: "Double apical impulse" is a hallmark of?

Front: "Machine-like" murmur heard throughout the cardiac cycle?

Back: Patent Ductus Arteriosus (PDA).

Back: Aortic Regurgitation.

Front: "Delta wave" on EKG?

Back: Wolff-Parkinson-White (WPW) Syndrome.

Front: "Osborn Wave" (J-wave) on EKG?

Back: Hypothermia.

Front: Class of antiarrhythmics that exhibit "Use-Dependence"?

Back: Class IC (Flecainide, Propafenone).

Front: Antiarrhythmic drug known for causing pulmonary fibrosis?

Back: Deeply inverted or biphasic T-waves in V2-V3 (signifies critical proximal LAD stenosis).

Front: Definition of Cardiac Index?

Back: Cardiac Output divided by Body Surface Area (BSA).

Front: Most common site of origin for Atrial Flutter?

Back: Cavotricuspid isthmus in the Right Atrium.

Front: Primary treatment for Symptomatic Bradycardia?

Back: Atropine.

Part 27: Pathology Tidbits (Robbins)

Front: Definition of "Cor Pulmonale"?

Back: Right ventricular hypertrophy and failure resulting from primary pulmonary hypertension or lung disease.

Front: Most common cause of a Paradoxical Embolus?

Back: Patent Foramen Ovale (PFO) or ASD.

Front: Microscopic finding in Viral Myocarditis?

Back: Lymphocytic infiltrate with focal myocyte necrosis.

Front: Histological hallmark of Monckeberg Medial Calcific Sclerosis?

Back: Ring-like calcifications in the media of medium-sized arteries; no lumen narrowing.

Part 28: Critical Pharmacology Nuances

Front: Drug of choice for Beta-blocker overdose?

Back: Glucagon (increases cAMP in myocytes via non-beta receptors).

Front: Mechanism of Ranolazine?

Back: Inhibits the late inward sodium current, reducing diastolic wall tension and O2 consumption.

Front: Why are ACE inhibitors contraindicated in Bilateral Renal Artery Stenosis?

Back: They inhibit efferent arteriole constriction, causing a sharp drop in GFR and acute renal failure.

Front: Main toxicity of Nitroprusside in patients with renal failure?

Back: Thiocyanate toxicity (presents as psychosis, seizures, or delirium).
