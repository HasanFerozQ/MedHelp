Front: Definition of Nephrotic Syndrome?

Back: Massive proteinuria (> 3.5 g/day), hypoalbuminemia, edema, and hyperlipidemia.

Front: Most common cause of Nephrotic Syndrome in children?

Back: Minimal Change Disease (MCD).

Front: Light microscopy finding in Minimal Change Disease?

Back: Normal-appearing glomeruli.

Front: Electron microscopy finding diagnostic for Minimal Change Disease?

Back: Effacement (flattening) of podocyte foot processes.

Front: First-line treatment for Minimal Change Disease?

Back: Corticosteroids (Prednisone).

Front: Most common cause of Nephrotic Syndrome in African Americans and Hispanics?

Back: Focal Segmental Glomerulosclerosis (FSGS).

Front: Secondary causes associated with FSGS?

Back: HIV infection, sickle cell disease, and heroin abuse.

Front: Key light microscopy finding in FSGS?

Back: Segmental sclerosis and hyalinosis in some, but not all, glomeruli.

Front: Characteristic EM finding in FSGS?

Back: Diffuse effacement of foot processes (similar to MCD but with structural damage).

Front: Nephrotic syndrome associated with HBV, HCV, and NSAIDs?

Back: Membranous Nephropathy.

Front: Pathognomonic "Spike and Dome" appearance on silver stain?

Back: Membranous Nephropathy.

Front: Immunofluorescence (IF) pattern for Membranous Nephropathy?

Back: Granular IgG and C3 along the basement membrane.

Front: Major autoantibody associated with primary Membranous Nephropathy?

Back: Anti-phospholipase A2 receptor (PLA2R) antibody.

Front: Defining clinical triad of Nephritic Syndrome?

Back: Hypertension, hematuria (RBC casts), and mild-to-moderate proteinuria.

Front: Classic presentation of Poststreptococcal Glomerulonephritis (PSGN)?

Back: Tea-colored urine 2–3 weeks after a skin or throat infection.

Front: "Lumpy-bumpy" granular IF pattern in PSGN?

Back: Subepithelial deposits of IgG, IgM, and C3.

Front: Electron microscopy hallmark of PSGN?

Back: Large subepithelial "humps."

Front: Most common cause of glomerulonephritis worldwide?

Back: IgA Nephropathy (Berger Disease).

Front: Clinical timing of IgA Nephropathy hematuria?

Back: Occurs concurrently with or days after an upper respiratory infection.

Front: Systemic vasculitis involving IgA deposits, purpura, and abdominal pain?

Back: Henoch-Schönlein Purpura (HSP).

Front: Histological finding in Rapidly Progressive Glomerulonephritis (RPGN)?

Back: Crescents in Bowman’s space consisting of fibrin and macrophages.

Front: Mechanism of Goodpasture Syndrome?

Back: Type II hypersensitivity; antibodies against alpha-3 chain of Type IV collagen.

Front: Linear IF pattern on glomerular basement membrane?

Back: Goodpasture Syndrome.

Front: Nephritic condition presenting with hemoptysis and hematuria?

Back: Goodpasture Syndrome (or Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis).

Front: Differentiation of Wegener’s (GPA) from Goodpasture?

Back: GPA is c-ANCA positive and lacks linear GBM deposits (pauci-immune).

Front: "Tram-track" appearance on Light Microscopy?

Back: Membranoproliferative Glomerulonephritis (MPGN).

Front: Association for MPGN Type I?

Back: Hepatitis B and C infections.

Front: Pathophysiology of MPGN Type II (Dense Deposit Disease)?

Back: C3 nephritic factor stabilizes C3 convertase, leading to persistent complement activation.

Front: Alport Syndrome inheritance and defect?

Back: X-linked dominant; defect in Type IV collagen.

Front: Clinical triad of Alport Syndrome?

Back: Hereditary nephritis (hematuria), sensorineural deafness, and ocular disturbances.

Front: "Basket-weave" appearance on EM?

Back: Alport Syndrome.

II. Tubulointerstitial and Vascular Diseases

Front: Most common cause of Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) in hospitalized patients?

Back: Acute Tubular Necrosis (ATN).

Front: Three phases of Acute Tubular Necrosis?

Back: Prodromal (initiation), Oliguric (maintenance), and Polyuric (recovery).

Front: Pathognomonic urinary cast in ATN?

Back: Granular "Muddy Brown" casts.

Front: Key lab finding in ATN: FENA?

Back: FENA > 2% (inability to reabsorb sodium).

Front: Nephrotoxic agents causing ATN?

Back: Aminoglycosides, radiocontrast dye, lead, and ethylene glycol.

Front: Specific site of tubular injury in ischemic ATN?

Back: Straight segment of proximal tubule and thick ascending limb of Henle.

Front: "Eosinophils in urine" suggests which diagnosis?

Back: Acute Interstitial Nephritis (AIN).

Front: Common drug triggers for Acute Interstitial Nephritis?

Back: Penicillins, NSAIDs, sulfonamides, and diuretics (The "P" drugs).

Front: Classic triad of Acute Interstitial Nephritis?

Back: Fever, rash, and eosinophilia.

Front: Renal Papillary Necrosis is commonly seen in which patients?

Back: Sickle cell disease/trait, Diabetes Mellitus, Chronic Analgesic use, and Pyelonephritis.

Front: Gross appearance of the kidney in Chronic Pyelonephritis?

Back: Coarse, asymmetric corticomedullary scarring and blunted calyces.

Front: "Thyroidization" of the kidney on histology?

Back: Chronic Pyelonephritis (tubules filled with eosinophilic casts resembling thyroid follicles).

Front: Difference between Hyaline and Hyperplastic Arteriolosclerosis?

Back: Hyaline is from chronic HTN/Diabetes; Hyperplastic ("onion skin") is from Malignant HTN.

Front: Renal pathology associated with Diffuse Cortical Necrosis?

Back: Obstetric catastrophes (e.g., abruptio placentae) and septic shock.

III. Cystic and Congenital Diseases

Front: Inheritance and age of onset for ADPKD?

Back: Autosomal Dominant; presents in adulthood (30s–40s).

Front: Gene mutations in ADPKD?

Back: PKD1 (Chromosome 16) or PKD2 (Chromosome 4).

Front: Common extra-renal complication of ADPKD?

Back: Berry aneurysms (Subarachnoid hemorrhage) and hepatic cysts.

Front: ARPKD association in infants?

Back: Congenital hepatic fibrosis and Potter sequence.

Front: Cause of Potter Sequence?

Back: Oligohydramnios (due to renal agenesis or posterior urethral valves) leading to lung hypoplasia.

Front: Most common cause of bladder outlet obstruction in male infants?

Back: Posterior Urethral Valves.

Front: Multicystic Dysplastic Kidney (MCDK) pathogenesis?

Back: Abnormal interaction between ureteric bud and metanephric mesenchyme; non-inherited.

Front: Medullary Sponge Kidney clinical risk?

Back: Increased risk of calcium stones but usually maintains normal renal function.

IV. Metabolic and Systemic Involvement

Front: Earliest clinical sign of Diabetic Nephropathy?

Back: Microalbuminuria.

Front: Pathognomonic histological lesion in Diabetic Nephropathy?

Back: Kimmelstiel-Wilson nodules (nodular glomerulosclerosis).

Front: Effect of ACE inhibitors in Diabetic Nephropathy?

Back: Dilates efferent arteriole to reduce intraglomerular pressure and slow progression.

Front: Renal Amyloidosis stain and microscopy?

Back: Congo Red stain; Apple-green birefringence under polarized light.

Front: Most common kidney stone composition?

Back: Calcium oxalate.

Front: Appearance of Calcium Oxalate stones?

Back: Envelope or dumbbell-shaped crystals.

Front: Stone type associated with Proteus/Urease-positive infections?

Back: Ammonium magnesium phosphate (Struvite) stones.

Front: Classic shape of Struvite stones?

Back: Coffin lid crystals; can form Staghorn calculi.

Front: Hexagonal crystals in urine indicate?

Back: Cystinuria (defect in COLA transporter).

Front: Treatment for Uric Acid stones?

Back: Alkalinization of urine and hydration.

V. Neoplasia of the Urinary System

Front: Most common primary renal malignancy in adults?

Back: Renal Cell Carcinoma (RCC).

Front: Origin of Renal Cell Carcinoma cells?

Back: Proximal convoluted tubule cells.

Front: Paraneoplastic syndromes associated with RCC?

Back: EPO (polycythemia), Renin (HTN), PTHrP (hypercalcemia), and ACTH (Cushing).

Front: Genetic association with Clear Cell RCC?

Back: Loss of VHL gene on Chromosome 3.

Front: Classic triad of RCC (only in 10% of cases)?

Back: Hematuria, palpable mass, and flank pain.

Front: Most common site of metastasis for RCC?

Back: Lung (via hematogenous spread to the renal vein).

Front: Wilms Tumor (Nephroblastoma) typical patient?

Back: Child aged 2–4 years.

Front: WAGR Syndrome components?

Back: Wilms tumor, Aniridia, Genitourinary malformations, and Retardation.

Front: Histology of Wilms Tumor?

Back: Blastema, primitive tubules/glomeruli, and stromal cells (triphasic).

Front: Most common tumor of the urinary collecting system?

Back: Transitional Cell (Urothelial) Carcinoma.

Front: Major risk factors for Urothelial Carcinoma?

Back: Smoking, Aniline dyes, Phenacetin, and Cyclophosphamide (Pee SAC).

Front: Classic presentation of Bladder Cancer?

Back: Painless gross hematuria in an older patient.

Front: Risk factor for Squamous Cell Carcinoma of the bladder?

Back: Chronic irritation (Schistosoma haematobium, chronic stones, long-term catheters).

VI. Clinical Pathology & Laboratory Correlation

Front: Formula for Fractional Excretion of Sodium (FENA)?

Back: $(U_{Na} \times P_{Cr}) / (P_{Na} \times U_{Cr}) \times 100$.

Front: Meaning of FENA < 1%?

Back: Prerenal azotemia (kidneys are conserving sodium).

Front: Urine Osmolality in Prerenal Azotemia?

Back: High (> 500 mOsm/kg) as the kidney concentrates urine to save volume.

Front: BUN/Creatinine ratio in Prerenal Azotemia?

Back: > 20:1 (Urea is reabsorbed with water; Creatinine is not).

Front: Hyaline casts clinical significance?

Back: Non-specific; seen in concentrated urine or dehydration.

Front: WBC casts signify?

Back: Pyelonephritis or Acute Interstitial Nephritis (Inflammation).

Front: Fatty casts ("Maltese Cross") signify?

Back: Nephrotic Syndrome.

Front: Broad/Waxy casts signify?

Back: End-stage renal disease/Chronic renal failure.

Front: RBC casts signify?

Back: Glomerulonephritis or Malignant HTN.

VII. Electrolytes and Acid-Base (Renal Focus)

Front: Type 1 Renal Tubular Acidosis (RTA) defect?

Back: Inability of distal tubule to secrete $H^+$.

Front: Urinary pH in Type 1 RTA?

Back: High (> 5.5).

Front: Type 2 RTA defect?

Back: Inability of proximal tubule to reabsorb $HCO_3^-$.

Front: Association for Type 2 RTA?

Back: Fanconi Syndrome.

Front: Type 4 RTA cause?

Back: Hypoaldosteronism or aldosterone resistance; leads to hyperkalemia.

Front: Renal response to metabolic acidosis?

Back: Increased ammonia ($NH_3$) production to buffer $H^+$ as $NH_4^+$.

VIII. Pharmacology of the Renal System

Front: Mechanism of Acetazolamide?

Back: Carbonic anhydrase inhibitor in the proximal convoluted tubule.

Front: Main clinical use for Acetazolamide (non-renal)?

Back: Glaucoma and altitude sickness.

Front: Mechanism of Loop Diuretics (e.g., Furosemide)?

Back: Inhibits $Na^+/K^+/2Cl^-$ cotransporter in the thick ascending limb.

Front: Major side effect of Loop Diuretics on calcium?

Back: Hypocalcemia ("Loops Lose calcium").

Front: Mechanism of Thiazide Diuretics?

Back: Inhibits $Na^+/Cl^-$ cotransporter in the distal convoluted tubule.

Front: Effect of Thiazides on calcium?

Back: Hypercalcemia (increases reabsorption).

Front: Mechanism of Spironolactone?

Back: Aldosterone receptor antagonist in the cortical collecting duct.

Front: Life-threatening side effect of Potassium-sparing diuretics?

Back: Hyperkalemia.

IX. Pathology of the Lower Urinary Tract

Front: Most common cause of UTI (Cystitis)?

Back: E. coli.

Front: Sterile pyuria (WBCs in urine with negative culture) suggests?

Back: Chlamydia trachomatis or Neisseria gonorrhoeae.

Front: Morphological hallmark of Malakoplakia?

Back: Michaelis-Gutmann bodies (calcified siderotic bodies).

Front: Urachal cyst/adenocarcinoma remnant?

Back: Allantois.

Front: Location of Urachal cancer?

Back: Dome of the bladder.

Front: Definition of Exstrophy of the bladder?

Back: Failure of the abdominal wall to close, exposing the bladder.

Front: Association of Bladder Exstrophy?

Back: Increased risk of Adenocarcinoma.

Front: Hallmark of Chronic Cystitis?

Back: Brunn nests (inward growth of urothelium).

Front: Cystitis Glandularis?

Back: Transformation of Brunn nests into cuboidal or columnar epithelium.

Front: Common cause of obstruction in elderly men?

Back: Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH).

Front: BPH vs Prostate Cancer location?

Back: BPH occurs in the periurethral/transition zone; Cancer in the peripheral zone.

Front: Histological finding in Interstitial Cystitis (Hunner Ulcer)?

Back: Chronic inflammation and mast cells; negative cultures.

Front: Emphysematous Cystitis is most common in?

Back: Diabetic patients (due to gas-forming organisms like E. coli).

Front: Xanthogranulomatous Pyelonephritis appearance?

Back: Yellow-orange nodules; associated with Proteus and "foamy" macrophages.

Front: Renal Infarction gross appearance?

Back: Pale, wedge-shaped cortical infarct.

Front: Fanconi Syndrome electrolyte losses?

Back: Loss of glucose, amino acids, $HCO_3^-$, and $PO_4^{3-}$ in urine.

Front: Bartter Syndrome mimicry?

Back: Mimics chronic Loop Diuretic use (defect in thick ascending limb).

Front: Gitelman Syndrome mimicry?

Back: Mimics chronic Thiazide Diuretic use (defect in distal tubule).

Front: Liddle Syndrome pathophysiology?

Back: Gain-of-function mutation increasing ENaC activity (presents like hyperaldosteronism but low aldosterone).

Front: Most common cause of "Wire Loop" glomerular lesions?

Back: Lupus Nephritis (specifically Class IV: Diffuse Proliferative GN).

Front: Immunofluorescence pattern in Lupus Nephritis?

Back: "Full House" pattern (granular staining for IgG, IgA, IgM, C3, and C1q).

Front: Components of the Glomerular Filtration Barrier?

Back: Fenestrated endothelium, basement membrane (Type IV collagen/heparan sulfate), and podocyte foot processes.

Front: Function of Heparan Sulfate in the GBM?

Back: Provides a negative charge barrier to repel proteins like albumin.

Front: Genetic defect in Finnish-type Congenital Nephrotic Syndrome?

Back: Mutation in the NPHS1 gene encoding Nephrin.

Front: Pathogenesis of Dense Deposit Disease (MPGN Type II)?

Back: Autoantibody (C3 Nephritic Factor) stabilizes C3 convertase, leading to hypocomplementemia.

Front: Primary location of IgA deposits in Berger Disease?

Back: Mesangium.

Front: What defines "Rapidly Progressive" Glomerulonephritis?

Back: Loss of renal function >50% within weeks to months, histologically characterized by crescents.

Front: Major cytokine responsible for mesangial cell proliferation?

Back: PDGF (Platelet-Derived Growth Factor).

Front: Primary composition of the "crescents" in RPGN?

Back: Fibrin, plasma proteins, and parietal epithelial cells (macrophages).

XI. Renal Vascular & Ischemic Pathology

Front: Pathogenesis of Hemolytic Uremic Syndrome (HUS)?

Back: Shiga-like toxin (E. coli O157:H7) damages endothelium, causing microthrombi and mechanical hemolysis.

Front: Clinical pentad of Thrombotic Thrombocytopenic Purpura (TTP)?

Back: Fever, Microangiopathic Hemolytic Anemia, Thrombocytopenia, Neurological symptoms, and Renal failure.

Front: Enzyme deficiency in TTP?

Back: ADAMTS13 (vWF-cleaving protease).

Front: "Flea-bitten" kidney appearance (gross) indicates?

Back: Malignant Hypertension or Hyperplastic Arteriolosclerosis.

Front: Typical cause of Renal Artery Stenosis in elderly men?

Back: Atherosclerosis.

Front: Typical cause of Renal Artery Stenosis in young women?

Back: Fibromuscular Dysplasia ("string of beads" appearance on imaging).

Front: Effect of Renal Artery Stenosis on the affected kidney size?

Back: Atrophy due to chronic ischemia.

Front: Effect of Renal Artery Stenosis on the unaffected kidney?

Back: Hyaline arteriolosclerosis due to exposure to high systemic blood pressure.

Front: Most common cause of Renal Vein Thrombosis?

Back: Nephrotic Syndrome (loss of Antithrombin III in urine).

Front: Classic presentation of Renal Vein Thrombosis in males?

Back: Left-sided varicocele (left gonadal vein drains into left renal vein).

XII. Tubular, Interstitial & Metabolic Fine Details

Front: Fanconi Syndrome occurs in which part of the nephron?

Back: Proximal Convoluted Tubule.

Front: Hartnup Disease clinical presentation?

Back: Pellagra-like symptoms (dermatitis, diarrhea, dementia) due to urinary loss of Tryptophan.

Front: Major cause of Secondary Hyperparathyroidism in Chronic Kidney Disease?

Back: Phosphate retention and decreased 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D (Calcitriol).

Front: Mechanism of "Renal Osteodystrophy"?

Back: Hypocalcemia and hyperphosphatemia lead to increased PTH, causing bone resorption.

Front: Most common type of metabolic alkalosis seen with diuretics?

Back: Contraction alkalosis (volume depletion leading to RAAS activation and $H^+$ secretion).

Front: Mechanism of Ethylene Glycol renal toxicity?

Back: Metabolized to Oxalic Acid, causing ATN and Calcium Oxalate crystals.

Front: Histological finding in Acute Biliary Nephrosis (Cholemic Nephrosis)?

Back: Greenish-yellow bile pigment casts in tubules.

Front: What are "Bence-Jones" proteins?

Back: Immunoglobulin light chains found in the urine of Multiple Myeloma patients.

Front: Appearance of Myeloma Kidney on histology?

Back: Hard, eosinophilic, "fractured" intratubular casts with giant cell reaction.

Front: Common cause of renal stones in patients with Crohn’s disease?

Back: Increased oxalate absorption in the gut (hyperoxaluria).

XIII. Congenital, Cystic & Structural Anomalies

Front: Horseshoe Kidney is trapped under which artery during ascent?

Back: Inferior Mesenteric Artery (IMA).

Front: Horseshoe Kidney increases the risk of which cancer?

Back: Renal Pelvis Transitional Cell Carcinoma.

Front: Renal Agenesis (Bilateral) leads to what amniotic fluid volume?

Back: Oligohydramnios.

Front: Potter Sequence face appearance?

Back: Flattened nose, recessed chin, and low-set ears.

Front: Difference between Simple Cysts and RCC on CT?

Back: Simple cysts are smooth-walled, fluid-filled, and do not enhance with contrast.

Front: Acquired Cystic Disease of the Kidney is associated with?

Back: Long-term Dialysis (increased risk of RCC).

Front: Medullary Cystic Disease (Nephronophthisis) vs. Medullary Sponge Kidney?

Back: Nephronophthisis leads to renal failure and shrunken kidneys; Sponge Kidney is benign.

Front: Autosomal Dominant Tubulointerstitial Kidney Disease (ADTKD) symptom?

Back: Hyperuricemia and early-onset gout.

XIV. Further Neoplasia & Urologic Pathology

Front: Angiomyolipoma is a classic renal finding in which systemic disease?

Back: Tuberous Sclerosis.

Front: Components of an Angiomyolipoma?

Back: Blood vessels, smooth muscle, and adipose tissue.

Front: Oncocytoma gross appearance?

Back: Tan/Mahogany brown with a well-circumscribed central stellate scar.

Front: Ultrastructural hallmark of Oncocytoma?

Back: Cells packed with numerous mitochondria.

Front: Clear Cell RCC arises from which specific tubule?

Back: Proximal Tubule.

Front: Chromophobe RCC arises from which cells?

Back: Intercalated cells of the Collecting Duct.

Front: Most common benign tumor of the bladder?

Back: Inverted Papilloma.

Front: Typical location of Urothelial Carcinoma "in situ"?

Back: Flat lesion of malignant cells within the basement membrane; high risk of progression.

Front: Primary difference between Stage and Grade in Bladder Cancer?

Back: Stage (depth of invasion into the muscle) is the most important prognostic factor.

Front: Gene mutation associated with Fibroepithelial polyp?

Back: None typically, but it is the most common benign mass of the ureter in children.

XV. Practical Lab & Imaging Correlation

Front: "Muddy brown" casts vs. Hyaline casts?

Back: Muddy brown = ATN; Hyaline = Normal/Dehydration.

Front: Presence of "Glitter Cells" in urine indicates?

Back: Pyelonephritis (neutrophils with Brownian motion).

Front: Urine pH in Uric Acid stones?

Back: Acidic (pH < 5.5).

Front: Best imaging modality to visualize kidney stones?

Back: Non-contrast CT scan.

Front: Significance of "Esmarch’s" sign or high urinary Nitrites?

Back: Presence of Gram-negative bacteria (e.g., E. coli, Proteus, Klebsiella).

Front: Why are Nitrites negative in some UTIs?

Back: Some organisms (Enterococcus, S. saprophyticus) do not reduce nitrate to nitrite.

Front: What does "Telescoped Sediment" mean?

Back: A urine sediment showing features of Nephritic, Nephrotic, and Chronic syndromes simultaneously (seen in SLE).

Front: Formula for Serum Osmolality?

Back: $2[Na^+] + \text{Glucose}/18 + \text{BUN}/2.8$.

Front: Immunofluorescence pattern in Henoch-Schönlein Purpura (HSP)?

Back: Granular IgA deposits in the mesangium (identical to IgA Nephropathy).

Front: The "Three D's" of Dense Deposit Disease?

Back: Dense deposits, Decreased C3, and Drusen (retinal deposits).

Front: Most common cause of "Pauci-immune" Glomerulonephritis?

Back: ANCA-associated vasculitides (GPA, MPA, or EGPA).

Front: Target antigen in Goodpasture Syndrome?

Back: Non-collagenous domain of the alpha-3 chain of Type IV Collagen.

Front: Significance of C1q deposits in a "Full House" IF pattern?

Back: Highly suggestive of Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE) involvement.

Front: Gene mutation in Von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) disease?

Back: VHL gene on Chromosome 3p.

Front: Retinal finding associated with VHL disease?

Back: Hemangioblastoma.

Front: Major renal complication of Tuberous Sclerosis?

Back: Bilateral Renal Angiomyolipomas.

XVII. Advanced Renal Physiology & Lab Interpretation

Front: Primary site of Magnesium reabsorption in the nephron?

Back: Thick Ascending Limb of the Loop of Henle (paracellularly).

Front: Effect of PTH on the Proximal Tubule?

Back: Inhibits $Na^+/PO_4^{3-}$ cotransport, leading to phosphaturia.

Front: Effect of PTH on the Distal Tubule?

Back: Stimulates $Ca^{2+}/Na^+$ exchange, increasing calcium reabsorption.

Front: How does Glucose cause osmotic diuresis in Diabetes?

Back: Filtered load exceeds the $T_m$ (transport maximum) of the PCT, keeping water in the tubule.

Front: Urea recycling occurs in which part of the nephron?

Back: Collecting Ducts (stimulated by ADH).

Front: What is "Winter’s Formula" used for?

Back: To calculate the expected respiratory compensation ($pCO_2$) in metabolic acidosis.

Front: Winter’s Formula?

Back: $pCO_2 = 1.5 \times [HCO_3^-] + 8 \pm 2$.

Front: Definition of "Renal Clearance"?

Back: Volume of plasma from which a substance is completely removed per unit time.

XVIII. Toxicology & Drug-Induced Pathology

Front: Renal side effect of Amphotericin B?

Back: Distal Renal Tubular Acidosis (Type 1) and nephrogenic diabetes insipidus.

Front: Renal side effect of Lithium?

Back: Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus (antagonizes ADH at the collecting duct).

Front: Mechanism of Cisplatin-induced kidney injury?

Back: Direct tubular toxicity (ATN), often prevented with Amifostine and saline diuresis.

Front: How do NSAIDs cause "Analgesic Nephropathy"?

Back: Inhibition of Prostaglandins leads to afferent arteriole vasoconstriction and papillary necrosis.

Front: Renal complication of high-dose Acyclovir?

Back: Obstructive crystalline nephropathy (crystal precipitation in tubules).

Front: Class of diuretics that causes "Ototoxicity"?

Back: Loop Diuretics (e.g., Furosemide, Ethacrynic acid).

Front: Why is Ethacrynic acid used instead of Furosemide?

Back: It is not a sulfonamide; used in patients with sulfa allergies.

XIX. Urological Conditions & Miscellaneous

Front: Most common location for a "Ureteropelvic Junction" (UPJ) obstruction?

Back: The junction between the renal pelvis and the ureter.

Front: Classic presentation of a UPJ obstruction in a neonate?

Back: Palpable flank mass; most common cause of neonatal hydronephrosis.

Front: Pathogenesis of Retroperitoneal Fibrosis (Ormond Disease)?

Back: Proliferation of fibrous tissue (often IgG4-related) compressing the ureters.

Front: Renal finding in "Nutcracker Syndrome"?

Back: Compression of the left renal vein between the SMA and Aorta; causes hematuria and flank pain.

Front: Most common malignant tumor of the ureter?

Back: Urothelial (Transitional Cell) Carcinoma.

Front: Defining feature of Malakoplakia?

Back: Chronic inflammatory reaction with soft, yellow mucosal plaques (Michaelis-Gutmann bodies).

Front: Risk factor for primary Renal Lymphoma?

Back: Usually occurs in the setting of systemic Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma.

Front: Mechanism of "Overflow Incontinence"?

Back: Impaired detrusor contractility or bladder outlet obstruction (bladder never fully empties).

Front: Mechanism of "Stress Incontinence"?

Back: Urethral hypermobility or sphincter weakness (leakage with coughing/sneezing).

XX. Final "Quick Fire" High-Yields

Front: "Phrygian cap" refers to?

Back: A gallbladder anomaly (not renal, but often confused in abdominal imaging cards).

Front: "Miley-tous" appearance of the kidney surface?

Back: Benign Nephrosclerosis (from chronic hypertension).

Front: Gold standard for diagnosing Renal Artery Stenosis?

Back: Renal Arteriography.

Front: Most common cause of death in ADPKD?

Back: Cardiovascular disease or complications of Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD).

Front: Primary renal cell type that senses $Cl^-$ concentration?

Back: Macula Densa cells.

Front: Effect of ANP on the afferent arteriole?

Back: Vasodilation (increases GFR).

Front: Effect of ANP on the efferent arteriole?

Back: Vasoconstriction (increases GFR).

Front: Classic hormone elevated in Choriocarcinoma/Testicular tumors that can cause hyperthyroidism?

Back: hCG (shares an alpha-subunit with TSH).

Front: Most common type of renal stone in children?

Back: Calcium oxalate (just like adults), but always screen for metabolic defects.

Front: Type of amyloidosis seen in long-term hemodialysis?

Back: $\beta_2$-microglobulin amyloidosis.

Front: Most common glomerulopathy associated with Malignancy (Solid Tumors)?

Back: Membranous Nephropathy.

Front: Glomerulopathy associated with Hodgkin Lymphoma?

Back: Minimal Change Disease.

Front: Pathogenesis of "Linear" IF in Goodpasture Syndrome?

Back: Anti-GBM antibodies bind evenly to the Type IV collagen of the basement membrane.

Front: Define "Pauci-immune" in the context of GN?

Back: Glomerulonephritis with minimal to no immunoglobulin or complement deposits on IF.

Front: Microscopic Polyangiitis (MPA) vs. Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis (GPA) renal findings?

Back: Both cause pauci-immune RPGN; GPA involves granulomatous inflammation and c-ANCA, while MPA lacks granulomas and is p-ANCA positive.

Front: Primary location of "Subendothelial" deposits in MPGN Type I?

Back: Between the endothelial cell and the basement membrane.

Front: Morphological feature of "Wire Loop" lesions in SLE?

Back: Massive subendothelial immune complex deposits.

Front: Fibrillary Glomerulonephritis pathology?

Back: Congo Red-negative extracellular fibrils (larger than amyloid fibrils) in the mesangium.

XXII. Extended Tubulointerstitial & Obstructive Pathology

Front: Definition of "Hydronephrosis"?

Back: Dilation of the renal pelvis and calyces due to obstruction of urine outflow.

Front: Common causes of acquired bilateral hydronephrosis?

Back: BPH, bladder tumors, and retroperitoneal fibrosis.

Front: Renal Papillary Necrosis (RPN) mnemonic "SAAD"?

Back: Sickle cell, Acute pyelonephritis, Analgesics (NSAIDs), and Diabetes.

Front: Mechanism of "Urate Nephropathy" in Tumor Lysis Syndrome?

Back: Precipitation of uric acid crystals in the acidic environment of the distal tubules/collecting ducts.

Front: Prevention of Urate Nephropathy during chemotherapy?

Back: Allopurinol or Rasburicase and aggressive hydration.

Front: Major electrolyte abnormality in "Fanconi Syndrome"?

Back: Type 2 (proximal) RTA leading to metabolic acidosis, hypophosphatemia, and hypokalemia.

XXIII. Genetic & Molecular Drivers in Renal Tumors

Front: Gene involved in Papillary Renal Cell Carcinoma?

Back: MET proto-oncogene.

Front: Distinction between Birt-Hogg-Dubé syndrome and RCC?

Back: Genetic syndrome involving skin tumors, lung cysts, and Chromophobe RCC.

Front: Denys-Drash Syndrome triad?

Back: Wilms tumor, male pseudohermaphroditism, and early-onset renal failure (diffuse mesangial sclerosis).

Front: Beckwith-Wiedemann Syndrome clinical features?

Back: Wilms tumor, macroglossia, organomegaly, and hemihyperplasia (WT2 gene).

Front: Distinction between Sporadic and Hereditary RCC?

Back: Sporadic is typically single/unilateral in older adults; Hereditary is often multiple/bilateral in younger patients.

XXIV. Lower Urinary Tract & Bladder Pathology

Front: Pathogenesis of Malakoplakia?

Back: Defective phagocytosis by macrophages leading to the accumulation of partially digested bacteria (Michaelis-Gutmann bodies).

Front: Most common location for "Ectopic Ureter" in females?

Back: Vestibule or vagina (bypasses the sphincter, leading to constant dribbling).

Front: Predominant cell type in a "Urachal Adenocarcinoma"?

Back: Glandular cells (resembling colonic mucosa) at the bladder dome.

Front: Distinction between "Cystitis Cystica" and "Cystitis Glandularis"?

Back: Both involve Brunn nests; Cystica has cystic spaces, Glandularis has intestinal-type goblet cells.

Front: Typical risk factor for Squamous Cell Carcinoma of the Bladder in the Middle East?

Back: Chronic Schistosomiasis (Schistosoma haematobium).

XXV. Renal Pharmacology & Acid-Base Integration

Front: Primary acid-base disturbance in Aspirin (Salicylate) overdose?

Back: Mixed Respiratory Alkalosis and Anion Gap Metabolic Acidosis.

Front: Diuretic used to treat idiopathic hypercalciuria (stone prevention)?

Back: Thiazides (they increase distal $Ca^{2+}$ reabsorption).

Front: Effect of Loop Diuretics on Magnesium?

Back: Hypomagnesemia (disrupts the positive lumen potential needed for paracellular reabsorption).

Front: Mechanism of Mannitol?

Back: Osmotic diuretic that increases tubular fluid osmolarity, primarily in the PCT and descending limb of Henle.

Front: Contraindication for Mannitol use?

Back: Congestive Heart Failure and Pulmonary Edema (causes initial volume expansion).

Front: Primary renal mechanism for compensating for Respiratory Acidosis?

Back: Increased $HCO_3^-$ reabsorption and increased $H^+$ secretion (via $NH_4^+$ excretion).

Front: Lab definition of "Azotemia"?

Back: Elevation of BUN and Creatinine levels without clinical symptoms.

Front: Lab definition of "Uremia"?

Back: Azotemia accompanied by clinical symptoms (e.g., pericarditis, asterixis, encephalopathy).

XXVI. Microscopic "Buzzwords" for Pathology

Front: Buzzword: "Subepithelial Humps"?

Back: Post-Streptococcal Glomerulonephritis (PSGN).

Front: Buzzword: "Subendothelial Deposits"?

Back: MPGN Type I or Lupus Nephritis.

Front: Buzzword: "Intramembranous Deposits"?

Back: MPGN Type II (Dense Deposit Disease).

Front: Buzzword: "Linear IgG"?

Back: Goodpasture Syndrome.

Front: Buzzword: "Granular IgG/C3"?

Back: PSGN or Membranous Nephropathy.

Front: Buzzword: "Mesangial IgA"?

Back: Berger Disease or Henoch-Schönlein Purpura.

Front: Buzzword: "Orphan Annie Eye" nuclei in the kidney?

Back: Not standard—usually Thyroid, but can be seen in some variants of Papillary RCC (rarely tested, stick to Thyroid).

Front: Buzzword: "Eosinophilic granular cytoplasm" in a well-circumscribed renal mass?

Back: Renal Oncocytoma.


Front: Definition of Hyperacute Transplant Rejection?

Back: Occurs within minutes; mediated by pre-formed antibodies (Type II hypersensitivity) causing thrombosis.

Front: Most common cause of Hyperacute Rejection?

Back: ABO blood group incompatibility or anti-HLA antibodies.

Front: Hallmark of Acute Cellular Rejection?

Back: Endothelialitis or interstitial infiltration by T-cells (Type IV hypersensitivity).

Front: Histological finding in Chronic Transplant Rejection?

Back: Intimal thickening (vascular fibrosis) and interstitial fibrosis with tubular atrophy.

Front: Viral infection frequently causing graft failure in renal transplants?

Back: BK Polyomavirus.

Front: Diagnostic histological finding for BK Virus nephropathy?

Back: Intranuclear viral inclusions in tubular epithelial cells.

XXVIII. Pregnancy and the Kidney

Front: Normal physiological change in GFR during pregnancy?

Back: GFR increases by approximately 50% due to increased plasma volume.

Front: Definition of Preeclampsia?

Back: New-onset hypertension (>140/90) and proteinuria (or end-organ dysfunction) after 20 weeks gestation.

Front: Pathognomonic renal lesion in Preeclampsia?

Back: Glomerular Endotheliosis (swelling of endothelial cells obliterating capillary lumens).

Front: Significance of HELLP syndrome in renal context?

Back: Hemolysis, Elevated Liver enzymes, and Low Platelets; high risk for Acute Kidney Injury and DIC.

XXIX. Advanced Acid-Base & Electrolyte Pathophysiology

Front: Formula for the Anion Gap?

Back: $Na^+ - (Cl^- + HCO_3^-)$.

Front: Normal range for the Serum Anion Gap?

Back: 8 to 12 mEq/L.

Front: Mnemonic for High Anion Gap Metabolic Acidosis (HAGMA)?

Back: MUDPILES (Methanol, Uremia, DKA, Propylene glycol, Iron/INH, Lactic acidosis, Ethylene glycol, Salicylates).

Front: Calculation for the Urinary Anion Gap?

Back: $Na^+ + K^+ - Cl^-$.

Front: Significance of a negative Urinary Anion Gap in NAGMA?

Back: Suggests the kidney is appropriately excreting $NH_4Cl$ (e.g., diarrhea).

Front: Significance of a positive Urinary Anion Gap in NAGMA?

Back: Suggests a renal defect in $H^+$ excretion (e.g., Renal Tubular Acidosis).

Front: Primary renal compensation for Chronic Respiratory Alkalosis?

Back: Decreased $HCO_3^-$ reabsorption in the proximal tubule.

XXX. Specialized Morphology & Molecular Markers

Front: Staining method to identify the basement membrane "spikes" in Membranous Nephropathy?

Back: Jones Silver Stain.

Front: Difference between "Primary" and "Secondary" FSGS?

Back: Primary is podocyte injury (effacement); Secondary is due to reduced nephron mass and hyperfiltration.

Front: Major ultrastructural difference between MCD and FSGS?

Back: MCD shows diffuse foot process effacement; FSGS shows effacement plus focal detachment of podocytes from the GBM.

Front: Characterize "Collapsing Glomerulopathy"?

Back: A severe variant of FSGS typically associated with HIV or APOL1 high-risk genotypes.

Front: Function of the "Mesangial Cell"?

Back: Contractile cell that provides structural support and regulates GFR surface area.

XXXI. Clinical Syndromes & Manifestations

Front: Definition of "Fractional Excretion of Urea" (FEUrea)?

Back: Used instead of FENA when the patient is on diuretics; <35% suggests prerenal etiology.

Front: Relationship between GFR and Serum Creatinine?

Back: Non-linear/Inversely proportional; a doubling of Creatinine represents a ~50% loss of GFR.

Front: Most common electrolyte cause of Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus?

Back: Hypercalcemia or Hypokalemia.

Front: Clinical finding of "Asterixis" in a renal patient indicates?

Back: Uremic Encephalopathy (an urgent indication for dialysis).

Front: The "AEIOU" indications for urgent Dialysis?

Back: Acidosis, Electrolytes (Hyperkalemia), Ingestions (Toxins), Overload (Volume), and Uremia.

XXXII. Final "Mastery" Cards

Front: Distinction between "Isolated Hematuria" and "Nephritic Syndrome"?

Back: Isolated hematuria (e.g., IgA, Thin Basement Membrane) lacks HTN, edema, or impaired GFR.

Front: Thin Basement Membrane Disease (Benign Familial Hematuria) cause?

Back: Mutation in Type IV Collagen (alpha-3 or alpha-4 chains), but milder than Alport.

Front: Common renal complication of Sarcoidosis?

Back: Hypercalciuria and Hypercalcemia (due to 1-alpha-hydroxylase activity in granulomas).

Front: Mechanism of "Salt-Wasting" in Medullary Cystic Kidney Disease?

Back: Inability of damaged distal tubules to reabsorb sodium, leading to polyuria and hypotension.

Front: Renal cell type responsible for EPO (Erythropoietin) production?

Back: Interstitial cells in the peritubular capillary bed of the renal cortex.

Front: Most common use for the "PAS (Periodic Acid-Schiff)" stain in renal pathology?

Back: Highlighting the Basement Membrane and mesangial matrix (stains glycogen and mucopolysaccharides).

Front: Histological appearance of "Fibrinoid Necrosis" in the kidney?

Back: Smudgy, bright pink (eosinophilic) areas in the vessel wall, typically seen in Malignant HTN or Vasculitis.

Front: What does "Mesangial Matrix Expansion" look like on Light Microscopy?

Back: Increased pink material between glomerular capillaries, seen in Diabetes and Amyloidosis.

Front: Significance of "Extracapillary Proliferation"?

Back: Synonym for Crescent formation in RPGN.

Front: Appearance of "Subepithelial Deposits" on EM?

Back: Electron-dense deposits located between the visceral epithelial cell (podocyte) and the GBM.

XXXIV. Pediatric & Developmental Renal Pathology

Front: Most common cause of "Multicystic Dysplastic Kidney" (MCDK)?

Back: Atresia of the ureteric bud during development.

Front: Is Multicystic Dysplastic Kidney (MCDK) usually unilateral or bilateral?

Back: Usually unilateral; bilateral is incompatible with life.

Front: Clinical finding in "Ureterocele"?

Back: Cystic dilation of the terminal ureter, often presenting with a "cobra head" sign on imaging.

Front: Define "Prune Belly Syndrome" (Eagle-Barrett)?

Back: Triad of abdominal muscle deficiency, undescended testes, and urinary tract anomalies (e.g., megaureter).

Front: Pathogenesis of "Megaureter"?

Back: Aperistaltic distal ureteral segment causing functional obstruction and dilation.

XXXV. Advanced Renal Vascular Physiology-Pathology

Front: Mechanism of "Pressure Natriuresis"?

Back: Increase in sodium excretion by the kidney in response to an elevated arterial pressure.

Front: Define "Autoregulation" of GFR?

Back: The ability of the kidney to maintain constant GFR between Mean Arterial Pressures of 80–180 mmHg.

Front: Myogenic mechanism of GFR regulation?

Back: Afferent arteriole constricts in response to high stretch (increased pressure) to protect the glomerulus.

Front: Tubuloglomerular Feedback (TGF) mechanism?

Back: Macula densa senses high $NaCl$ → releases adenosine → constricts afferent arteriole to decrease GFR.

Front: Primary renal site of "Dopamine" synthesis?

Back: Proximal tubule cells.

Front: Effect of low-dose Dopamine on renal blood flow?

Back: Dilates interlobular arteries and afferent/efferent arterioles, increasing RBF.

XXXVI. Metabolic and Rare Systemic States

Front: Renal manifestation of "Fabry Disease"?

Back: Accumulation of Gb3 in podocytes (appears as "Zebra bodies" on EM), leading to ESRD.

Front: Typical urinary finding in "Alkaptonuria"?

Back: Urine turns black when left standing (Homogentisic acid oxidation).

Front: Renal pathology in "Cystinosis"?

Back: Accumulation of cystine crystals in lysosomes, leading to PCT damage and Fanconi Syndrome.

Front: Distinction between "Primary Hyperoxaluria" and "Enteric Hyperoxaluria"?

Back: Primary is a liver enzyme defect; Enteric is due to increased fat malabsorption (e.g., Crohn's) binding calcium and leaving oxalate free for absorption.

XXXVII. Integrated Pharmacology Fine Print

Front: Class of drugs that can cause "Retroperitoneal Fibrosis"?

Back: Ergot alkaloids (e.g., Methysergide) and certain Beta-blockers.

Front: Renal effect of "Cyclosporine" toxicity?

Back: Potent vasoconstriction of afferent arterioles leading to chronic interstitial fibrosis.

Front: Difference in mechanism between "Conivaptan" and "Furosemide"?

Back: Conivaptan is a V2-receptor antagonist (aquaretic); Furosemide is a natriuretic.

Front: Effect of "Spironolactone" on the heart in CKD?

Back: Reduces cardiac fibrosis but carries a high risk of life-threatening hyperkalemia.

Front: Mechanism of "Sevelamer"?

Back: Non-absorbable phosphate binder used in CKD to prevent hyperphosphatemia.

XXXVIII. Final "Red Flag" Clinical Findings

Front: Presence of "Pneumaturia" (air in urine) suggests?

Back: Vesicocolic fistula (often from Diverticulitis) or emphysematous cystitis.

Front: "Hard" prostate on DRE with elevated PSA and "Blastic" bone lesions?

Back: Metastatic Prostate Adenocarcinoma.

Front: "Soft" boggy prostate with exquisite tenderness and fever?

Back: Acute Prostatitis (usually E. coli).

Front: Definition of "Microscopic Hematuria"?

Back: >3 RBCs per high-power field in a settled urine sediment.

Front: Key difference between "Primary" and "Secondary" Hyperaldosteronism?

Back: Primary has low Renin (Conn’s); Secondary has high Renin (e.g., Renal Artery Stenosis).

Front: Most common renal manifestation of Systemic Sclerosis (Scleroderma)?

Back: Scleroderma Renal Crisis (abrupt malignant hypertension and oligo-anuric AKI).

Front: Histological hallmark of Scleroderma Renal Crisis?

Back: "Onion-skin" hyperplastic arteriolosclerosis and thrombotic microangiopathy.

Front: Mechanism of "Churg-Strauss" (EGPA) renal involvement?

Back: Necrotizing glomerulonephritis (pauci-immune) associated with asthma and peripheral eosinophilia.

Front: Difference in ANCA specificity for GPA vs. MPA?

Back: GPA is typically PR3-ANCA (c-ANCA); MPA is typically MPO-ANCA (p-ANCA).

Front: Target of the autoantibody in Anti-GBM Disease?

Back: The NC1 domain of the alpha-3 chain of Type IV Collagen.

XL. Rare Syndromes and Hereditary Conditions

Front: Gitelman Syndrome vs. Bartter Syndrome: Primary difference in urinary calcium?

Back: Gitelman has hypocalciuria; Bartter has hypercalciuria (or normal).

Front: Clinical features of "Liddle Syndrome"?

Back: Early-onset hypertension, hypokalemia, and metabolic alkalosis with low renin and low aldosterone.

Front: Treatment for Liddle Syndrome?

Back: Amiloride or Triamterene (to block the overactive ENaC channels).

Front: Genetic defect in "Syndrome of Apparent Mineralocorticoid Excess" (SAME)?

Back: Deficiency of 11$\beta$-HSD2 enzyme, allowing cortisol to activate mineralocorticoid receptors.

Front: Substance in black licorice that mimics "SAME"?

Back: Glycyrrhetinic acid (inhibits 11$\beta$-HSD2).

XLI. Advanced Tubular & Interstitial Fine Print

Front: Renal "Tylosis" (thickening) is associated with which rare condition?

Back: Howel-Evans Syndrome (also associated with esophageal cancer).

Front: Most common cause of "Granulomatous Interstitial Nephritis"?

Back: Sarcoidosis or Tuberculosis.

Front: What are "Balkan Endemic Nephropathy" risks?

Back: Aristolochic acid exposure; leads to interstitial fibrosis and high risk of Urothelial Carcinoma.

Front: Mechanism of "Lead Nephropathy"?

Back: Chronic tubulointerstitial nephritis and "saturnine gout" due to reduced urate excretion.

Front: Diagnostic finding in Lead poisoning histology?

Back: Eosinophilic intranuclear inclusion bodies in proximal tubule cells.

XLII. Complex Electrolyte & Acid-Base Scenarios

Front: Definition of "Type 3 RTA"?

Back: A rare combination of Type 1 and Type 2 RTA (usually due to Carbonic Anhydrase II deficiency).

Front: Renal response to "Hypokalemia"?

Back: $K^+$ is reabsorbed in exchange for $H^+$ in the alpha-intercalated cells, potentially causing metabolic alkalosis.

Front: What is the "Osmolar Gap" in renal toxicology?

Back: Measured Osmolality minus Calculated Osmolality; >10 suggests presence of unmeasured toxins (e.g., Ethanol, Ethylene Glycol).

Front: Effect of Hypermagnesemia on the kidney?

Back: Acts as a calcium-channel blocker; inhibits PTH release and can lead to hypocalcemia.

XLIII. Final Neoplasia and Surgical Pathology

Front: Difference between "Cystic Nephroma" and "Multilocular Cystic RCC"?

Back: Cystic Nephroma is benign; Multilocular Cystic RCC is a low-malignant-potential tumor.

Front: Defining feature of "Medullary Thyroid Carcinoma" in MEN 2A/2B?

Back: Not renal, but often associated with Pheochromocytoma, which causes secondary renal hypertension.

Front: Most common site for a "Ureteral Calculi" to lodge?

Back: The Ureterovesical Junction (UVJ).

Front: Hallmark of "Xanthogranulomatous Pyelonephritis" on CT?

Back: "Bear Paw Sign" (dilated calyces mimicking a paw print).

Front: Characteristic IHC marker for Renal Cell Carcinoma?

Back: PAX8 (positive in most renal and thyroid tumors).

Front: Appearance of "Hobnail Cells" in the kidney?

Back: Suggestive of Collecting Duct Carcinoma or Clear Cell Adenocarcinoma of the urinary tract.

XLIV. Wrap-Up High-Yield Facts

Front: Normal value for the "Albumin-to-Creatinine Ratio" (ACR)?

Back: < 30 mg/g.

Front: Definition of "Macroscopic Proteinuria"?

Back: > 300 mg/day.

Front: Common renal side effect of "VEGF Inhibitors" (e.g., Bevacizumab)?

Back: Hypertension and Thrombotic Microangiopathy (TMA).

Front: Distinction between "Ureteritis Cystica" and "Cystitis Cystica"?

Back: Location; Ureteritis Cystica occurs in the ureter, often due to chronic inflammation/stones.

Front: Primary nerve responsible for bladder contraction?

Back: Pelvic Splanchnic Nerves (Parasympathetic S2-S4).

Front: Definition of "Pseudohypoaldosteronism Type I"?

Back: Genetic resistance to aldosterone; presents with salt-wasting, hyperkalemia, and high renin/aldosterone.

Front: Difference between "Primary" and "Secondary" Pseudohypoaldosteronism?

Back: Primary is a genetic receptor defect; Secondary is often due to urinary tract obstruction or infection in infants.

Front: Genetic defect in "Gitelman Syndrome"?

Back: Mutation in the $SLC12A3$ gene encoding the $Na^+-Cl^-$ cotransporter in the distal tubule.

Front: Typical urinary $Ca^{2+}$ finding in Bartter Syndrome?

Back: Hypercalciuria (leads to nephrocalcinosis).

Front: Major electrolyte risk when rapidly correcting Hyponatremia?

Back: Osmotic Demyelination Syndrome (Central Pontine Myelinolysis).

Front: Major electrolyte risk when rapidly correcting Hypernatremia?

Back: Cerebral Edema.

XLVI. Advanced Vascular & Ischemic Pathology

Front: Pathophysiology of "Atheroembolic Renal Disease"?

Back: Cholesterol crystals break off from the aorta (often after catheterization) and lodge in small renal arteries.

Front: Pathognomonic histological finding in Atheroembolic Renal Disease?

Back: Biconvex, needle-shaped "cholesterol clefts" within the arterial lumen.

Front: Classic "triad" of physical findings in Atheroembolic Disease?

Back: Livedo reticularis, eosinophilia, and acute/subacute renal failure.

Front: Definition of "Abdominal Compartment Syndrome" in renal failure?

Back: Intra-abdominal pressure > 20 mmHg causing extrinsic compression of renal veins and decreased GFR.

XLVII. Pregnancy-Related Renal Physiology

Front: What happens to Serum Creatinine levels in normal pregnancy?

Back: They decrease (typical range 0.4–0.8 mg/dL) due to increased GFR.

Front: Cause of "Acute Fatty Liver of Pregnancy" (AFLP) renal involvement?

Back: Microvesicular steatosis can lead to hepatorenal-like syndrome and AKI.

Front: Primary risk factor for "Renal Cortical Necrosis"?

Back: Massive obstetric hemorrhage or abruptio placentae causing vasospasm and DIC.

XLVIII. Clinical Signs and Physical Exam Correlation

Front: Meaning of a "Positive Lloyd's Sign"?

Back: Costovertebral angle tenderness (CVAT), indicative of pyelonephritis or renal calculi.

Front: Cause of "Bruit" heard over the renal arteries?

Back: Turbulent blood flow due to Renal Artery Stenosis.

Front: Significance of "Palpable Kidneys" in a neonate?

Back: Often represents Multicystic Dysplastic Kidney or ARPKD.

XLIX. Specialized Diagnostic Tests

Front: What is the "Water Deprivation Test" used for?

Back: Distinguishing between Central Diabetes Insipidus, Nephrogenic DI, and Primary Polydipsia.

Front: Result of Water Deprivation Test in Primary Polydipsia?

Back: Increase in urine osmolality (> 800 mOsm/kg) after water restriction alone.

Front: Response to Desmopressin (dDAVP) in Central Diabetes Insipidus?

Back: Significant increase in urine osmolality (> 50% increase).

Front: Response to Desmopressin (dDAVP) in Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus?

Back: No or minimal change in urine osmolality.

L. Final Comprehensive Summary Cards

Front: Mnemonic for the causes of Renal Papillary Necrosis?

Back: POSTCARDS: Pyelonephritis, Obstruction, Sickle cell, Tuberculosis, Cirrhosis, Analgesics, Renal vein thrombosis, Diabetes, Systemic vasculitis.

Front: Difference between "Hypertrophy" and "Hyperplasia" in the remaining kidney after a nephrectomy?

Back: The remaining kidney undergoes compensatory hypertrophy (increase in cell size) to increase GFR.

Front: Most common location of "Vesicoureteral Reflux" (VUR) damage?

Back: The poles of the kidney (superior and inferior), where compound papillae allow retrograde flow.

Front: Key clinical distinction between "Cystitis" and "Pyelonephritis"?

Back: Pyelonephritis presents with systemic symptoms (fever, chills) and CVAT; Cystitis is localized to the bladder.

Front: Effect of "Hypomagnesemia" on Potassium?

Back: Makes hypokalemia refractory to treatment; Mg is required as a cofactor to inhibit ROMK channels (potassium secretion).

Front: Part of the nephron where the most water and sodium are reabsorbed?

Back: Proximal Convoluted Tubule (~65%).

Front: Mechanism of sodium reabsorption in the Thick Ascending Limb?

Back: $Na^+/K^+/2Cl^-$ cotransporter (NKCC2).

Front: Driving force for paracellular Calcium and Magnesium reabsorption in the Loop of Henle?

Back: The lumen-positive potential created by $K^+$ back-leak through ROMK channels.

Front: Primary site of Action for Antidiuretic Hormone (ADH)?

Back: V2 receptors on the Principal cells of the Collecting Duct.

Front: Effect of ADH on Aquaporin-2?

Back: Triggers insertion of Aquaporin-2 channels into the apical membrane to increase water permeability.

Front: Electrolyte hallmark of "Fanconi-Bickel Syndrome"?

Back: Proximal tubular dysfunction combined with glycogen storage issues (GLUT2 mutation).

LII. Rare Deposition & Systemic Renal Pathology

Front: Renal pathology seen in "Light Chain Deposition Disease" (LCDD)?

Back: Nodular glomerulosclerosis (resembling Kimmelstiel-Wilson) but Congo Red negative.

Front: What is "Cryoglobulinemic Glomerulonephritis"?

Back: A Type II or III MPGN-like pattern often associated with Hepatitis C.

Front: Characteristic EM finding in Cryoglobulinemic GN?

Back: Subendothelial deposits with "curvilinear" or "fingerprint" patterns.

Front: Type of amyloidosis associated with chronic inflammatory diseases (e.g., RA, IBD)?

Back: AA Amyloid (Serum Amyloid A).

Front: Location of "Medullary Nephrocalcinosis" on imaging?

Back: Calcium deposits within the renal pyramids.

LIII. Microbiology & Infections of the Urinary Tract

Front: Primary virulence factor for Uropathogenic E. coli (UPEC)?

Back: P-fripriae (allow adherence to urothelium).

Front: Organism associated with "Struvite" stones that produces a "swarming" pattern on agar?

Back: Proteus mirabilis.

Front: Most common cause of fungal UTI in catheterized or diabetic patients?

Back: Candida albicans.

Front: Classic renal complication of "Secondary Syphilis"?

Back: Membranous Nephropathy.

Front: Effect of "Schistosoma haematobium" on bladder cytology?

Back: Causes squamous metaplasia leading to Squamous Cell Carcinoma.

LIV. Advanced Clinical Indices & Formulas

Front: Mnemonic for "High Anion Gap" Metabolic Acidosis?

Back: GOLD MARK (Glycols, Oxoproline, L-lactate, D-lactate, Methanol, Aspirin, Renal failure, Ketoacidosis).

Front: What does "FENA > 2%" indicate?

Back: Intrinsic renal damage (e.g., ATN) where the kidney cannot reabsorb sodium.

Front: Formula for "Creatinine Clearance" ($C_{Cr}$)?

Back: $C = (U \times V) / P$ (Urine concentration $\times$ Urine flow rate / Plasma concentration).

Front: Relationship between Serum Potassium and $pH$?

Back: Acidosis leads to hyperkalemia (intercellular exchange of $H^+$ for $K^+$).

LV. Urothelial & Scrotal-Renal Links

Front: Significance of "Hydrocele" in a patient with a renal mass?

Back: Can indicate lymphatic obstruction or a reactive process to a nearby tumor.

Front: Most common location for "Ectopic Ureteral" insertion in males?

Back: Prostatic urethra or seminal vesicles.

Front: Definition of "Vesicoureteral Reflux" (VUR) Grade V?

Back: Severe dilation and tortuosity of the ureter with significant blunting of calyces.

Front: Primary risk factor for "Transitional Cell Carcinoma" of the renal pelvis?

Back: Smoking and exposure to phenacetin.

LVI. Final High-Yield Integration

Front: Difference between "Primary" and "Secondary" Hyperoxaluria?

Back: Primary is a liver enzyme defect (glyoxylate metabolism); Secondary is usually dietary or malabsorption.

Front: Most common renal side effect of "Gold salts" or "Penicillamine"?

Back: Membranous Nephropathy.

Front: What is the "White Line of Toldt"?

Back: A surgical landmark for reflecting the colon to access the kidney (useful for surgery/anatomy-pathology crossover).

Front: Cause of "Toxemic" (Preeclamptic) renal changes?

Back: Placental ischemia leads to the release of sFlt-1, which neutralizes VEGF, damaging glomerular endothelium.

Front: Effect of "Hypokalemia" on the EKG?

Back: U-waves and flattened T-waves.

Front: Effect of "Hyperkalemia" on the EKG?

Back: Peaked T-waves, widened QRS, and loss of P-waves.
