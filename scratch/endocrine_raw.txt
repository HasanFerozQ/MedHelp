Diabetes & Pancreatic Neuroendocrine Tumors

Back: Insulitis (lymphocytic infiltration).

Front: Definition of Carcinoid Syndrome?
Back: Flushing, diarrhea, and right-sided heart valve lesions.

Front: Why are Carcinoid heart lesions only right-sided?
Back: Lung MAO (Monoamine Oxidase) metabolizes serotonin before it reaches the left heart.

Front: Urinary marker for Carcinoid Syndrome?
Back: 5-HIAA (5-hydroxyindoleacetic acid).

Front: Common trigger for Carcinoid Syndrome symptoms?
Back: Alcohol or emotional stress causing hormone release.

Front: Association of "Brown Tumors"?
Back: Osteitis fibrosa cystica in Hyperparathyroidism.

Front: Liddle Syndrome mimic?
Back: Hyperaldosteronism presentation but with low Aldosterone/Renin (gain of function of ENaC).

Front: Syndrome of Apparent Mineralocorticoid Excess (AME) cause?
Back: Deficiency of 11-beta-HSD2 (often from licorice), allowing Cortisol to activate Mineralocorticoid receptors.

Front: Difference between MEN 2A and 2B?
Back: 2A includes Hyperparathyroidism; 2B includes Mucosal Neuromas and Marfanoid habitus.

Front: Common genetic screening for families of MEN 2 patients?
Back: Testing for RET proto-oncogene mutations.

Front: Prophylactic treatment for RET mutation carriers in MEN 2?
Back: Prophylactic thyroidectomy (due to 100% risk of Medullary Thyroid Carcinoma).

Front: Histological characteristic of an Adrenal Adenoma?
Back: Well-circumscribed yellow mass with cells resembling the normal zona fasciculata.

Front: Most common site of ectopic adrenal tissue?
Back: Along the path of gonadal descent (e.g., retroperitoneum, ovaries, testes).

Front: Histological appearance of Adrenocortical Carcinoma?
Back: Large, invasive mass with areas of necrosis, hemorrhage, and cystic change.

Front: Distinction between Adrenal Adenoma and Carcinoma?
Back: Carcinomas are typically larger (>100g), show vascular invasion, and have higher mitotic rates.

Front: Effect of 11-beta-hydroxylase deficiency on Renin levels?
Back: Low Renin (due to hypertension caused by 11-deoxycorticosterone).

Front: Underlying cause of Adrenal Crisis in Addison patients?
Back: Any form of acute stress (infection, trauma, surgery) that increases cortisol demand.

Front: "Zellballen" pattern on histology is characteristic of?
Back: Pheochromocytoma (nests of cells surrounded by vascular stroma).

Front: Immunohistochemical stain positive in Pheochromocytoma?
Back: Chromogranin and Synaptophysin.

Front: S100 stain utility in Pheochromocytoma?
Back: Highlights the sustenacular cells surrounding the Zellballen nests.

Thyroid & Parathyroid: Nuance & Morphology
Front: Definition of the "Tall Cell Variant" of Papillary Carcinoma?
Back: Aggressive variant with height of cells at least twice their width and abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm.

Front: Significance of the RET/PTC rearrangement?
Back: Genetic driver commonly found in Papillary Thyroid Carcinoma.

Front: Follicular Thyroid Carcinoma spread pattern?
Back: Hematogenous (unlike Papillary, which is primarily lymphatic).

Back: Hyperphosphatemia leads to Hypocalcemia, which chronically stimulates PTH secretion.

Front: Histology of Parathyroid Adenoma?
Back: Predominantly chief cells with a rim of normal parathyroid tissue and absence of stromal fat.

High-Yield Laboratory Interferences
Front: Effect of high Biotin intake on Thyroid Function Tests?
Back: Can falsely elevate T4/T3 and falsely lower TSH.

Front: Why is C-peptide useful in Hypoglycemia workup?
Back: Distinguishes endogenous insulin production (high C-peptide) from exogenous insulin injection (low C-peptide).

Front: Lab profile of Factitious Hyperthyroidism?
Back: Low TSH, High T4, and Low Thyroglobulin (due to exogenous ingestion).

Front: Most common cause of a "Thyroid Storm"?
Back: Acute stress (infection, surgery, or trauma) in a patient with pre-existing Graves disease.

Front: Effect of Pregnancy on Total T4 vs. Free T4?
Back: Total T4 increases (due to high TBG), but Free T4 remains normal (eustates).

Front: Significance of "Cold" vs "Hot" nodules on RAIU scan?
Back: Cold nodules are non-functional and more likely to be malignant; Hot nodules are functional and usually benign.

Front: Histological feature of a Hurthle Cell?
Back: Large follicular cell with abundant, granular, eosinophilic cytoplasm and numerous mitochondria.

Front: Condition where Hurthle Cells are most prominently seen?
Back: Hashimoto Thyroiditis and Hurthle Cell Adenoma/Carcinoma.

Front: Most common location for an ectopic Parathyroid gland?
Back: Within the Thymus (due to shared origin from the 3rd pharyngeal pouch).

Front: Pathogenesis of Calciphylaxis?
Back: Systemic calcification of small vessels leading to skin necrosis, often seen in end-stage renal disease/secondary hyperparathyroidism.

Front: Difference between Osteitis Fibrosa Cystica and Renal Osteodystrophy?
Back: Osteitis Fibrosa Cystica is specific to high PTH; Renal Osteodystrophy is the broader bone disease seen in chronic kidney failure.

Adrenal & Pituitary: Advanced Diagnostics
Front: Mechanism of the Metyrapone stimulation test?
Back: Blocks 11-beta-hydroxylase; a normal response is an increase in ACTH and 11-deoxycortisol.

Front: Utility of the Metyrapone test?
Back: To evaluate the integrity of the entire Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Adrenal (HPA) axis.

Front: Histological finding in the pituitary of a patient with Cushing Syndrome?
Back: Crooke Hyaline Change (accumulation of intermediate filaments in ACTH-producing cells).

Front: Primary difference between Lymphocytic Hypophysitis and Pituitary Adenoma?
Back: Lymphocytic Hypophysitis is an autoimmune inflammation (often postpartum) that can mimic a mass on MRI.

Front: Genetic mutation associated with MEN 1?
Back: Mutation in the MENIN tumor suppressor gene.

Front: Most common pituitary tumor in MEN 1?
Back: Prolactinoma.

Front: Relationship between Pituitary Gigantism and the Cardiovascular system?
Back: Often leads to heart failure due to concentric left ventricular hypertrophy and cardiomyopathy.

Metabolic & Pancreatic: The "Grey Zones"
Front: Mechanism of "Amyloidosis" in Type 2 Diabetes?
Back: Co-secretion of Islet Amyloid Polypeptide (Amylin) with insulin leads to its deposition in islets.

Front: Distinction between Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA) and Hyperosmolar Hyperglycemic State (HHS)?
Back: DKA (Type 1) has ketones and acidosis; HHS (Type 2) has extreme dehydration and higher glucose (>600 mg/dL).

Front: Primary reason for the "Dawn Phenomenon" in Diabetics?
Back: Early morning rise in blood sugar due to the physiological surge of Growth Hormone and Cortisol.

Front: Definition of the "Somogyi Effect"?
Back: Rebound hyperglycemia in the morning in response to an undiagnosed hypoglycemic episode during the night.

Front: Diagnostic use of the Glucagon Stimulation Test?
Back: Used to diagnose Pheochromocytoma (triggers catecholamine release) or growth hormone deficiency.

Front: Hormone responsible for the "Incretin Effect"?
Back: GLP-1 (Glucagon-like peptide-1) and GIP, which increase insulin secretion after oral glucose.

Neuroendocrine & Rare Syndromes
Front: Definition of Paraganglioma?
Back: A tumor of the extra-adrenal sympathetic or parasympathetic ganglia (the "extra-adrenal pheochromocytoma").

Front: Most common location for a Paraganglioma?
Back: The Organ of Zuckerkandl (near the origin of the inferior mesenteric artery).

Front: Components of Carney Complex?
Back: Myxomas (heart/skin), hyperpigmentation (lentigines), and endocrine overactivity (adrenal/pituitary).

Front: Characteristic of Von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) syndrome in Endocrine?
Back: High risk for Pheochromocytoma (often bilateral).

Front: Difference in Pheochromocytoma risk between MEN 2A and 2B?
Back: The risk is roughly equal (~50%), but 2B presents with a more aggressive thyroid component.

Front: Histological "Salt and Pepper" chromatin is a classic description of?
Back: Neuroendocrine tumors (e.g., Carcinoid, Pancreatic NETs, Medullary Thyroid Carcinoma).

Front: Most common hormone secreted by a Pancreatic Neuroendocrine Tumor (PanNET)?
Back: Insulin (Insulinoma).

Front: What is the "Rule of 10s" for Insulinomas?
Back: Unlike Pheochromocytoma, only 10% of Insulinomas are malignant (most are benign).

Front: Most common cause of death in patients with Acromegaly?
Back: Heart failure (due to dilated cardiomyopathy).

Front: Mechanism of hypercalcemia in Sarcoidosis?
Back: 1-alpha-hydroxylase activity in granulomatous macrophages converts Vitamin D to its active form (1,25-OH).

Front: Histological appearance of the thyroid in Riedel Thyroiditis?
Back: Dense hyalinized fibrosis and a chronic inflammatory infiltrate.

Front: Effect of chronic Lithium use on the thyroid?
Back: Inhibition of thyroid hormone release, commonly causing hypothyroidism.

Front: Definition of the "Pendred Syndrome"?
Back: Genetic disorder causing sensorineural hearing loss and goiter due to iodine transport defects.

Front: Primary difference in laboratory findings between 21-hydroxylase and 11-beta-hydroxylase deficiency?
Back: 21-hydroxylase deficiency presents with hypotension; 11-beta-hydroxylase deficiency presents with hypertension.

Front: Why does 17-alpha-hydroxylase deficiency cause hypertension?
Back: Accumulation of 11-deoxycorticosterone, which has mineralocorticoid activity.

Front: Most common cause of primary adrenal insufficiency in children?
Back: Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia (CAH).

Front: Distinguishing feature of an Adrenal Myelolipoma?
Back: A benign, non-functioning tumor composed of mature adipose tissue and hematopoietic elements.

Pediatric & Developmental Endocrine Pathology
Front: Presentation of a Lingual Thyroid?
Back: A mass at the base of the tongue; may be the only functional thyroid tissue present.

Front: Most common cause of congenital hypothyroidism worldwide?
Back: Thyroid dysgenesis (aplasia, hypoplasia, or ectopic gland).

Front: Effect of maternal gestational diabetes on the newborn?
Back: Islet cell hyperplasia in the fetus leading to neonatal hypoglycemia after birth.

Front: Classic cardiac defect associated with infants of diabetic mothers?
Back: Transposition of the Great Arteries or Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (septal hypertrophy).

Front: What is the "Water-Clear Cell" hyperplasia?
Back: A rare form of parathyroid hyperplasia where cells have abundant clear cytoplasm.
