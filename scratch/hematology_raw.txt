Section 9: Advanced RBC & Hemoglobin Pathophysiology

Front: Direct effect of 2,3-BPG on the hemoglobin oxygen affinity?

Back: It stabilizes the T (Taut) state, shifting the curve to the right and promoting $O_2$ unloading.

Front: Why is the ESR (Erythrocyte Sedimentation Rate) increased in inflammation?

Back: Fibrinogen (an acute phase reactant) coats RBCs, neutralizing their negative surface charge (Zeta potential) and allowing them to aggregate (Rouleaux).

Front: Distinction between Hemoglobinuria and Myoglobinuria on dipstick?

Back: Both show "Blood" on dipstick, but only Hemoglobinuria shows pink/red serum (Myoglobin is cleared rapidly, leaving serum clear).

Front: What is the "Rule of Three" in RBC indices?

Back: $RBC \times 3 \approx Hb$ and $Hb \times 3 \approx Hct$.

Front: Most common cause of Death in Sickle Cell patients (Adults)?

Back: Acute Chest Syndrome.

Front: Organism responsible for Osteomyelitis in Sickle Cell patients?

Back: Salmonella (most common/classic) and Staphylococcus aureus.

Front: Effect of Hydroxyurea in Sickle Cell Disease?

Back: Increases production of HbF, which interferes with the polymerization of HbS.

Front: Clinical finding in Lead Poisoning involving the gums?

Back: Burton lines (bluish-gray lead sulfide deposits).

Section 10: Platelet Disorders & Vessel Wall Pathology

Front: Mechanism of Bernard-Soulier Syndrome?

Back: Deficiency of GpIb; impaired platelet-to-vWF adhesion.

Front: Classic finding on peripheral smear for Bernard-Soulier?

Back: Giant platelets (often as large as RBCs).

Front: Mechanism of Glanzmann Thrombasthenia?

Back: Deficiency/defect of GpIIb/IIIa; impaired platelet-to-platelet aggregation.

Front: Ristocetin cofactor assay result in von Willebrand Disease (vWD)?

Back: Abnormal (no agglutination), because vWF is required for Ristocetin-induced platelet clumping.

Front: Why is the PTT sometimes prolonged in vWD?

Back: vWF acts as a stabilizer/carrier for Factor VIII.

Front: What is the "Pseudo-Thrombocytopenia" artifact in lab reports?

Back: Platelet clumping in the test tube caused by EDTA (requires re-testing with a citrate tube).

Front: Mechanism of Immune Thrombocytopenic Purpura (ITP)?

Back: Anti-GpIIb/IIIa antibodies $\rightarrow$ splenic macrophage consumption of platelets.

Front: First-line treatment for ITP in a patient with a dangerously low platelet count?

Back: IVIG (acts as a "decoy" for splenic macrophages) or Corticosteroids.

Section 11: Hypercoagulability & Anticoagulation

Front: Effect of Protein C/S deficiency on the body?

Back: Hypercoagulability; unable to inactivate Factors Va and VIIIa.

Front: Why does Warfarin cause "Skin Necrosis" in Protein C deficient patients?

Back: Protein C has a shorter half-life than Factors II, IX, and X; its rapid depletion creates a transient hypercoagulable state.

Front: Mechanism of Antithrombin III deficiency?

Back: PTT fails to rise significantly after giving Heparin (Heparin resistance).

Front: Difference between UFH (Unfractionated Heparin) and LMWH (Enoxaparin) mechanism?

Back: UFH inhibits both Thrombin and Factor Xa; LMWH predominantly inhibits Factor Xa.

Front: Lab test used to monitor Warfarin therapy?

Back: PT / INR.

Front: Direct Thrombin Inhibitors (e.g., Argatroban, Bivalirudin) are the treatment of choice for?

Back: Heparin-Induced Thrombocytopenia (HIT).

Section 12: Myeloid & Lymphoid Malignancies (Detailed)

Front: Mutation found in almost 100% of Hairy Cell Leukemia cases?

Back: BRAF V600E.

Front: Distinctive "Dry Tap" on bone marrow aspiration is seen in which two conditions?

Back: Primary Myelofibrosis and Hairy Cell Leukemia.

Front: Difference between Leukemoid Reaction and CML regarding Basophils?

Back: Basophilia is characteristic of CML, not a Leukemoid Reaction.

Front: Typical immunophenotype of CLL B-cells?

Back: CD5+ and CD23+ (Note: CD5 is usually a T-cell marker).

Front: What is a "Richter Transformation"?

Back: CLL/SLL transforming into an aggressive Diffuse Large B-Cell Lymphoma (DLBCL).

Front: "Waldenström Macroglobulinemia" involves the overproduction of which Ig?

Back: IgM (leads to hyperviscosity syndrome).

Front: Radiographic "Punch-out" lesions in Multiple Myeloma are caused by?

Back: RANKL activation of Osteoclasts (and inhibition of Osteoblasts).

Front: Key difference between MGUS and Multiple Myeloma?

Back: MGUS has $< 10\%$ plasma cells in marrow and NO "CRAB" symptoms.

Section 13: Transfusion Medicine & Miscellaneous

Front: Most common transfusion reaction?

Back: Febrile Non-hemolytic Transfusion Reaction (caused by cytokines in stored blood).

Front: Primary symptom of TRALI (Transfusion-Related Acute Lung Injury)?

Back: Non-cardiogenic pulmonary edema (sudden onset respiratory distress/hypoxia).

Front: Mechanism of Acute Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction?

Back: Type II Hypersensitivity (preformed antibodies against donor ABO antigens).

Front: Blood product used to rapidly reverse Warfarin?

Back: Prothrombin Complex Concentrate (PCC) or Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP).

Front: Indication for giving "Washed" Red Blood Cells?

Back: IgA deficiency (to prevent anaphylaxis).

Front: Storage lesion of RBCs involves a decrease in which metabolite?

Back: 2,3-BPG (leads to increased $O_2$ affinity and decreased tissue delivery).

Front: Definition of Pancytopenia?

Back: Decrease in all three cell lines (RBCs, WBCs, and Platelets).

Front: Most common cause of Aplastic Anemia?

Back: Idiopathic (immune-mediated T-cell destruction of stem cells).

Front: Morphology of RBCs in Myelophthisic Anemia?

Back: Teardrop cells (Dacrocytes) – caused by the marrow being "squeezed" by fibrosis or tumor.

Section 14: Storage & Metabolic Disorders (Heme-related)

Front: Deficiency in Acute Intermittent Porphyria (AIP)?

Back: Porphobilinogen (PBG) deaminase.

Front: The "5 Ps" of Acute Intermittent Porphyria symptoms?

Back: Painful abdomen, Port wine-colored urine, Polyneuropathy, Psychological disturbances, Precipitated by drugs (CYP450 inducers).

Front: Deficiency in Porphyria Cutanea Tarda (PCT)?

Back: Uroporphyrinogen decarboxylase.

Front: Classic clinical presentation of Porphyria Cutanea Tarda?

Back: Blistering cutaneous photosensitivity and hyperpigmentation (often associated with Hepatitis C).

Front: Iron overload in the heart (Hemochromatosis) typically leads to?

Back: Restrictive or Dilated Cardiomyopathy.

Section 15: Laboratory Medicine & Flow Cytometry

Front: Marker for identifying Hematopoietic Stem Cells (HSC)?

Back: CD34+.

Front: Markers used to identify B-cell lineage via Flow Cytometry?

Back: CD19, CD20, and CD21.

Front: Marker used to identify the monocyte lineage?

Back: CD14 (the receptor for LPS).

Front: What does a "Shift to the Left" signify in a CBC?

Back: Increased presence of immature neutrophils (band cells), usually indicating acute infection or inflammation.

Front: How is the "Mentzer Index" used to differentiate Iron Deficiency from Thalassemia?

Back: $MCV / RBC count$. If $< 13$, it suggests Thalassemia; if $> 13$, it suggests Iron Deficiency.

Front: Meaning of "Anisocytosis" on a lab report?

Back: Variation in RBC size (measured by RDW - Red Cell Distribution Width).

Front: Meaning of "Poikilocytosis"?

Back: Variation in RBC shape.

Section 16: Specialized Hemolytic States & Inclusions

Front: Mechanism of Parvovirus B19 induced anemia?

Back: Directly infects and lyses Erythroid Precursor cells; causes Aplastic Crisis in patients with high RBC turnover (e.g., Sickle Cell).

Front: Hallmark peripheral smear finding in Microangiopathic Hemolytic Anemia (MAHA)?

Back: Schistocytes (Helmet cells).

Front: What causes the "Starry Sky" appearance in Burkitt Lymphoma histology?

Back: Tingible body macrophages (white "stars") consuming apoptotic B-cells (dark "sky").

Front: Which enzyme is deficient in Methemoglobinemia?

Back: Cytochrome $b_5$ reductase (Methemoglobin reductase).

Front: Treatment for symptomatic Methemoglobinemia?

Back: Methylene blue (reduces $Fe^{3+}$ back to $Fe^{2+}$).

Front: Clinical "classic" presentation of Methemoglobinemia?

Back: Cyanosis (chocolate-colored blood) that does not improve with supplemental Oxygen.

Section 17: Bone Marrow & Myeloproliferative Nuances

Front: Diagnostic hallmark of Myelodysplastic Syndromes (MDS)?

Back: "Ringed Sideroblasts" and "Pseudo-Pelger-Huët anomaly" (bilobed neutrophils).

Front: Major risk of Myelodysplastic Syndrome (MDS)?

Back: Transformation into Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML).

Front: In Polycythemia Vera, what happens to Serum Erythropoietin (EPO) levels?

Back: EPO is low (negative feedback from high RBC mass).

Front: Difference between "Primary" and "Secondary" Polycythemia?

Back: Primary (PV) has low EPO; Secondary (Altitude, Smoking, RCC) has high EPO.

Front: Term for marrow being replaced by "non-hematopoietic" material (tumor, granuloma)?

Back: Myelophthisis.

Section 18: Pediatric & Hereditary Hematology

Front: Most common cause of Hydrops Fetalis?

Back: $\alpha$-Thalassemia (4-gene deletion; Hb Barts / $\gamma_4$).

Front: Most common clinical presentation of Diamond-Blackfan Anemia?

Back: Rapid-onset anemia in the 1st year of life, short stature, and craniofacial/thumb anomalies.

Front: Genetic defect in Fanconi Anemia?

Back: DNA cross-link repair defect; leads to marrow failure and increased risk of AML.

Front: Typical physical findings in Fanconi Anemia?

Back: Cafe-au-lait spots, short stature, and absent/hypoplastic thumbs.

Front: HbH disease involves how many $\alpha$-globin gene deletions?

Back: 3 gene deletions ($\beta_4$ tetramers).

Section 19: High-Yield Clinical Signs

Front: What is "Extramedullary Hematopoiesis"?

Back: RBC production outside the marrow (Spleen/Liver), often seen in Thalassemia or Myelofibrosis.

Front: Pathogenesis of "Bony Bossing" in Thalassemia?

Back: Reactive marrow hyperplasia expanding the marrow space, leading to "Chipmunk facies."

Front: Cause of "Pain Crisis" in Sickle Cell Disease?

Back: Vaso-occlusion (infarction) in small vessels due to sickled RBCs.

Front: Immediate treatment for Heparin-Induced Thrombocytopenia (HIT)?

Back: Stop Heparin immediately and start a Direct Thrombin Inhibitor (e.g., Argatroban).

Front: Lab marker for effective B12 replacement therapy?

Back: Significant increase in Reticulocyte count within 3-5 days.

Front: Most common cause of Vitamin K deficiency in newborns?

Back: Lack of gut flora and poor placental transfer (prevented by Vitamin K injection at birth).

Front: Marker for identifying "Blast" cells in Acute Leukemia?

Back: CD34 (marker of immaturity).

Front: Most common genetic mutation in Systemic Mastocytosis?

Back: KIT D816V mutation.

Front: Enzyme deficient in Congenital Erythropoietic Porphyria (Gunther Disease)?

Back: Uroporphyrinogen III cosynthase.

Front: Effect of Hemolysis on Serum LDH (Lactate Dehydrogenase) and 왜?

Back: LDH is significantly increased; it is an enzyme abundant inside RBCs released upon rupture.

Front: What is the "Ham Test" (Acidified Serum Test) historically used for?

Back: Paroxysmal Nocturnal Hemoglobinuria (PNH) – superseded by flow cytometry for CD55/59.

Section 21: Clinical Morphology & Specific Smear Findings

Front: "Pencil Cells" on peripheral smear are characteristic of?

Back: Iron Deficiency Anemia.

Front: "Acanthocytes" (Spur Cells) are most commonly associated with?

Back: Liver disease or Abetalipoproteinemia.

Front: "Echinocytes" (Burr Cells) are most commonly associated with?

Back: Uremia (Renal Failure) or Pyruvate Kinase deficiency.

Back: Bite Cells (seen in G6PD deficiency).

Section 22: Rare Coagulopathies & Vessel Disorders

Front: Mechanism of Hereditary Hemorrhagic Telangiectasia (Osler-Weber-Rendu)?

Back: Autosomal dominant disorder of blood vessels leading to telangiectasias and AVMs.

Front: Screening test for Factor XIII deficiency?

Back: 5-Molar Urea Solubility Test (Standard PT/PTT will be normal).

Front: Function of Protein S?

Back: Acts as a cofactor for Protein C to inactivate Factors Va and VIIIa.

Front: What is "Lupus Anticoagulant" paradox?

Back: It prolongs PTT in vitro but causes a hypercoagulable state (thrombosis) in vivo.

Front: Most common cause of "Acquired" Hemophilia?

Back: Autoantibodies against Factor VIII (often postpartum or malignancy-associated).

Section 23: The Spleen & Reticuloendothelial System

Front: What are "Pitting" and "Culling" in the spleen?

Back: Pitting: Removing inclusions (Heinz bodies) without destroying the cell. Culling: Removing damaged RBCs entirely.

Front: Immunological consequence of Splenectomy?

Back: Decreased IgM and decreased complement activation, leading to susceptibility to encapsulated organisms.

Front: The "Encapsulated Trio" to vaccinate against post-splenectomy?

Back: S. pneumoniae, H. influenzae type b, and N. meningitidis.

Front: Cause of "Gaucher Cells" (crinkled paper appearance) in the marrow?

Back: Glucocerebrosidase deficiency.

Section 24: Treatment-Specific High Yields

Front: Specific antidote for Dabigatran?

Back: Idarucizumab.

Front: Specific antidote for Factor Xa inhibitors (Rivaroxaban/Apixaban)?

Back: Andexanet alfa.

Front: Primary side effect of Desmopressin (DDAVP) when treating vWD?

Back: Hyponatremia (due to its ADH-like effect).

Front: What is "Leucovorin Rescue" used for?

Back: To reduce the toxicity of high-dose Methotrexate by providing a source of reduced folate.

Section 25: Quantitative Indices & Formulae

Front: Formula for Mean Corpuscular Hemoglobin Concentration (MCHC)?

Back: $MCHC = (Hemoglobin / Hematocrit) \times 100$.

Front: What condition is the ONLY one with an increased MCHC?

Back: Hereditary Spherocytosis (due to membrane loss and cellular dehydration).

Front: Corrected Reticulocyte Count formula?

Back: $Retics \% \times (Hct / 45)$.

Front: How many days does it take for a Reticulocyte to mature in the peripheral blood?

Back: Approximately 1 day.

Section 26: Differential Diagnoses (The "Final Boss" Cards)

Front: Differentiate "Iron Deficiency" vs "Anemia of Chronic Disease" via TIBC?

Back: Iron Def: TIBC is $\uparrow$. Chronic Disease: TIBC is $\downarrow$.

Front: Differentiate "Leukemoid Reaction" vs "CML" via Cytogenetics?

Back: CML will have $t(9;22)$; Leukemoid reaction will be cytogenetically normal.

Front: Differentiate "TTP" vs "HUS" in clinical presentation?

Back: HUS typically presents with bloody diarrhea (EHEC) and predominant renal failure; TTP has more neurological symptoms.

Front: Differentiate "Follicular Lymphoma" vs "Reactive Lymph Node Hyperplasia"?

Back: Follicular Lymphoma: BCL-2 (+), lacks tingible body macrophages, monoclonal.

Front: Differentiate "Multiple Myeloma" vs "Primary Amyloidosis" (AL)?

Back: MM is a plasma cell malignancy; AL is a protein folding disorder (misfolded light chains) often secondary to a plasma cell dyscrasia.

Front: Most common inherited cause of Sideroblastic Anemia?

Back: X-linked deficiency of ALAS2 (erythroid-specific $\delta$-aminolevulinic acid synthase).

Front: Direct effect of Lead on the heme pathway enzymes?

Back: Inhibits Ferrochelatase and ALA Dehydratase.

Front: Which enzyme deficiency is protected against P. falciparum malaria?

Back: G6PD deficiency and Pyruvate Kinase deficiency.

Front: Mechanism of Pyruvate Kinase (PK) deficiency?

Back: $\downarrow$ ATP $\rightarrow$ rigid RBCs $\rightarrow$ extravascular hemolysis in the splenic red pulp.

Front: Distinctive finding on peripheral smear for Pyruvate Kinase deficiency?

Back: Echinocytes (Burr cells) that are not related to uremia.

Section 28: Lymphoid and Myeloid Nuances

Front: What is the "TRAP" stain?

Back: Tartrate-Resistant Acid Phosphatase; used for Hairy Cell Leukemia.

Front: Typical immunophenotype for Hairy Cell Leukemia?

Back: CD11c, CD25, CD103.

Front: The "Sezary Syndrome" triad?

Back: Erythroderma, Lymphadenopathy, and circulating Sezary cells (cerebriform nuclei).

Front: Distinction between Myelofibrosis and CML on marrow biopsy?

Back: Myelofibrosis shows "collagen fibrosis" (Silver/Reticulin stain); CML is hypercellular with myeloid maturation.

Front: What is "Aleukemic Leukemia"?

Back: Leukemia where the abnormal cells are confined to the marrow; the peripheral WBC count is normal or low.

Section 29: Coagulation and Thrombosis "Deep Dives"

Front: Why is the "Bleeding Time" usually normal in Hemophilia?

Back: Bleeding time tests primary hemostasis (platelets); Hemophilia is a defect in secondary hemostasis (clotting factors).

Front: Mechanism of Tranexamic Acid (TXA)?

Back: Antifibrinolytic; inhibits Plasminogen activation to Plasmin.

Front: What is "Glucanzmann Thrombasthenia" vs "Bernard-Soulier" on Ristocetin test?

Back: Bernard-Soulier: No agglutination with Ristocetin. Glanzmann: Normal agglutination with Ristocetin (but fails with ADP/Collagen).

Front: What is the "Russell Viper Venom Time" (dRVVT) used for?

Back: To confirm the presence of Lupus Anticoagulant (Antiphospholipid Syndrome).

Section 30: Hemoglobin Variants and Advanced Physiology

Front: What is "Hemoglobin Barts"?

Back: $\gamma_4$ (four gamma chains); seen in 4-gene deletion $\alpha$-Thalassemia (Hydrops Fetalis).

Front: What is "Hemoglobin H"?

Back: $\beta_4$ (four beta chains); seen in 3-gene deletion $\alpha$-Thalassemia.

Front: Effect of "Acidosis" on the P50 of the Oxygen-Hb curve?

Back: Increases the P50 (shifts curve to the right, meaning lower affinity).

Front: What is the "P50"?

Back: The partial pressure of oxygen at which hemoglobin is 50% saturated.

Front: Which Hemoglobin variant is most "Polymerization-prone"?

Back: Deoxygenated HbS.

Section 31: Therapeutic Monitoring and Toxicology

Front: Why is Cimetidine or Sulfa drugs dangerous for G6PD patients?

Back: They induce oxidative stress, leading to Hemolysis.

Front: Treatment of choice for "Iron Overload" (Hemochromatosis)?

Back: Therapeutic phlebotomy (or Deferoxamine/Deferasirox chelation if phlebotomy is contraindicated).

Front: Common side effect of EPO (Erythropoietin) therapy?

Back: Hypertension and increased risk of thromboembolic events.

Front: What is the "D-dimer" specifically measuring?

Back: Fibrin degradation products that have been cross-linked by Factor XIII.

Section 32: Clinical "Associations" (Rapid Fire)

Front: Association: Thymoma?

Back: Pure Red Cell Aplasia (PRCA) or Myasthenia Gravis.

Front: Association: "Doughnut-shaped" nuclei in neutrophils?

Back: Pseudo-Pelger-Huet anomaly (Myelodysplastic Syndrome).

Front: Association: "Hyper-segmented" neutrophils + Glossitis?
