Front: Fundamental definition of anemia based on lab values?

Back: Decrease in circulating red blood cell (RBC) mass, typically measured by Hemoglobin (Hb), Hematocrit (Hct), or RBC count.

Front: Morphologic classification based on Mean Corpuscular Volume (MCV)?

Back: Microcytic (<80 fL), Normocytic (80–100 fL), and Macrocytic (>100 fL).

Front: Primary cause of Microcytic Anemia related to heme synthesis?

Back: Iron deficiency (most common), Sideroblastic anemia, and Lead poisoning.

Front: Primary cause of Microcytic Anemia related to globin synthesis?

Back: Thalassemia.

Front: Most common cause of Iron Deficiency Anemia in adult males and postmenopausal females?

Back: Chronic GI bleeding (must rule out malignancy).

Front: Distinctive lab profile of Iron Deficiency Anemia?

Back: Low serum iron, high TIBC (Total Iron Binding Capacity), low Ferritin, and high RDW.

Front: Classic clinical triad of Plummer-Vinson Syndrome?

Back: Iron deficiency anemia, esophageal webs, and atrophic glossitis.

Front: Genetic defect in $\alpha$-Thalassemia?

Back: Gene deletion on Chromosome 16.

Front: Genetic defect in $\beta$-Thalassemia?

Back: Point mutations in splice sites or promoter sequences on Chromosome 11.

Front: Appearance of RBCs on blood smear in Thalassemia?

Back: Target cells and microcytic, hypochromic cells.

Front: Bone marrow finding in Sideroblastic Anemia?

Back: Ring sideroblasts (iron-laden mitochondria encircling the nucleus).

Front: Mechanism of Anemia of Chronic Disease (ACD)?

Back: Chronic inflammation increases Hepcidin, which sequesters iron in macrophages and inhibits ferroportin.

Front: Lab findings in Anemia of Chronic Disease?

Back: Low serum iron, low TIBC, and high/normal Ferritin.

Front: Primary mechanism of Megaloblastic Anemia?

Back: Impaired DNA synthesis leading to delayed nuclear maturation relative to cytoplasm.

Front: Key smear finding in Vitamin B12 or Folate deficiency?

Back: Hypersegmented neutrophils (>5 lobes) and oval macrocytes.

Front: Neurological symptom unique to Vitamin B12 deficiency (not in Folate)?

Back: Subacute combined degeneration of the spinal cord (dorsal columns/lateral corticospinal tracts).

Front: Common cause of B12 deficiency involving an autoimmune process?

Back: Pernicious Anemia (destruction of gastric parietal cells/Intrinsic Factor).

Front: Reticulocyte response in Hemolytic Anemias?

Back: Increased Reticulocyte Count (RC), often >3% (corrected).

Front: Distinguishing lab feature of Intravascular Hemolysis?

Back: Low Haptoglobin, Hemoglobinuria, and Hemosiderinuria.

Front: Inheritance pattern and defect in Hereditary Spherocytosis?

Back: Autosomal dominant; defect in RBC membrane proteins (Ankyrin, Spectrin, or Band 3).

Front: Diagnostic test for Hereditary Spherocytosis?

Back: Osmotic Fragility Test or Eosin-5-maleimide (EMA) binding test.

Front: Precise molecular mutation in Sickle Cell Anemia?

Back: Glutamic acid replaced by Valine at position 6 of the $\beta$-globin chain.

Front: Primary cause of RBC sickling in HbS patients?

Back: Deoxygenation (hypoxia), dehydration, or acidosis.

Front: Most common cause of osteomyelitis in Sickle Cell patients?

Back: Salmonella.

Front: Mutation in Hemoglobin C (HbC)?

Back: Glutamic acid replaced by Lysine at position 6 of the $\beta$-globin chain.

Front: Mechanism of Paroxysmal Nocturnal Hemoglobinuria (PNH)?

Back: Acquired PIGA gene mutation leading to loss of GPI anchor and decay-accelerating factor (CD55/59).

Front: Classic presentation of PNH?

Back: Dark urine in the morning and increased risk of venous thrombosis (e.g., Budd-Chiari syndrome).

Front: Enzyme deficiency causing episodic hemolysis after oxidative stress?

Back: Glucose-6-Phosphate Dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency.

Front: Key smear findings in G6PD deficiency?

Back: Heinz bodies (denatured Hb) and Bite cells.

Front: Differentiation between Warm and Cold Autoimmune Hemolytic Anemia?

Back: Warm is IgG-mediated (extravascular); Cold is IgM-mediated (complement-mediated).

White Blood Cell Disorders: Leukemias & Lymphomas

Front: Characterization of Acute Leukemia in the bone marrow?

Back: >20% blasts in the marrow.

Front: Pathognomonic biopsy finding for Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML)?

Back: Auer Rods (peroxidase-positive cytoplasmic inclusions).

Front: Cytogenetic translocation associated with Acute Promyelocytic Leukemia (APL)?

Back: $t(15;17)$ involving the Retinoic Acid Receptor (RAR).

Front: Standard treatment to induce differentiation in APL?

Back: All-trans retinoic acid (ATRA).

Front: Most common pediatric malignancy?

Back: Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia (ALL).

Front: Marker for Pre-B and Pre-T ALL?

Back: TdT (Terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase).

Front: Common translocation in B-ALL with a better prognosis?

Back: $t(12;21)$.

Front: Hallmark lab finding in Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia (CLL)?

Back: Absolute lymphocytosis and "Smudge cells" on peripheral smear.

Front: Genetic hallmark of Chronic Myeloid Leukemia (CML)?

Back: Philadelphia Chromosome $t(9;22)$ forming the $BCR-ABL$ fusion gene.

Front: Distinguishing CML from a Leukemoid Reaction?

Back: CML has low Leukocyte Alkaline Phosphatase (LAP) score and increased basophils.

Front: Target of Imatinib therapy?

Back: Tyrosine kinase activity of the $BCR-ABL$ protein.

Front: Mutation commonly found in Polycythemia Vera and Essential Thrombocythemia?

Back: $JAK2$ V617F mutation.

Front: Clinical sign of Polycythemia Vera after a hot shower?

Back: Aquagenic pruritus (due to histamine release from increased mast cells).

Front: Pathological cell type in Hodgkin Lymphoma?

Back: Reed-Sternberg (RS) cells (CD15+ and CD30+).

Front: Most common subtype of Hodgkin Lymphoma?

Back: Nodular Sclerosis (often presents with a mediastinal mass in young females).

Front: Translocation and gene involved in Follicular Lymphoma?

Back: $t(14;18)$ involving $BCL-2$ overexpression (inhibits apoptosis).

Front: Translocation and gene involved in Burkitt Lymphoma?

Back: $t(8;14)$ involving $c-MYC$ overexpression.

Front: Classic histological appearance of Burkitt Lymphoma?

Back: "Starry sky" appearance (tingible body macrophages among neoplastic B cells).

Front: Lymphoma associated with chronic H. pylori infection?

Back: MALToma (Marginal Zone Lymphoma).

Plasma Cell & Myeloid Dyscrasias

Front: Triad of Multiple Myeloma?

Back: Lytic bone lesions, hypercalcemia, and monoclonal (M) protein spike.

Front: Key finding in the urine of Multiple Myeloma patients?

Back: Bence-Jones proteins (free light chains).

Front: Characteristic RBC arrangement on smear in Multiple Myeloma?

Back: Rouleaux formation (stacks of coins due to high serum protein).

Front: Definition of MGUS (Monoclonal Gammopathy of Undetermined Significance)?

Back: M-spike <3 g/dL, <10% plasma cells in marrow, and no CRAB symptoms.

Front: Features of Waldenström Macroglobulinemia?

Back: Monoclonal IgM spike, hyperviscosity syndrome, and no lytic bone lesions.

Hemostasis & Coagulation

Front: Primary hemostasis involves which two components?

Back: Platelets and the vessel wall.

Front: Adhesion molecule for platelets to subendothelial collagen?

Back: von Willebrand Factor (vWF) binding to GP Ib.

Front: Common drug that irreversibly inhibits Cyclooxygenase (COX-1)?

Back: Aspirin.

Front: Lab finding that distinguishes Bernard-Soulier from Glanzmann Thrombasthenia?

Back: Bernard-Soulier has large platelets and low GP Ib; Glanzmann has low GP IIb/IIIa.

Front: Classic clinical presentation of Immune Thrombocytopenic Purpura (ITP)?

Back: Isolated thrombocytopenia following a viral infection, caused by anti-GP IIb/IIIa antibodies.

Front: The "Pentad" of Thrombotic Thrombocytopenic Purpura (TTP)?

Back: Fever, Anemia (MAHA), Thrombocytopenia, Renal failure, and Neurologic symptoms.

Front: Enzyme deficiency in TTP?

Back: ADAMTS13 (vWF cleaving protease).

Front: Most common inherited bleeding disorder?

Back: von Willebrand Disease (vWD).

Front: Effect of vWD on lab values?

Back: Increased Bleeding Time and potentially increased PTT (vWF stabilizes Factor VIII).

Front: Deficiency in Hemophilia A?

Back: Factor VIII (X-linked recessive).

Front: Deficiency in Hemophilia B?

Back: Factor IX (X-linked recessive).

Front: Definition of Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC)?

Back: Pathologic activation of the coagulation cascade leading to widespread microthrombi and subsequent consumption of factors/platelets.

Front: Sensitive lab marker for DIC?

Back: Elevated D-dimer.

Front: Most common inherited cause of Hypercoagulability (thrombophilia)?

Back: Factor V Leiden (resistance to Protein C cleavage).

Myelodysplastic & Others

Front: Definition of Myelodysplastic Syndromes (MDS)?

Back: Cytopenias with hypercellular marrow and maturation defects (ineffective hematopoiesis).

Front: Bone marrow finding in Primary Myelofibrosis?

Back: Extensive collagen fibrosis (Silver stain positive) and "Dry tap" on aspiration.

Front: Characteristic RBC shape in Primary Myelofibrosis?

Back: Teardrop cells (Dacrocytes).

Front: Function of Protein C and S?

Back: Inactivate Factors Va and VIIIa (act as anticoagulants).

Front: Warfarin-induced skin necrosis is associated with which deficiency?

Back: Protein C deficiency.

Front: Mechanism of Heparin-Induced Thrombocytopenia (HIT)?

Back: IgG antibodies against Heparin-Platelet Factor 4 (PF4) complexes.

Front: Preferred anticoagulant in HIT?

Back: Direct Thrombin Inhibitors (e.g., Argatroban).

Front: Origin of the Thymic Cortex?

Back: Third Pharyngeal Pouch.

Front: Characteristic cell in Langerhans Cell Histiocytosis?

Back: Birbeck granules (tennis racket shape on EM).

Front: Most common cause of Eosinophilia?

Back: Parasitic infections, Allergies, or Asthma (NAACP: Neoplasia, Asthma, Allergy, Collagen vascular disease, Parasites).

Front: Significance of a "Left Shift" in a WBC differential?

Back: Increased proportion of immature neutrophils (band cells), indicating acute infection or inflammation.

Front: Lab finding in Iron Overload (Hemochromatosis)?

Back: High Ferritin, high Serum Iron, and high Transferrin Saturation (>45-50%).

Front: Effect of Lead Poisoning on RBCs?

Back: Basophilic stippling and inhibition of Ferrochelatase/ALA dehydratase.

Front: Vitamin required for the synthesis of ALA (heme precursor)?

Back: Vitamin B6 (Pyridoxine).

Front: Primary site of Erythropoietin (EPO) production?

Back: Peritubular interstitial cells of the kidney.

Front: Typical MCV in Sideroblastic Anemia?

Back: Microcytic (can sometimes be dimorphic).

Front: Role of Hepcidin in iron metabolism?

Back: It binds to Ferroportin to degrade it, preventing iron export from enterocytes and macrophages.

Front: Cause of "Crew-cut" appearance on skull X-ray?

Back: Expansion of marrow space due to severe erythroid hyperplasia (e.g., Sickle Cell or Thalassemia).

Front: Difference between Lymphoma and Leukemia?

Back: Lymphoma arises as a discrete mass in nodes; Leukemia involves lymphoid or myeloid cells in bone marrow and blood.

Front: Characteristic finding in infectious mononucleosis (EBV)?

Back: Reactive (atypical) CD8+ T cells (Downey cells).

Front: Lymph node region first involved in localized Hodgkin Lymphoma?

Back: Cervical or mediastinal nodes.

Front: Define Myelophthisic Anemia?

Back: Displacement of marrow by infiltrating processes (e.g., metastatic cancer, fibrosis).

Front: Lab distinction between Folate and B12 deficiency regarding Methylmalonic Acid (MMA)?

Back: B12 deficiency has high MMA; Folate deficiency has normal MMA.

Front: Common cause of secondary Polycythemia?

Back: Hypoxia (lung disease, high altitude) or EPO-secreting tumors (Renal cell carcinoma).

Front: Marker for Myeloid differentiation?

Back: Myeloperoxidase (MPO).

Front: Marker for Megakaryocytic differentiation?

Back: CD41 or CD61.

Front: Primary lab screening for Lupus Anticoagulant?

Back: Prolonged PTT that does not correct with a 1:1 mixing study (inhibitor present).

Front: Target of Desmopressin (DDAVP) in vWD?

Back: Induces release of vWF from Weibel-Palade bodies of endothelial cells.

Front: Clinical feature of Vitamin K deficiency?

Back: Bleeding (increased PT and PTT) due to decreased Factors II, VII, IX, X, C, and S.

Front: Source of Vitamin K in humans?

Back: Dietary green leafy vegetables and bacterial synthesis in the gut.

Front: Cause of Splenomegaly in Pyruvate Kinase deficiency?

Back: Work hypertrophy from removing damaged RBCs (extravascular hemolysis).

Front: Expected lab change in Chronic Blood Loss?

Back: Development of Iron Deficiency Anemia.

Front: Major risk of untreated Polycythemia Vera?

Back: Hyperviscosity leading to thrombosis (stroke, MI).

Front: Defining feature of "Blast Crisis" in CML?

Back: Progression of CML to Acute Leukemia (AML or ALL).

Front: Key diagnostic marker for Hairy Cell Leukemia?

Back: TRAP positive (Tartrate-Resistant Acid Phosphatase).

Front: Treatment for Hairy Cell Leukemia?

Back: Cladribine (2-CDA).

Front: Common location for Extranodal Marginal Zone Lymphoma?

Back: Salivary glands (Sjögren), Thyroid (Hashimoto), or Stomach (H. pylori).

Front: Typical patient profile for Mantle Cell Lymphoma?

Back: Older males; presents with late-stage disease/lymphadenopathy.

Front: Translocation for Mantle Cell Lymphoma?

Back: $t(11;14)$ involving Cyclin D1.

Front: Significance of the "B symptoms" in Lymphoma?

Back: Fever, night sweats, and weight loss (indicate poorer prognosis).

Front: Function of Antithrombin III?

Back: Inhibits Thrombin and Factor Xa; potentiated by Heparin.

Front: Common cause of Sideroblastic Anemia (acquired)?

Back: Alcoholism, Lead poisoning, or Isoniazid (B6 deficiency).

Front: Effect of Splenectomy on RBC morphology?

Back: Presence of Howell-Jolly bodies (nuclear remnants).

Front: Mechanism of Schistocyte formation?

Back: Shear stress as RBCs pass through fibrin mesh (Microangiopathic Hemolytic Anemia).

Front: Laboratory feature of Hemolytic Disease of the Newborn (Rh incompatibility)?

Back: Positive Direct Coombs test on fetal RBCs.

Front: Differentiation between HbS trait and disease on Electrophoresis?

Back: Trait (HbAS) has <50% HbS; Disease (HbSS) has >90% HbS and no HbA.

Front: Relationship between LDH and Hemolysis?

Back: LDH is an intracellular enzyme; levels rise significantly during RBC destruction.

Front: Clinical significance of Hairy Cell Leukemia "dry tap"?

Back: Marrow fibrosis prevents aspiration.

Front: Most common presentation of Follicular Lymphoma?

Back: Painless "waxing and waning" lymphadenopathy.

Front: Role of the Mantle Zone in a lymph node?

Back: Area of B cells surrounding the germinal center.

Front: Characteristic of Adult T-cell Leukemia/Lymphoma (ATLL)?

Back: Associated with HTLV-1; presents with skin lesions and hypercalcemia.

Front: Define Myeloid:Erythroid (M:E) ratio?

Back: The ratio of myeloid elements to nucleated erythroid elements in the marrow (Normal is ~3:1).

Front: Reason for decreased M:E ratio?

Back: Erythroid hyperplasia (hemolysis) or myeloid hypoplasia.

Front: Common trigger for Aplastic Crisis in Sickle Cell or Spherocytosis?

Back: Parvovirus B19 infection (infects erythroid precursors).

Front: Definition of Aplastic Anemia?

Back: Pancytopenia with hypocellular bone marrow (fatty infiltration).

Front: Common drugs causing Aplastic Anemia?

Back: Carbamazepine, Chloramphenicol, Sulfonamides, and Chemotherapy.

Front: Morphologic hallmark of Myelodysplastic Syndrome?

Back: Pseudo-Pelger-Huët cells (neutrophils with only two nuclear lobes).

Front: Key difference between Acute and Chronic Leukemias?

Back: Acute = proliferation of blasts (immature); Chronic = proliferation of mature/differentiating cells.

Front: Most common cause of death in Multiple Myeloma?

Back: Infection (due to loss of humoral immunity/hypogammaglobulinemia).

Front: Classic finding in Amyloidosis associated with Multiple Myeloma?

Back: Apple-green birefringence under polarized light with Congo Red stain.

Front: Common cause of False Positive VDRL/RPR?

Back: Antiphospholipid Syndrome (Lupus anticoagulant/Anticardiolipin antibodies).

Front: Preferred diagnostic step for suspected Lymphoma?

Back: Excisional lymph node biopsy (rather than Fine Needle Aspiration).

Front: Composition of Hemoglobin A2?

Back: Two alpha and two delta chains ($\alpha_2\delta_2$).

Front: Dominant hemoglobin type in a patient with $\beta$-Thalassemia major?

Back: Hemoglobin F ($\alpha_2\gamma_2$).

Front: Reason for "Chipmunk facies" in Thalassemia?

Back: Extramedullary hematopoiesis causing expansion of facial bones.

Front: Protective benefit of Sickle Cell Trait?

Back: Resistance to Plasmodium falciparum malaria.

Front: Leading cause of death in adult Sickle Cell patients?

Back: Acute Chest Syndrome.

Front: Why is Hydroxyurea used in Sickle Cell Anemia?

Back: It increases the production of Hemoglobin F, which interferes with the polymerization of HbS.

Front: Clinical consequence of Autosplenectomy in Sickle Cell patients?

Back: Increased susceptibility to encapsulated organisms (S. pneumoniae, H. influenzae).

White Cell & Lymphoid Pathology: Advanced

Front: Most common translocation in Follicular Lymphoma?

Back: $t(14;18)$.

Front: Protein overexpressed in Mantle Cell Lymphoma?

Back: Cyclin D1 (promotes G1 to S phase transition).

Front: Defining immunophenotype of Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia (CLL)?

Back: CD5, CD19, CD20, and CD23 positive.

Front: Common transformation of CLL into Diffuse Large B-Cell Lymphoma (DLBCL)?

Back: Richter Transformation.

Front: Classic presentation of Mycosis Fungoides?

Back: Skin patches, plaques, or tumors characterized by Pautrier microabscesses.

Front: Definition of Sézary Syndrome?

Back: Leukemic phase of Mycosis Fungoides with cerebriform nuclei on blood smear.

Front: Histological hallmark of Nodular Sclerosis Hodgkin Lymphoma?

Back: Lacunar cells (RS cell variants) and collagen bands circumscribing nodules.

Front: Lymphoma associated with Sjögren Syndrome?

Back: MALT Lymphoma of the salivary gland.

Myeloproliferative & Myelodysplastic Neoplasms

Front: Characteristic finding in the bone marrow of Essential Thrombocythemia?

Back: Increased numbers of enlarged, mature megakaryocytes in clusters.

Front: Primary cause of death in Myelofibrosis?

Back: Infection, hemorrhage, or transformation to AML.

Front: Definition of "Dry Tap" on bone marrow aspiration?

Back: Inability to aspirate marrow due to fibrosis (Myelofibrosis) or hypercellularity (Hairy Cell Leukemia).

Front: Differentiation between Polycythemia Vera and Stress Erythrocytosis (Gaisböck syndrome)?

Back: PV has increased RBC mass; Stress erythrocytosis has decreased plasma volume with normal RBC mass.

Coagulation & Platelet Dynamics

Front: Mechanism of Action of Tranexamic Acid?

Back: Antifibrinolytic that inhibits the conversion of plasminogen to plasmin.

Front: Result of a 1:1 Mixing Study in Factor VIII Deficiency?

Back: The PTT corrects to normal (indicates a deficiency, not an inhibitor).

Front: Most common site of bleeding in Hemophilia?

Back: Hemarthrosis (bleeding into joints).

Front: Effect of Uremia (Renal Failure) on platelet function?

Back: Qualitative platelet defect (impaired adhesion and aggregation) with normal platelet count.

Front: Diagnostic criteria for Antiphospholipid Syndrome?

Back: Clinical thrombosis (or pregnancy loss) plus positive Lupus Anticoagulant, Anti-cardiolipin, or Anti-$\beta$2-glycoprotein I antibodies.

Front: Mechanism of Aspirin's antiplatelet effect?

Back: Irreversible acetylation of COX-1, preventing Thromboxane A2 ($TXA_2$) synthesis.

Front: Why is PT (INR) used to monitor Warfarin?

Back: Factor VII has the shortest half-life and is the first to decrease under Warfarin therapy.

Specialized Morphological Findings

Front: Appearance of RBCs in Lead Poisoning?

Back: Basophilic stippling (due to inhibition of pyrimidine 5'-nucleotidase).

Front: Appearance of RBCs in Liver Disease?

Back: Acanthocytes (spur cells) or Target cells.

Front: Appearance of RBCs in Uremia?

Back: Echinocytes (burr cells).

Front: Finding on peripheral smear in Microangiopathic Hemolytic Anemia (MAHA)?

Back: Schistocytes (helmet cells).

Front: Histology of the "Starry Sky" pattern in Burkitt Lymphoma?

Back: Sheets of small malignant B cells (the "sky") with interspersed pale-stained tingible body macrophages (the "stars").

Additional Clinical Correlations

Front: Most common cause of B12 deficiency in the Western world?

Back: Pernicious Anemia.

Front: Site of iron absorption in the GI tract?

Back: Duodenum.

Front: Site of Folate absorption in the GI tract?

Back: Jejunum.

Front: Site of Vitamin B12 absorption in the GI tract?

Back: Terminal Ileum (requires Intrinsic Factor).

Front: Condition associated with an elevated Mean Corpuscular Hemoglobin Concentration (MCHC)?

Back: Hereditary Spherocytosis.

Front: Why are patients with Sickle Cell and Spherocytosis prone to Gallstones?

Back: Chronic hemolysis leads to increased bilirubin, forming pigmented (calcium bilirubinate) stones.

Front: Primary treatment for Iron Overload (Hemochromatosis)?

Back: Therapeutic phlebotomy.

Front: Mechanism of Anemia in Myelophthisic process?

Back: Space-occupying lesions (like metastatic cancer) replace hematopoietic tissue.

Front: The "Doughnut Cell" (Anaplastic Large Cell Lymphoma)?

Back: Hallmarks cells with kidney-shaped nuclei and ALK protein positivity.

Front: Major side effect of Heparin therapy?

Back: Bleeding and Heparin-Induced Thrombocytopenia (HIT).

Front: Reversal agent for Heparin?

Back: Protamine Sulfate.

Front: Reversal agent for Warfarin (immediate)?

Back: Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP) or Prothrombin Complex Concentrate (PCC).

Front: Reversal agent for Warfarin (delayed)?

Back: Vitamin K.

Front: Significance of hypersegmented neutrophils?

Back: Megaloblastic anemia (B12 or Folate deficiency).

Front: Primary pathology in Post-Transfusion Purpura?

Back: Development of antibodies against platelet antigens (usually HPA-1a) following transfusion.

Front: Common causes of non-megaloblastic macrocytic anemia?

Back: Alcoholism, liver disease, and Diamond-Blackfan anemia.

Front: Clinical features of Diamond-Blackfan Anemia?

Back: Rapid-onset anemia in the first year of life, short stature, and craniofacial or thumb anomalies.

Front: Difference between Diamond-Blackfan and Fanconi Anemia?

Back: Diamond-Blackfan is pure red cell aplasia; Fanconi is a global pancytopenia (DNA repair defect).

Front: Characteristic physical exam finding in Fanconi Anemia?

Back: Cafe-au-lait spots, short stature, and absent/hypoplastic radii or thumbs.

Front: Laboratory finding in Parvovirus B19-induced Aplastic Crisis?

Back: Acute drop in hemoglobin with a reticulocyte count near 0%.

Hemolysis & RBC Enzymes (Advanced)

Front: Inheritance of G6PD Deficiency?

Back: X-linked recessive.

Front: Why is the Direct Coombs test negative in G6PD deficiency?

Back: The hemolysis is due to enzyme deficiency and oxidative stress, not autoantibodies.

Front: Definition of the "Warm" antibody in AIHA?

Back: IgG antibody that reacts at body temperature ($37^\circ\text{C}$); usually causes extravascular hemolysis.

Front: Definition of the "Cold" antibody in AIHA?

Back: IgM antibody that reacts at low temperatures; often follows Mycoplasma pneumoniae or EBV.

Front: Why do Howell-Jolly bodies appear after splenectomy?

Back: The spleen is no longer present to "pit" or remove nuclear remnants from RBCs.

Myeloproliferative Neoplasms (In-depth)

Front: Genetic hallmark of Systemic Mastocytosis?

Back: KIT D816V mutation.

Front: Diagnostic finding in Systemic Mastocytosis?

Back: Increased serum tryptase and clusters of spindle-shaped mast cells in the bone marrow.

Front: Clinical presentation of Mast Cell Activation Syndrome (MCAS)?

Back: Recurrent anaphylaxis, flushing, and diarrhea triggered by heat, exercise, or alcohol.

Front: Distinguishing feature of Essential Thrombocythemia (ET) vs. Reactive Thrombocytosis?

Back: ET is a clonal malignancy (often JAK2+); Reactive is driven by cytokines (IL-6) in inflammation or iron deficiency.

Coagulation & Transfusion Medicine

Front: Major risk of Large-Volume Transfusion?

Back: Dilutional coagulopathy and hypocalcemia (due to citrate anticoagulant in blood bags).

Front: Leading cause of transfusion-related fatality?

Back: Transfusion-Related Acute Lung Injury (TRALI).

Front: Mechanism of TRALI?

Back: Donor anti-HLA or anti-neutrophil antibodies activate recipient neutrophils in the pulmonary vasculature.

Front: Difference between TRALI and TACO (Transfusion-Associated Circulatory Overload)?

Back: TRALI is non-cardiogenic pulmonary edema (normal PCWP); TACO is volume overload (high PCWP).

Front: Lab marker used to monitor Low Molecular Weight Heparin (LMWH)?

Back: Anti-Factor Xa assay (PT/PTT are usually unreliable for LMWH).

Front: Mechanism of Argatroban?

Back: Direct Thrombin Inhibitor (DTI).

Front: Primary treatment for a patient with Factor VIII Inhibitors (Acquired Hemophilia)?

Back: Bypassing agents like Activated Prothrombin Complex Concentrate (aPCC) or Recombinant Factor VIIa.

Lymphoid Neoplasms & Histiocytosis

Front: Immunophenotype of Mantle Cell Lymphoma?

Back: CD5+ and Cyclin D1+; CD23 negative (distinguishes it from CLL).

Front: Characteristic cell in Rosai-Dorfman Disease?

Back: Large histiocytes showing emperipolesis (engulfment of intact lymphocytes).

Front: Clinical triad of Hand-Schüller-Christian Disease?

Back: Diabetes insipidus, exophthalmos, and lytic bone lesions (a variant of Langerhans Cell Histiocytosis).

Front: "Letterer-Siwe Disease" patient demographic?

Back: Infants (<2 years old); presents as a skin rash and multi-organ involvement.

Front: Most common site for an Extranodal Marginal Zone Lymphoma?

Back: The stomach (associated with H. pylori).

General Pathology & Lab Signs

Front: Significance of a "Dry Tap" in Bone Marrow?

Back: Suggests Myelofibrosis, Hairy Cell Leukemia, or metastatic cancer (marrow is too fibrotic or packed to aspirate).

Front: Clinical significance of a "Leukemoid Reaction"?

Back: Exaggerated WBC elevation (>50,000) due to infection/stress, simulating leukemia but with mature cells.

Front: Definition of Pelger-Huët Anomaly?

Back: Benign inherited condition where neutrophils have bilobed (pince-nez) nuclei; can be "pseudo" in MDS.

Front: Primary metabolic abnormality in Tumor Lysis Syndrome?

Back: Hyperkalemia, hyperuricemia, hyperphosphatemia, and hypocalcemia.

Front: Consequence of splenic congestion (e.g., in portal hypertension)?

Back: Hypersplenism leading to sequestration and destruction of all three blood cell lines (pancytopenia).

Front: Defining feature of Splenic Infarcts?

Back: Wedge-shaped, pale areas of necrosis due to lack of collateral blood flow (common in Sickle Cell or Emboli).

Front: Condition associated with Thymic Hyperplasia?

Back: Myasthenia Gravis (present in ~65-75% of cases).

Front: Definition of a Thymoma?

Back: Neoplasm of thymic epithelial cells (can be associated with Pure Red Cell Aplasia or Myasthenia Gravis).

Advanced Flow Cytometry & Markers

Front: Pan-B cell markers used in flow cytometry?

Back: CD19, CD20, and CD21.

Front: Pan-T cell markers used in flow cytometry?

Back: CD2, CD3, CD5, and CD7.

Front: Surface marker used to identify hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs)?

Back: CD34.

Front: Diagnostic markers for Reed-Sternberg cells?

Back: CD15+ and CD30+.

Front: Classic immunophenotype for Mantle Cell Lymphoma?

Back: CD5+, CD19+, and Cyclin D1+.

Myeloid Neoplasms & Rare Variations

Front: Clinical feature of Chronic Myelomonocytic Leukemia (CMML)?

Back: Persistent monocytosis (>1,000/µL) and absence of the Philadelphia chromosome.

Front: Morphology of "Faggot cells" in APL?

Back: Promyelocytes containing bundles of Auer rods in the cytoplasm.

Front: Significance of "Hypersegmentation" in Myelodysplastic Syndrome?

Back: It is a sign of dysgranulopoiesis (though more common in Megaloblastic Anemia).

Hemostatic Disorders: Rare & Clinical

Front: Mechanism of Hemolytic Uremic Syndrome (HUS)?

Back: Shiga-like toxin from E. coli O157:H7 damages endothelial cells, leading to microthrombi.

Front: Primary clinical difference between HUS and TTP?

Back: HUS predominantly involves renal failure and follows diarrhea; TTP involves CNS symptoms and ADAMTS13 deficiency.

Front: Result of Ristocetin Cofactor Assay in von Willebrand Disease?

Back: Abnormal (vWF is required for Ristocetin-induced platelet agglutination).

Front: Effect of Vitamin C deficiency (Scurvy) on blood vessels?

Back: Defective collagen cross-linking leads to weakened vessel walls and easy bruising/petechiae.

Front: Pathology of Henoch-Schönlein Purpura (HSP)?

Back: IgA immune complex deposition in small vessels, causing palpable purpura and hematuria.

Special Stains & Diagnostics

Front: Purpose of the Prussian Blue stain in Hematology?

Back: To visualize iron (ferritin/hemosiderin) in macrophages or ring sideroblasts.

Front: Purpose of the Periodic Acid-Schiff (PAS) stain in Leukemia?

Back: Positive in some cases of ALL (shows "chunky" positivity).

Front: Significance of "Tartrate-Resistant Acid Phosphatase" (TRAP)?

Back: Specific marker for Hairy Cell Leukemia.

Front: Most accurate way to confirm a diagnosis of Hemoglobinopathy?

Back: High-Performance Liquid Chromatography (HPLC) or Hemoglobin Electrophoresis.

Red Cell Metabolism & Structure

Front: Enzyme responsible for converting Methemoglobin back to Hemoglobin?

Back: Cytochrome b5 reductase (NADH-methemoglobin reductase).

Front: Clinical presentation of Methemoglobinemia?

Back: Cyanosis that does not improve with oxygen and "chocolate-colored" blood.

Front: Role of Ankyrin and Spectrin in the RBC?

Back: They maintain the biconcave shape and deformability of the red cell membrane.

Front: Definition of "Pancytopenia"?

Back: Simultaneous decrease in RBCs, WBCs, and Platelets.

Front: Most common cause of vitamin B12 deficiency in a strict vegan?

Back: Dietary deficiency (B12 is only found in animal products).

Front: Diagnosis for a child with "painless" cervical lymphadenopathy and a "starry sky" biopsy?

Back: Burkitt Lymphoma.

Front: Most common pediatric cause of Vitamin B12 deficiency?

Back: Imerslund-Gräsbeck syndrome (selective malabsorption of B12 with proteinuria).

Front: Primary pathology in Schwachman-Diamond Syndrome?

Back: Pancreatic exocrine insufficiency and neutropenia (ribosomal stress).

Front: Defining RBC morphology in Abetalipoproteinemia?

Back: Acanthocytes (spur cells).

Front: Most common vascular complication of Sickle Cell Trait?

Back: Renal papillary necrosis (presenting as painless hematuria).

Morphological "Red Herrings" & Artifacts

Front: Cause of "Pappenheimer bodies" on a smear?

Back: Sideroblastic anemia (these are iron inclusions).

Front: Significance of "Cabot rings"?

Back: Remnants of mitotic spindles; seen in megaloblastic anemia or lead poisoning.

Front: Distinguishing feature of "Pseudo-thrombocytopenia"?

Back: Platelet clumping in the presence of EDTA; corrected by using a sodium citrate tube.

Front: Cause of "Stomatocytes" (mouth-shaped cells)?

Back: Alcoholism or hereditary stomatocytosis.

Advanced Pharmacology & Therapeutics

Front: Mechanism of Eculizumab in PNH?

Back: Monoclonal antibody against C5 complement protein (prevents MAC formation).

Front: Mechanism of Romiplostim in ITP?

Back: Thrombopoietin (TPO) receptor agonist (stimulates platelet production).

Front: Primary toxic effect of Doxorubicin in lymphoma treatment?

Back: Dilated cardiomyopathy.

Front: Mechanism of Cyclophosphamide-induced hemorrhagic cystitis?

Back: Accumulation of the metabolite Acrolein in the bladder.

Front: Drug used to prevent Hemorrhagic Cystitis?

Back: Mesna.

Systemic Associations

Front: Hematologic association with Thymoma?

Back: Pure Red Cell Aplasia (PRCA) or Good Syndrome (hypogammaglobulinemia).

Front: Triad of Felty Syndrome?

Back: Rheumatoid Arthritis, Splenomegaly, and Neutropenia.

Front: Meaning of "Extramedullary Hematopoiesis"?

Back: Blood cell production outside the bone marrow (e.g., liver/spleen), typically due to marrow failure.

Front: Finding in the bone marrow of Gaucher Disease?

Back: "Crinkled tissue paper" appearance of macrophages (glucocerebrosidase deficiency).

Front: Define "Hyperleukocytosis" (Leukostasis)?

Back: WBC >100,000; a medical emergency where high blast counts cause vascular occlusion and tissue hypoxia.

Front: Diagnostic significance of a "Dry Tap" in an elderly patient with massive splenomegaly?

Back: Highly suggestive of Primary Myelofibrosis or Hairy Cell Leukemia.
