Front: Definition of Zero-order elimination?

Back: Rate of elimination is constant regardless of plasma concentration (e.g., Ethanol, Aspirin at high doses).

Back: Constant fraction of drug is eliminated per unit time (rate depends on concentration).

Back: $MD = (C_p \times CL \times \tau) / F$.

Back: Approximately 4 to 5.

Back: $V_{max}$ remains unchanged; $K_m$ increases.

Back: $V_{max}$ decreases; $K_m$ remains unchanged.

Back: Oxidation, reduction, or hydrolysis (usually via Cytochrome P450).

Back: Conjugation (Glucuronidation, Acetylation, Sulfation) to make drugs polar for excretion.

Back: Phase II (prolonged exposure/desensitization); Phase I is worsened by AChE inhibitors.

Front: Mechanism of action of Theophylline?

Back: Inhibits Phosphodiesterase (PDE), increasing cAMP, and antagonizes Adenosine receptors.

Front: Why is Theophylline rarely used now?

Back: Narrow therapeutic index and many drug interactions (CYP450 metabolism).

Final Scrutiny Cards

Front: Mechanism of action of Prucalopride?

Back: 5-HT4 receptor agonist used for chronic idiopathic constipation.

Front: Mechanism of action of Rifaximin in Hepatic Encephalopathy?

Back: Non-absorbable antibiotic that kills ammonia-producing gut bacteria.

Front: Mechanism of action of Lactulose in Hepatic Encephalopathy?

Back: Converted by gut bacteria to lactic acid, trapping $NH_3$ as $NH_4^+$ in the lumen.

Front: Mechanism of action of Fenoldopam?

Back: Dopamine D1 receptor agonist; used in hypertensive emergencies to dilate renal/coronary vessels.

Front: Mechanism of action of Midodrine?

Back: Alpha-1 agonist used to treat symptomatic orthostatic hypotension.

Front: Genetic deficiency that increases the risk of hemolytic anemia with Primaquine or Sulfonamides?

Back: Glucose-6-Phosphate Dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency.

Front: Genetic variation that necessitates a lower dose of Warfarin?

Back: Polymorphisms in CYP2C9 or VKORC1.

Front: Effect of being a "Slow Acetylator" on Isoniazid (INH) therapy?

Back: Increased risk of peripheral neuropathy and drug-induced lupus.

Front: HLA allele associated with a high risk of Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS) when taking Abacavir?

Back: HLA-B*5701.

Front: HLA allele associated with SJS in Asian populations taking Carbamazepine?

Back: HLA-B*1502.

Advanced Cardiovascular & Autonomic Nuances

Front: Effect of "Low Dose" vs "High Dose" Epinephrine on Blood Pressure?

Back: Low dose decreases BP (Beta-2 vasodilation); High dose increases BP (Alpha-1 vasoconstriction).

Front: Primary side effect of Minoxidil (other than hypertrichosis)?

Back: Reflex tachycardia and fluid retention (should be given with a beta-blocker and diuretic).

Front: Mechanism of action of Sacubitril?

Back: Inhibits Neprilysin, increasing levels of atrial natriuretic peptide (ANP) and BNP.

Front: Why must ACE inhibitors be stopped 36 hours before starting Sacubitril/Valsartan?

Back: To avoid a significant increase in the risk of angioedema.

Front: Mechanism of "Nitrate Tolerance"?

Back: Depletion of sulfhydryl groups or oxidative stress; managed by a "nitrate-free interval" (usually at night).

Specialized Toxicology & Environmental

Front: Treatment for Lead poisoning in an adult with severe symptoms?

Back: Dimercaprol and EDTA.

Front: Mechanism of Arsenic poisoning?

Back: Inhibits lipoic acid, a necessary cofactor for Pyruvate Dehydrogenase.

Front: Classic clinical presentation of Arsenic poisoning?

Back: Garlic breath, rice-water stools, and "mees lines" (white lines on nails).

Front: Antidote for Arsenic poisoning?

Back: Dimercaprol.

Front: Mechanism of action of Cyanide?

Back: Inhibits Cytochrome c oxidase in the electron transport chain (inhibits aerobic respiration).

Neuro-Psychiatry & Behavioral Health (Advanced)

Front: Mechanism of action of Ramelteon?

Back: Melatonin receptor (MT1 and MT2) agonist used for insomnia.

Front: Mechanism of action of Suvorexant?

Back: Orexin (hypocretin) receptor antagonist used for insomnia.

Front: Drug of choice for Bulimia Nervosa?

Back: Fluoxetine.

Front: Mechanism of action of Atomoxetine?

Back: Selective Norepinephrine Reuptake Inhibitor (SNRI) used for ADHD.

Front: Why are MAO inhibitors contraindicated with Tyramine-rich foods (cheese, wine)?

Back: Tyramine displaces stored Norepinephrine, leading to a hypertensive crisis.

Hematology & Oncology (Niche Agents)

Front: Mechanism of action of Bortezomib?

Back: Proteasome inhibitor; induces apoptosis (used in Multiple Myeloma).

Front: Mechanism of action of Bevacizumab?

Back: Monoclonal antibody against VEGF (inhibits angiogenesis).

Front: Mechanism of action of Cetuximab?

Back: Monoclonal antibody against EGFR (Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor).

Front: Mechanism of action of Rasburicase?

Back: Recombinant uricase that catalyzes the metabolism of uric acid to allantoin.

Front: Common use for Rasburicase in Oncology?

Back: Prevention and treatment of Tumor Lysis Syndrome.

Clinical "Best" Practices (Quick Hitters)

Front: Best drug for treating hypertensive emergency in a patient with aortic dissection?

Back: Esmolol (requires rapid heart rate and BP control).

Front: Preferred anticoagulant for a pregnant patient with a DVT?

Back: Low Molecular Weight Heparin (LMWH), such as Enoxaparin.

Front: Preferred induction agent for a patient with asthma or hypotension?

Back: Ketamine (causes bronchodilation and sympathetic stimulation).

Front: First-line medication for trigeminal neuralgia?

Back: Carbamazepine.

Front: Best drug for prophylaxis of migraines?

Back: Propranolol, Topiramate, or Amitriptyline.

Front: Best drug for acute management of status epilepticus?

Back: IV Benzodiazepines (Lorazepam or Diazepam).

Final Check: Systemic "Oddities"

Front: Mechanism of action of Pentazocine?

Back: Opioid Kappa-receptor agonist and Mu-receptor partial agonist/antagonist.

Front: Risk of giving Pentazocine to a patient already on Morphine?

Back: May precipitate withdrawal due to Mu-receptor antagonism.

Front: Mechanism of action of Tranexamic Acid?

Back: Antifibrinolytic; inhibits Plasminogen activation.

Front: Drug that causes "Yellow-green" vision distortion?

Back: Digoxin.

Front: Drug that causes "Blue-tinted" vision (Cyanopsia)?

Back: Sildenafil (PDE-5 inhibitors).

Front: Mechanism of action of Denosumab?

Back: Monoclonal antibody against RANKL, inhibiting osteoclast maturation (used in Osteoporosis).

Front: Mechanism of action of Romosozumab?

Back: Inhibits Sclerostin, increasing bone formation and decreasing resorption.

Front: Mechanism of action of Evolocumab/Alirocumab?

Back: PCSK9 inhibitors; increase LDL receptor recycling to lower plasma LDL levels.

Front: Mechanism of action of Omalizumab?

Back: Binds unbound IgE to prevent it from binding Mast cells (used in allergic asthma).

Front: Mechanism of action of Dupilumab?

Back: IL-4 receptor alpha antagonist; inhibits IL-4 and IL-13 signaling (used in Eczema/Asthma).

Front: Mechanism of action of Ustekinumab?

Back: Monoclonal antibody against IL-12 and IL-23 (used in Psoriasis/Crohn's).

Advanced Oncology & Targeted Therapy

Front: Mechanism of action of Vemurafenib?

Back: Small molecule inhibitor of BRAF V600E mutated kinase (used in Melanoma).

Front: Mechanism of action of Olaparib?

Back: PARP inhibitor; prevents DNA repair in BRCA-deficient cells (synthetic lethality).

Front: Mechanism of action of Ipilimumab?

Back: CTLA-4 inhibitor; enhances T-cell activation against tumor cells.

Front: Mechanism of action of Abemaciclib/Palbociclib?

Back: CDK4/6 inhibitors; arrest the cell cycle in the G1 phase.

Critical Drug-Drug Interactions (Final)

Front: Interaction between Amiodarone and Warfarin?

Back: Amiodarone inhibits CYP2C9, leading to increased Warfarin levels and bleeding risk.

Front: Interaction between Macrolides (except Azithromycin) and Statins?

Back: Inhibition of CYP3A4 increases Statin concentration, leading to Rhabdomyolysis.

Front: Effect of NSAIDs on Lithium levels?

Back: Decreased renal clearance of Lithium, leading to toxicity.

Front: Interaction between Sildenafil and Alpha-1 blockers?

Back: Can lead to severe orthostatic hypotension (spaced 4 hours apart).

Systemic Miscellany & Clinical "Pearls"

Front: Drug of choice for treating malignant hyperthermia?

Back: Dantrolene.

Front: Mechanism of action of Midazolam?

Back: Short-acting Benzodiazepine used for procedural sedation.

Front: Drug that causes "Red Man Syndrome" due to histamine release?

Back: Vancomycin (prevented by slow infusion).

Front: Drug class that causes "Fanconi-like" syndrome?

Back: Ifosfamide (or expired Tetracyclines).

Front: Medication used to treat the EPS side effect "Akathisia"?

Back: Beta-blockers or Benzodiazepines.

Front: Antidote for anticholinergic toxicity?

Back: Physostigmine (tertiary amine that crosses the BBB).

Front: Antidote for Beta-blocker or Calcium Channel Blocker overdose?

Back: Glucagon and/or high-dose Insulin with Glucose.

Front: Mechanism of action of Cilostazol?

Back: PDE-3 inhibitor; increases cAMP in platelets (inhibits aggregation) and dilates arteries.

Front: Primary use for Cilostazol?

Back: Symptomatic treatment of Intermittent Claudication.

Front: Mechanism of action of Roflumilast?

Back: PDE-4 inhibitor used to reduce exacerbations in severe COPD.

Front: Drug used to treat nocturnal enuresis by decreasing urine volume?

Back: Desmopressin (Oral or Nasal spray).

Front: Drug used for rapid sequence intubation that is a nicotinic ACh receptor agonist?

Back: Succinylcholine.

Front: Major electrolyte side effect of Succinylcholine in burn or trauma patients?

Back: Severe Hyperkalemia.

Front: Mechanism of action of Sugammadex?

Back: Selective relaxant binding agent used to reverse Rocuronium or Vecuronium.

Front: Mechanism of action of Ivabradine?

Back: Selective inhibitor of the $I_f$ (funny) current in the SA node.

Front: Specific indication for Ivabradine?

Back: Chronic HFrEF in patients with a resting heart rate $\ge$ 70 bpm who are on max beta-blockers.

Front: Mechanism of action of Epoprostenol?

Back: Prostacyclin ($PGI_2$) analog used for Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension to cause vasodilation.

Front: Primary side effect of Minoxidil (systemic)?

Back: Hypertrichosis and pericardial effusion.

Front: Mechanism of action of Milrinone?

Back: PDE-3 inhibitor that increases cAMP to provide positive inotropy and systemic vasodilation.

Front: Unique mechanism of Nitroprusside toxicity?

Back: Cyanide toxicity due to the release of 5 cyanide groups per molecule.

Front: First-line medication for Raynaud’s phenomenon?

Back: Calcium Channel Blockers (specifically Dihydropyridines like Nifedipine).

Endocrine & Metabolic "Niches"

Front: Mechanism of action of Miglitol or Acarbose?

Back: Alpha-glucosidase inhibitors that delay carbohydrate absorption in the brush border.

Front: Mechanism of action of Pramlintide?

Back: Amylin analog that delays gastric emptying and suppresses glucagon secretion.

Front: Mechanism of action of Exenatide or Liraglutide?

Back: GLP-1 receptor agonists that increase glucose-dependent insulin secretion.

Front: Major side effect of GLP-1 agonists?

Back: Nausea, vomiting, and risk of Pancreatitis.

Front: Mechanism of action of Canagliflozin (SGLT2 inhibitors)?

Back: Inhibit glucose reabsorption in the Proximal Convoluted Futule.

specialized Cellular & Molecular Targets

Front: Mechanism of action of Dipyridamole?

Back: Inhibits phosphodiesterase and adenosine uptake to decrease platelet aggregation.

Front: Mechanism of action of Cilostazol in peripheral vascular disease?

Back: PDE-3 inhibitor that increases cAMP, causing vasodilation and anti-platelet effects.

Front: Mechanism of action of Abciximab vs. Eptifibatide?

Back: Both are GPIIb/IIIa inhibitors; Abciximab is a monoclonal antibody, Eptifibatide is a peptide.

Front: Mechanism of action of Tranexamic Acid?

Back: Antifibrinolytic that competitively inhibits plasminogen activation.

Front: Mechanism of action of Idarucizumab?

Back: Specific reversal agent (monoclonal antibody) for the direct thrombin inhibitor Dabigatran.

Sensory & Neuromuscular Extras

Front: Mechanism of action of Pilocarpine in Sjögren's Syndrome?

Back: Muscarinic agonist used to stimulate saliva and tear production.

Front: Mechanism of action of Varenicline (Chantix)?

Back: Partial agonist at the $\alpha_4\beta_2$ nicotinic acetylcholine receptor.

Front: Side effect of Sildenafil on the eyes?

Back: Cyanopsia (blue-tinted vision) due to inhibition of PDE-6 in the retina.

Front: Mechanism of action of Botulinum Toxin?

Back: Cleaves SNARE proteins to inhibit the release of Acetylcholine.

The "Step 1" Tie-Breakers (Toxins & Odds)

Front: Mechanism of St. Anthony's Fire (Ergotism)?

Back: Ergot alkaloids (e.g., Ergotamine) causing intense peripheral vasoconstriction and gangrene.

Front: Antidote for Theophylline overdose?

Back: Beta-blockers (for arrhythmias) and activated charcoal.

Front: Drug of choice for treating malignant hyperthermia triggered by volatile anesthetics?

Back: Dantrolene.

Front: Mechanism of action of Sodium Thiosulfate in cyanide poisoning?

Back: Provides a sulfur donor for the enzyme Rhodanese to convert cyanide to thiocyanate.

Front: Mechanism of action of Hydroxocobalamin in cyanide poisoning?

Back: Binds cyanide to form Cyanocobalamin (Vitamin B12), which is excreted in the urine.

Front: Mechanism of action of Riluzole in ALS?

Back: Reduces glutamate neurotransmission (excitotoxicity).

Front: Mechanism of action of Nusinersen?

Back: Antisense oligonucleotide used in Spinal Muscular Atrophy to increase SMN protein production.

Front: Mechanism of action of Ivacaftor in Cystic Fibrosis?

Back: CFTR potentiator that keeps the chloride channel open longer in patients with the G551D mutation.

Final High-Yield Interactions

Front: Interaction between Clozapine and Carbamazepine?

Back: Both can cause agranulocytosis; co-administration is strictly avoided.

Front: Interaction between Lithium and Thiazide Diuretics?

Back: Thiazides decrease lithium clearance, leading to potential lithium toxicity.

Front: Interaction between Spironolactone and ACE inhibitors?

Back: Increased risk of life-threatening hyperkalemia.

Front: Interaction between Aminoglycosides and Loop Diuretics?

Back: Synergistic ototoxicity.

Front: Interaction between Tetracyclines and Antacids/Iron?

Back: Divalent/trivalent cations chelate the drug, preventing absorption.

Front: Mechanism of action of Basiliximab?

Back: Monoclonal antibody that blocks the IL-2 receptor (CD25) on activated T cells.

Front: Mechanism of action of Fingolimod?

Back: Sphingosine-1-phosphate (S1P) receptor modulator that sequesters lymphocytes in lymph nodes.

Front: Clinical use of Filgrastim (G-CSF)?

Back: Recovery of bone marrow (neutrophils) after chemotherapy.

Front: Mechanism of action of Sargramostim (GM-CSF)?

Back: Stimulates both granulocyte and monocyte/macrophage lineages.

Front: Mechanism of action of Aldesleukin?

Back: Recombinant Interleukin-2 used in the treatment of Renal Cell Carcinoma and Melanoma.

Front: Mechanism of action of IFN-alpha?

Back: Used for Hepatitis B and C, Kaposi Sarcoma, and Hairy Cell Leukemia.

Advanced Oncology (The "Hidden" High-Yields)

Front: Mechanism of action of Asparaginase?

Back: Depletes Asparagine to inhibit protein synthesis in leukemic cells (ALL).

Front: Side effect of Asparaginase?

Back: Pancreatitis and coagulation factor deficiencies.

Front: Mechanism of action of Cytarabine (ara-C)?

Back: Pyrimidine analog that inhibits DNA polymerase.

Front: Primary toxicity of Cytarabine?

Back: Panpancytopenia (severe bone marrow suppression).

Front: Mechanism of action of Mercaptopurine (6-MP)?

Back: Purine analog that inhibits de novo purine synthesis (activated by HGPRT).

Reproductive & Specialized Endocrine

Front: Mechanism of action of Danazol?

Back: Synthetic androgen that acts as a partial agonist; used for Endometriosis.

Front: Side effect of Danazol?

Back: Weight gain, edema, and masculinization (hirsutism).

Front: Mechanism of action of Letrozole?

Back: Aromatase inhibitor used in ER-positive breast cancer and to induce ovulation in PCOS.

Front: Mechanism of action of Cabergoline/Bromocriptine?

Back: Dopamine agonists used to treat prolactinomas.

Critical Toxicology: Venoms & Environmentals

Front: Treatment for a bite from a Black Widow spider?

Back: Muscle relaxants (Benzodiazepines) and antivenom if severe.

Front: Clinical sign of Ciguatera toxin (found in large fish)?

Back: Reversal of hot and cold sensations.

Front: Treatment for Scorpion stings?

Back: Supportive care and specific antivenom (Anascorp) for severe cases.

Front: Mechanism of Scombroid poisoning?

Back: Bacterial histidine decarboxylase converts histidine to histamine in spoiled fish.

Back: Bisphosphonates, Tetracyclines, and NSAIDs.

Back: Metronidazole, certain Cephalosporins (Cefotetan), and Sulfonylureas.

Back: Sodium Valproate, Isoniazid, Cimetidine, Ketoconazole, Fluconazole, Alcohol (Acute), Chloramphenicol, Erythromycin, Sulfonamides, Ciprofloxacin, Omeprazole, Metronidazole.

Back: Misoprostol.

Front: Mechanism of action of Sacubitril/Valsartan (ARNI) in heart failure?

Back: Combined inhibition of neprilysin (increasing ANP/BNP) and blockade of AT1 receptors.
