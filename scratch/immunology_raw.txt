Back: RANKL; used to treat Osteoporosis.

XXXV. "Classic" Board Integration (First Aid Style)

Front: Mnemonic for the 4 Ts of Type IV Hypersensitivity?

Back: T-cells, Transplant (Rejection), TB (PPD), Touching (Contact Dermatitis).

Front: Differentiation between "Opsonization" and "Neutralization"?

Back: Opsonization marks for phagocytosis; Neutralization blocks binding to host cells.

Front: Part of the IgA molecule that prevents its degradation by proteases?

Back: Secretory Component.

Front: Major antibody isotype found in the primary (early) immune response?

Back: IgM.

Front: Major antibody isotype found in the secondary (amnestic) immune response?

Back: IgG.

Front: Location of "Margination" in the inflammatory process?

Back: Blood vessel wall (leukocytes move to the periphery).

Front: Proteins responsible for leukocyte "Rolling"?

Back: E-selectin and P-selectin on endothelium; Sialyl-Lewis X on leukocytes.

Front: Proteins responsible for leukocyte "Tight Adhesion"?

Back: ICAM-1 on endothelium; LFA-1 (Integrin) on leukocytes.

Front: Protein responsible for leukocyte "Diapedesis" (Transmigration)?

Back: PECAM-1 (CD31).

XXXVI. Final High-Yield Nuggets

Front: Name for the specialized macrophages in the Liver?

Back: Kupffer cells.

Front: Name for the specialized macrophages in the Brain?

Back: Microglia.

Front: Name for the specialized macrophages in the Lung?

Back: Alveolar macrophages (Dust cells).

Front: Function of "Properdin" in the complement system?

Back: Stabilizes C3 convertase (C3bBb) in the Alternative pathway.

Front: Result of "C3 deficiency"?

Back: Increased susceptibility to Type III hypersensitivity and recurrent pyogenic infections.

Front: Primary cytokine involved in "Class Switching" to IgA?

Back: TGF-$\beta$ (and IL-5 in some species).

Front: Cytokine that induces the production of "Acute Phase Reactants" from the liver?

Back: IL-6.

Front: HLA association: B51?

Back: Behcet disease.

Front: HLA association: DR4?

Back: Rheumatoid arthritis (Mnemonic: 4 walls in a "Rheum").

Front: HLA association: A3?

Back: Hemochromatosis.

Front: HLA association: B*5701?

Back: Abacavir hypersensitivity.

Front: HLA association: DR5?

Back: Hashimoto thyroiditis and Pernicious anemia.

XXXVIII. Advanced Intracellular Signaling

Front: Protein that acts as a "molecular switch" in TCR signaling by cycling between GTP and GDP?

Back: Ras.

Front: The three primary pathways activated by the TCR complex?

Back: MAP kinase (Ras/Raf), Calcium-Calmodulin (NFAT), and PKC-$\theta$ (NF-$\kappa$B).

Front: Enzyme inhibited by Cyclosporine and Tacrolimus?

Back: Calcineurin (a phosphatase).

Front: Target of the drug Sirolimus (Rapamycin) within the cell?

Back: mTOR (Mammalian Target of Rapamycin).

Front: Role of DAG (Diacylglycerol) in T-cell activation?

Back: Activates Protein Kinase C (PKC).

XXXIX. Mucosal & Cutaneous Immunity

Front: Specialized epithelial cells that transport IgA across the basement membrane?

Back: Enterocytes (via the Poly-Ig receptor).

Front: Primary antibody isotype in the urogenital and respiratory tracts?

Back: Secretory IgA.

Front: Major cytokine responsible for "Oral Tolerance"?

Back: TGF-$\beta$.

Front: Name of the dendritic cells found specifically in the epidermis?

Back: Langerhans cells.

Front: Characteristic electron microscopy finding in Langerhans cells?

Back: Birbeck granules (tennis racket shape).

XL. Tumor Immunology & Immunotherapy

Front: Mechanism of "Immune Subversion" by tumors via TGF-$\beta$?

Back: Induction of Tregs and inhibition of CTL/NK cell activity.

Front: Two common tumor-associated antigens used in clinical monitoring (not screening)?

Back: CEA (Carcinoembryonic antigen) and PSA (Prostate-specific antigen).

Front: Class of drugs that include Ipilimumab?

Back: Checkpoint inhibitors (specifically Anti-CTLA-4).

Front: Target of Nivolumab and Pembrolizumab?

Back: PD-1 (Programmed Cell Death Protein 1).

Front: Primary cytokine used in the treatment of Renal Cell Carcinoma and Melanoma?

Back: IL-2 (Aldesleukin).

XLI. Laboratory "High-Yields"

Front: Test used to identify the specific HLA alleles of a transplant patient?

Back: HLA Typing (PCR or Flow Cytometry).

Front: Significance of a "Positive Crossmatch" in transplantation?

Back: Recipient has preformed antibodies against donor HLA; contraindication to transplant.

Front: Difference between "Direct" and "Indirect" Immunofluorescence?

Back: Direct uses a labeled primary antibody; Indirect uses a labeled secondary antibody.

Front: Diagnostic use of Nitroblue Tetrazolium (NBT) reduction?

Back: Assessment of the oxidative burst in neutrophils (remains yellow in CGD).

XLII. Final Clinical Integration

Front: Mechanism of "Molecular Mimicry" in Rheumatic Fever?

Back: Antibodies against Group A Strep M-protein cross-react with cardiac myosin.

Front: Why do patients with IgA deficiency have an increased risk of anaphylaxis during blood transfusions?

Back: They may have anti-IgA antibodies (IgE class) that react to IgA in the donor blood.

Front: Definition of "Hapten"?

Back: A small molecule that is only immunogenic when attached to a large carrier protein.

Front: Why are patients with "Early" complement deficiencies (C1, C2, C4) at risk for SLE?

Back: Impaired clearance of apoptotic cells and immune complexes.

Front: Primary immune response to a "Superantigen" (toxic shock)?

Back: Massive release of IFN-$\gamma$ and IL-2 (T-cells) and TNF-$\alpha$ and IL-1 (Macrophages).

Front: Location of "T-cell independent" B-cell activation?

Back: Splenic marginal zone (responses to polysaccharide capsules).

Front: Most common cause of death in patients with SCID (if untreated)?

Back: Opportunistic infections (e.g., Pneumocystis jirovecii or Candida) in the first year of life.

Front: Primary mediator of the "Anamnestic" (memory) response?

Back: Memory B and T cells.

Front: Enzyme that creates the "N-nucleotide" diversity during VDJ recombination?

Back: TdT (Terminal Deoxynucleotidyl Transferase).

Front: Primary clinical use of Rho(D) Immune Globulin (RhoGAM)?

Back: Prevents Hemolytic Disease of the Newborn by opsonizing fetal RBCs in maternal circulation.
