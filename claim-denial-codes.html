What Are Claim Denial Codes?

Claim denial codes are used by insurance companies to explain why a medical claim was denied or not fully paid. Understanding these codes is the first step toward successfully appealing a denial and reducing your out-of-pocket costs.

These codes help identify issues such as:

  • Missing or incomplete patient information
  • Incorrect billing or CPT codes (Coding Errors)
  • Coverage limitations or out-of-network services
  • Policy restrictions and medical necessity denials

The two main types you will see on your EOB (Explanation of Benefits) are CARC (Claim Adjustment Reason Codes) and RARC (Remittance Advice Remark Codes).

Common Claim Denial Codes

Some of the most frequently occurring claim denial codes include:

  • CO-16 – Missing or incorrect information
  • CO-45 – Charges exceed fee schedule (Contractual obligation)
  • CO-97 – Service included in another procedure (Bundling issue)
  • PR-1 – Deductible amount remains (Patient responsibility)

What to Do If Your Claim Is Denied

Denials aren't final. If your insurance claim is denied, follow these 5 steps:

  • Review the denial code: Use our tool to understand the specific reason for denial.
  • Verify CPT codes: Cross-check your bill using our CPT code lookup tool.
  • Check coverage: Contact your insurer to clarify policy limitations.
  • Contact provider: Often, denials arise from simple billing office clerical errors.
  • File an Appeal: If the denial is unjustified, submit a formal dispute or appeal.

Pro Tip: Understanding the denial code is the first step to resolving the issue and getting your medical costs covered.
