Frequently Asked Questions

What sources does MediHelp use for global health statistics?
MediHelp uses exclusively authoritative global health organizations: WHO Global Health Observatory (GHO), International Diabetes Federation (IDF Diabetes Atlas 11th Edition), IARC Global Cancer Observatory (GLOBOCAN 2024), World Heart Federation (World Heart Report 2025), IHME Global Burden of Disease, CDC, NCD-RisC, and Our World in Data. All data points include full source attribution.
How often is the data updated?
Data is updated whenever source organizations release new reports or editions. The IDF Diabetes Atlas is published biennially, GLOBOCAN is updated every 2–3 years, WHO GHO indicators are updated annually, and CDC data is released yearly. Each statistics page displays the lastUpdated date prominently.
Can I cite MediHelp's health statistics?
Yes, but we recommend citing the primary source directly. Each statistic on MediHelp includes its original source (e.g., "IDF Diabetes Atlas 11th Edition, 2024"). For academic or journalistic use, cite the primary organization's publication. You may reference MediHelp as an aggregation tool that directed you to the primary source.
Are these statistics free to access?
Yes. All health statistics pages on MediHelp are completely free to access with no registration required. MediHelp's mission is to make authoritative health information accessible to patients, students, researchers, and healthcare professionals worldwide.
What future statistics categories will MediHelp add?
We plan to add: Stroke Statistics, Mental Health Statistics, Blood Pressure Statistics, Kidney Disease Statistics, Asthma & COPD Statistics, Smoking Statistics, Child Health Statistics, Women's & Men's Health Statistics, and Global Mortality Statistics. The architecture is modular, so new categories can be added without code changes.
