# Why Was My Insurance Claim Denied? (Reasons & How to Fix It)

Getting a medical bill is stressful — but seeing your insurance claim denied makes it even worse. Many patients don’t understand why their claim was rejected, and end up paying out-of-pocket when they shouldn’t.

The good news? Most claim denials can be understood — and often fixed. In this guide, you’ll learn why insurance claims get denied and what you can do about it.

![Managing denied insurance claims](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1579621970563-ebec7560ff3e?w=1200&q=80&auto=format&fit=crop)

## What does a claim denial mean?

A claim denial means your insurance company refused to pay for a medical service — either fully or partially. This doesn’t always mean the charge is valid; it often means there’s an issue with billing, coding, or coverage.

## Most common reasons for claim denial

### 1) Missing or incorrect information (CO-16)
If your claim has incomplete or incorrect details, it may be denied. This includes missing patient information, incorrect provider details, or wrong billing data.

### 2) Incorrect CPT codes
Medical procedures are billed using specific 5-digit CPT codes. If the wrong code is used (intentional or accidental), your claim may be rejected or paid incorrectly.

👉 You can verify codes using our [free CPT code lookup tool](https://www.medihelp.live/cpt-code-search) to ensure your provider used the correct procedure code.

### 3) Services not covered
Some treatments may not be included in your insurance plan. This could include out-of-network providers, experimental treatments, or specific policy exclusions.

### 4) Duplicate claims
If the same service is billed twice (a common clerical error), your insurance will automatically deny the second entry as a duplicate.

### 5) Late claim submission
Insurance companies have strict "timely filing" deadlines. If a doctor’s office submits a claim too late, it may be automatically denied regardless of medical necessity.

### 6) Medical necessity issues
Your insurer may decide a treatment wasn’t "medically necessary." This is one of the most common — and most disputed — reasons for denial.

## How to understand your denial code

Insurance companies use a standardized system of codes on your Explanation of Benefits (EOB):
- **CARC codes**: The primary reason for denial.
- **RARC codes**: Additional detailed explanation.

## How to fix a denied claim

### Step 1: Review the denial code
Understand the exact reason for denial using the lookup tool mentioned above.

### Step 2: Check your medical bill
Look for errors, incorrect charges, or coding issues that might have triggered the denial.

👉 Use our [medical bill audit tool](https://www.medihelp.live/medical-bill-audit) to run a automated check for billing errors.

### Step 3: Verify CPT codes
Ensure the code billed matches the procedure you received. Incorrect coding is a major cause of total denials.

### Step 4: Contact your provider
Often the issue is a simple billing mistake that the provider’s office can fix and resubmit without you needing to file a formal appeal.

### Step 5: File an appeal
If the denial is incorrect and the provider cannot fix it, you must submit an appeal. Provide supporting documents, medical records, and follow your insurer’s specific process.

## Can you still save money?
**Yes.** Many denied claims are corrected, resubmitted, and eventually reduced or fully paid. Taking proactive action can significantly lower your final bill.

![Success in patient advocacy](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1550751827-4bd374c3f58b?w=1200&q=80&auto=format&fit=crop)

## Common mistakes to avoid
- **Ignoring denial notices**: They don't go away on their own.
- **Paying immediately**: Never pay a bill that had services denied until you investigate why.
- **Not understanding denial codes**: Knowledge is your best weapon in a dispute.
- **Missing appeal deadlines**: Most plans only give you 60-180 days to dispute a denial.

## Final Thought
A denied claim doesn’t mean you have to pay everything. Understanding the reason—and taking the right steps—can save you thousands. **Always investigate before paying.**
