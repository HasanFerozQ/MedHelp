# First mRNA Flu Vaccine: What Adults 50+ Should Know

Flu season is starting early in the United States this year — and for the first time, there is a new kind of flu shot on offer. **mFlusiva (mRNA-1010)**, from Moderna, is the first **mRNA flu vaccine** ever licensed in the US. The Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approved it on August 5, 2026, for adults aged 50 and older, and it is available for the 2026–27 respiratory virus season. In the large trial behind the approval, it prevented about a quarter more flu cases than a standard flu shot.

In a phase 3 trial of more than 40,000 adults aged 50 and older, it was 26.6% more effective than a standard-dose flu vaccine at preventing lab-confirmed flu. It is approved only for adults 50 and older, side effects such as a sore arm, fatigue and headache were more common than with standard flu shots, and the CDC has not yet issued a recommendation for it — so talk to your doctor or pharmacist before choosing it.

## What Is mFlusiva? How an mRNA Flu Vaccine Works

Every flu shot you have had until now was made the old way: grown in chicken eggs, grown in cell culture, or built from recombinant protein. mFlusiva works differently. It contains messenger RNA (mRNA) — a set of instructions that tells your own cells to build a flu protein (hemagglutinin), triggering an immune response. There is no live flu virus in the shot, and no egg-based production.

That matters for two reasons. First, mRNA vaccines can be designed and manufactured faster than egg-grown vaccines, which take months and can pick up mutations while the virus adapts to growing in eggs — one reason standard flu shots sometimes match the circulating virus imperfectly. Second, the platform proved itself at scale during the COVID-19 pandemic, which is why regulators, doctors and exam boards are all watching this approval closely.

The road to approval was not smooth. In February 2026, the FDA initially refused even to review Moderna's application, citing concerns about the trial design. Two weeks later the agency reversed course and accepted the filing for priority review. On June 18, 2026, the FDA's Vaccines and Related Biological Products Advisory Committee (VRBPAC) voted 9–0 in favour, and the FDA approved the vaccine on August 5, 2026.

## The Trial Behind the Approval

The approval rests on a phase 3 trial enrolling more than 40,000 adults aged 50 and older, comparing mFlusiva against a licensed standard-dose flu vaccine (GlaxoSmithKline's Fluarix).

**Key results at a glance:**

- **Relative efficacy:** mFlusiva was **26.6% more effective** than the standard-dose vaccine at preventing lab-confirmed influenza — 26.1% in adults aged 50 to 64, and 27.4% in adults 65 and older.
- **Absolute numbers:** lab-confirmed flu occurred in **2.0%** of people who received mFlusiva versus **2.8%** of those who received the standard shot. The relative figure sounds large; the absolute difference is smaller, which is normal for flu vaccines — most vaccinated people would not have caught flu either way.
- **Older adults:** in a separate comparison, mFlusiva produced **stronger antibody responses than Sanofi's high-dose flu vaccine** in adults aged 65 and older.
- **Approval split:** the FDA granted **traditional approval for ages 50 to 64** and **accelerated approval for ages 65 and older**, where the decision rested on antibody levels rather than proven prevention of illness. Moderna must complete a confirmatory study in the older group, with the final report due in 2030.

**Safety — the trade-off to know about:** side effects were more common with mFlusiva than with standard flu shots. Sore arm, fatigue and headache were the most frequently reported reactions, and severe whole-body reactions that interfered with daily activity were more frequent than with the standard shot. Most reactions were short-lived, but anyone choosing this vaccine should expect a day or two of feeling rough more often than with a traditional flu shot.

## What This Means for Patients

**Who can receive it?** Adults aged 50 and older. mFlusiva is **not approved for anyone under 50**, and it does not replace the standard, high-dose or recombinant flu vaccines, which remain licensed, available and recommended.

**Should you ask for it by name?** That depends on what matters to you. If you are 50 or older and want the best-measured protection against flu this season, the trial data favour mFlusiva — but it also brings a higher chance of a sore arm and a day of fatigue. If you have had strong reactions to vaccines before, or you simply want the shot with the longest track record, a standard or high-dose flu vaccine is still a good choice. Any flu shot is far better than none.

**One important gap: the CDC has not recommended it yet.** As of October 2026, mFlusiva had no formal recommendation from the CDC or its Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices (ACIP), and the CDC's interim 2026–27 flu guidance does not mention it. That does not mean the vaccine is unsafe — the FDA approved it — but it does mean your doctor or pharmacist may not stock it, and insurance coverage may vary. Availability is also limited this season: Moderna missed the contracting cycle for the 2026 US flu season, so not every clinic or pharmacy will carry it. Ask ahead before making a special trip.

## Why Flu Shots Needed a Rethink

Flu is not a minor illness. According to preliminary CDC data, last fall and winter brought at least **390,000 flu hospitalizations and 24,000 deaths** in the US — the highest levels of respiratory illness in more than 25 years. And standard flu vaccines are only moderately effective: CDC data show a median effectiveness of about **39.5% over the past 15 flu seasons**.

This season, flu is arriving early. Johns Hopkins reported in early November 2026 that flu activity is low but increasing, particularly in the western and southern US, and called this "a good last call to get your influenza vaccination for this year, because vaccines work best when taken before the influenza season starts." The CDC expects a season similar to last year's, and a new mRNA flu vaccine option is now available for people 50 and older.

    <p>mRNA vaccines deliver messenger RNA in a lipid nanoparticle. Host cells translate it into viral protein (here, influenza hemagglutinin), which is presented on MHC-I and MHC-II, activating both CD8+ cytotoxic T cells and CD4+ helper T cells plus antibody production. There is no live virus and no integration into host DNA — a favourite exam distractor.</p>

    <h4>📚 Flu Vaccine Platforms (Step 1 / Step 2 CK)</h4>
    <p>Know the four platforms: inactivated (egg- or cell-grown), live-attenuated nasal spray (contraindicated in pregnancy, immunocompromise and asthma), recombinant protein, and now mRNA. Live-attenuated vaccines are contraindicated in immunocompromised patients; inactivated and mRNA vaccines are not live.</p>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is mFlusiva?**
Moderna's mRNA-based seasonal flu vaccine (mRNA-1010), the first mRNA flu vaccine licensed in the US. The FDA approved it on August 5, 2026, for adults aged 50 and older.

**How well does it work?**
In a phase 3 trial of more than 40,000 adults, it was 26.6% more effective than a standard-dose flu vaccine at preventing lab-confirmed flu (2.0% vs. 2.8% of participants caught flu).

**Who can get it?**
Adults aged 50 and older only. It is not approved for children or younger adults, who should receive a standard, recombinant or nasal-spray flu vaccine as appropriate.

**What are the side effects?**
Sore arm, fatigue and headache were more common than with standard flu shots, and severe reactions that interfered with daily activity were more frequent. Most reactions were short-lived.

**Why hasn't the CDC recommended it?**
As of October 2026, the CDC and its advisory committee (ACIP) had not issued a recommendation for mFlusiva, and the CDC's interim 2026–27 flu guidance does not mention it. FDA approval and CDC recommendation are separate steps.

**Can I get it this season?**
Possibly — it is available for the 2026–27 season, but supply is limited and not every clinic or pharmacy stocks it. Call ahead to check.
