# FDA Approves Wilserdly for Early Breast Cancer

On November 23, 2026, the FDA approved **Wilserdly (giredestrant)** — the first new breast cancer hormone therapy for early-stage disease in more than 20 years. The once-daily oral tablet is for adults with estrogen receptor–positive, HER2-negative (ER+/HER2−) breast cancer — the most common subtype — whose cancer carries an intermediate to high risk of coming back after surgery. In the phase 3 trial behind the approval, Wilserdly cut the risk of invasive recurrence or death by 30% compared with the hormone therapies patients already receive.

## What Is Wilserdly? A New Breast Cancer Hormone Therapy

Wilserdly belongs to a class of medicines called oral estrogen receptor antagonists — also known as oral SERDs (selective estrogen receptor degraders). In ER-positive breast cancer, estrogen binds to receptors on cancer cells and signals them to grow. Wilserdly blocks that process and breaks down the receptors, cutting off a key driver of tumor growth.

The mechanism is not entirely new — the injectable SERD fulvestrant has been used for years in advanced breast cancer — but Wilserdly is the first oral drug in this class to show a benefit in early-stage disease, and the first new hormone therapy for early-stage breast cancer in more than two decades. Standard after-surgery hormone therapy has long meant tamoxifen or an aromatase inhibitor (anastrozole, letrozole, or exemestane), taken daily for 5 years. Wilserdly gives higher-risk patients a new option in that same after-surgery window, when the goal is to keep the cancer from coming back. The approval was granted to Genentech, a member of the Roche Group, and received Priority Review from the FDA.

## The lidERA Trial: What the Evidence Showed

The approval rests on the randomized, multicenter **lidERA Breast Cancer trial (NCT04961996)**. It enrolled 4,170 adults with ER-positive, HER2-negative, stage I, II, or III breast cancer at intermediate to high risk of recurrence after surgery, assigned to Wilserdly or standard hormone therapy — tamoxifen or an aromatase inhibitor — for up to 5 years.

**Key results at a glance:**

- **Recurrence or death:** Wilserdly reduced the risk of invasive breast cancer recurrence or death by **30%** — 140 patients on Wilserdly experienced an event during follow-up, versus 196 on standard therapy.
- **Disease-free at 3 years:** **92.4%** of patients taking Wilserdly were alive and free of invasive breast cancer, compared with **89.6%** on standard hormone therapy — about 28 more patients out of every 1,000 alive and free of invasive disease.
- **Distant recurrence:** Patients receiving Wilserdly also had a lower risk of the cancer returning in another part of the body.
- **First of its kind:** lidERA is the first phase 3 trial to demonstrate a benefit with an oral estrogen receptor antagonist in early-stage breast cancer.

One question remains open: whether Wilserdly helps patients live longer. Patients are still being followed, and Genentech has agreed to submit overall survival results to the FDA as a postmarketing commitment.

## Who Can Receive Wilserdly?

The approval covers adults with estrogen receptor–positive, HER2-negative breast cancer, stage I, II, or III, with an intermediate to high risk of recurrence following surgery. A few practical points from the FDA announcement: premenopausal women should also receive an additional hormone-suppressing medicine alongside Wilserdly, and men may also receive it, as determined by their healthcare provider — about 2,670 new breast cancer cases in men are expected in the US in 2026.

Wilserdly is only for the ER-positive, HER2-negative subtype. HER2-positive disease and triple-negative disease are treated differently, and an oncologist will confirm the subtype from the pathology report before recommending any hormone therapy.

This is not the drug's first approval. On November 16, 2026 — one week earlier — the FDA approved it in combination with everolimus for certain adults with ER-positive, HER2-negative, ESR1-mutated advanced or metastatic breast cancer whose disease recurred or progressed during or after prior endocrine therapy. That approval was based on the evERA Breast Cancer trial (NCT05306340).

## Side Effects to Know About

The most common side effects (affecting at least 10% of patients) are muscle and joint pain, hot flashes, tiredness, headache, nausea, slower-than-normal heart rate, dizziness, and numbness or tingling in the hands and feet. Common blood test changes include increased liver enzymes, lower blood counts, high blood fat levels, and changes in mineral levels and kidney test results. Wilserdly may harm a developing baby if taken during pregnancy.

As with any 5-year course of hormone therapy, the side-effect profile is part of the decision — hot flashes and joint pain are the familiar trade-offs of estrogen-blocking treatment. Patients who struggle with them should tell their care team rather than stopping on their own, because stopping early raises recurrence risk.

## What This Means for Patients

**A new option where the standard had not changed in 20 years.** For two decades, the after-surgery hormone therapy menu was tamoxifen or an aromatase inhibitor. Wilserdly is the first addition to that menu — and the first oral SERD proven to work in early-stage disease. For patients at intermediate to high risk of recurrence, a 30% relative risk reduction is a meaningful improvement on the standard of care.

**It is a pill, not an infusion or injection.** Wilserdly is taken once daily by mouth for up to 5 years — no clinic visits for injections, no infusion chair. The trade-off is adherence: a daily pill only works if it is taken every day for years.

**The survival question is still open.** The trial proved Wilserdly keeps cancer from coming back better than standard therapy. Whether it also extends life is not yet known; the follow-up data will tell. That is normal for adjuvant trials, but worth knowing when weighing a switch.

**Cost and coverage will decide who actually gets it.** Wilserdly is a new branded cancer drug, the kind that typically arrives with prior authorization, high list prices, and complex billing. Before starting, ask your insurer whether it is covered under the medical or pharmacy benefit, what your share will be, and what patient-assistance programmes Genentech offers.     <p>Three ways to block estrogen signalling: SERMs (tamoxifen) block the receptor in breast but act as agonists elsewhere; aromatase inhibitors (letrozole, anastrozole, exemestane) stop estrogen production and only work after menopause; SERDs (fulvestrant, now oral giredestrant) bind the receptor and cause its degradation. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is Wilserdly?**
Giredestrant (Wilserdly), from Genentech/Roche, is a once-daily oral estrogen receptor antagonist — an oral SERD — approved by the FDA on November 23, 2026, for early-stage ER-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer after surgery.

**How is Wilserdly different from tamoxifen?**
Tamoxifen is a SERM: it blocks the estrogen receptor in breast tissue but can act like estrogen elsewhere. Wilserdly is a SERD: it binds the receptor and causes it to be broken down, shutting off estrogen signalling more completely.

**How well does Wilserdly work?**
In the 4,170-patient lidERA trial, Wilserdly reduced invasive recurrence or death by 30% versus tamoxifen or an aromatase inhibitor. At 3 years, 92.4% of Wilserdly patients were alive and free of invasive breast cancer, versus 89.6% on standard therapy.

**Who can take Wilserdly?**
Adults with ER-positive, HER2-negative, stage I–III breast cancer at intermediate to high risk of recurrence after surgery. Premenopausal women need an additional hormone-suppressing medicine with it.

**Is Wilserdly a cure?**
No. It is adjuvant therapy — treatment given after surgery to reduce the chance the cancer returns. It does not replace surgery, and whether it extends overall survival is still being studied.
