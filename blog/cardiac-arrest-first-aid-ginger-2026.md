More than **70% of all cardiac arrests occur at home** — not in hospitals, not near paramedics. In many parts of the world, an ambulance may be 15, 20, even 30 minutes away. In those minutes, one person's knowledge — or lack of it — determines whether someone lives or dies.

## Recognize It: Cardiac Arrest vs. Heart Attack

A **heart attack** means blood flow to the heart is blocked — the person is usually conscious and breathing. A **cardiac arrest** means the heart has stopped pumping altogether — the person collapses, loses consciousness, and stops breathing normally.

### Signs of Cardiac Arrest
*   **Sudden Unresponsiveness:** No response to tapping or shouting.
*   **No Normal Breathing:** Not breathing or making only gasping "agonal" breaths.
*   **Blue/Grey Skin:** Cyanosis around the face or lips.
*   **Fixed, Dilated Pupils:** Sign of oxygen deprivation to the brain.

## The Critical First 30 Seconds

1.  **Confirm Unresponsiveness:** Tap shoulders and shout.
2.  **Call for Help:** Assign someone to call emergency services.
3.  **Position the Person:** Lay them flat on a **hard surface** (not a bed).
4.  **Check Breathing:** Look for chest rise for no more than 10 seconds.
