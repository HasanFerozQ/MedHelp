# Russia Administers Its First Cancer Vaccine to a Human Patient

On April 1, 2026, doctors at the **National Medical Research Center for Radiology** in Russia administered the country's first domestically developed personalized mRNA cancer vaccine — known as **NeoOncovac** — to a patient. The recipient, a 60-year-old man from Russia's Kursk Region, had been diagnosed with stage three cutaneous (skin) melanoma with metastasis.

## What Is NeoOncovac?
**NeoOncovac** is a *personalized* mRNA-based therapeutic cancer vaccine. Unlike traditional preventive vaccines (like those for measles or influenza), it is not designed to prevent cancer — it is designed to treat existing cancer by training the immune system to attack tumor cells.

Developed collaboratively by the **Gamaleya National Research Center for Epidemiology and Microbiology**, NeoOncovac encodes tumor-specific neoantigens into mRNA, which is then delivered into the patient's body via **lipid nanoparticles**.

![Medical Lab](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1576671081837-49000212a370?w=900&q=80)

## Why Melanoma? 
Melanoma cells typically carry a high number of somatic mutations, meaning they generate an abundance of neoantigens — the unique cancer-specific proteins that NeoOncovac is designed to encode. The greater the number of neoantigens, the more opportunities the immune system has to mount a robust and specific attack against the tumor.

### Key Facts
*   **Target**: Personalised mRNA-based therapeutic.
*   **Method**: Encodes tumor-specific neoantigens.
*   **Result**: 90% metastasis response in pre-clinical trials.

> "The vaccine specifically helps fight metastases." — Aleksandr Ginzburg, Gamaleya Research Institute

### The Road Ahead
The first patient will be monitored closely over the coming months. If successful, researchers hope to expand clinical use to pancreatic and lung cancers. This development represents a cornerstone in global health oncology.
