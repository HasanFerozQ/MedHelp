Roughly 12% of Americans are currently on a GLP-1 receptor agonist — a class of medications that includes Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, and Zepbound. These drugs have become transformative in treating Type 2 diabetes and obesity.

But as GLP-1 prescriptions have exploded, a quieter reckoning has begun in orthopedic medicine. Two major stories broke in the same week that together define where GLP-1 medicine stands in 2026: a large-scale study linking these drugs to elevated bone risks, and a biochemistry breakthrough that could make the next generation of these drugs safer.

## Why Would a Weight-Loss Drug Harm Bones?

Scientists suspect several interrelated mechanisms rather than a single direct drug effect on bone tissue:

1.  **Nutrient Deficiency:** Appetite suppression leads to lower intake of calcium, vitamin D, and protein.
2.  **Rapid Weight Loss:** Bones experience less mechanical stress as weight drops quickly, leading the body to reduce bone density.
3.  **Uric Acid Spikes:** Rapid weight loss releases purines that break down into uric acid, triggering gout.

## What GLP-1 Users Should Do Right Now

Despite the risk signals, experts are not calling for patients to abandon GLP-1 therapy. Instead, they recommend:

*   **DEXA Scan:** Ask your doctor for a bone density baseline if you are at high risk.
*   **Supplementation:** Proactively discuss calcium and vitamin D with your physician.
*   **Protein Intake:** Aim for at least 1.2g of protein per kg of body weight to preserve bone and muscle.
*   **Resistance Training:** Weight-bearing exercise is the single most effective tool for preserving bone density.
