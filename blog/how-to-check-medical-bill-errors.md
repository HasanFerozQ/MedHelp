# How to Check Your Medical Bill for Errors: A Step-by-Step Guide

Medical bills are confusing — and often wrong. Many patients are overcharged due to billing mistakes, incorrect codes, or insurance errors. If you don’t review your bill carefully, you could end up paying more than you should.

In this guide, you’ll learn how to check your medical bill for errors and avoid unnecessary costs.

![Reviewing medical billing paperwork](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1586281380117-5a60ae2050cc?w=1200&q=80&auto=format&fit=crop)

## What are medical billing errors?

Medical billing errors happen when charges on your bill are incorrect, duplicated, or inflated. Even small errors can significantly increase your total bill.

**Common mistakes include:**
- **Duplicate charges**: Billing the same service twice.
- **Incorrect CPT codes**: Using the wrong code for a procedure.
- **Vague charges**: "Service not received" or "Misc fees".
- **Unbundled procedures**: Billing components of a surgery separately instead of as a single package.
- **Insurance processing errors**: Miscalculations in co-pays or deductibles.

## Step-by-Step: How to check your medical bill

### 1) Review every charge line-by-line
Carefully go through each item on your bill. Do not just look at the total "Balance Due." Look for unknown charges, repeated services, or unexpected fees that don't match your memory of the visit.

### 2) Verify CPT codes
Each service is represented by a 5-digit CPT code. Incorrect codes (like "Upcoding") can increase your bill massively. 

👉 You can use our [free CPT code lookup tool](https://www.medihelp.live/cpt-code-search) to understand exactly what each charge means.

### 3) Check for duplicate charges
Hospitals sometimes bill the same service multiple times by mistake. Compare the dates, procedure names, and quantities side-by-side to ensure you aren't paying twice for one item.

![Analyzing hospital bill line items](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1633158829585-23ba8f7c8caf?w=1200&q=80&auto=format&fit=crop)

### 4) Look for "Upcoding"
Upcoding happens when a more expensive procedure code is used instead of the correct, lower-cost one. This is one of the most common ways patients are overcharged.

### 5) Compare prices
Some charges may be significantly higher than standard geographical rates. Check if the amount matches the service you actually received and whether the hospital is charging way above average.

### 6) Review insurance adjustments
Make sure your insurance was applied correctly. Look for denied claims, partial payments, and how your deductible was calculated. 

👉 If you see denial codes (like CO-16 or PR-1), use our [claim denial code lookup tool](https://www.medihelp.live/claim-denial-codes) to decode them instantly.
