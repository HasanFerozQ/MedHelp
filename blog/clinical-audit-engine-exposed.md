## The Three Most Common "Invisible" Errors

When you look at a medical bill, you see codes and numbers. But behind those codes are specific clinical rules that are often ignored by hospital billing software.

### 1. The "Level 5" Padding (Upcoding)
The most common error in Emergency Room (ER) visits is **Upcoding**. Professional coders often assign a Level 5 (CPT 99285) to a visit that only clinically justifies a Level 3. This single error can add over **$800** to your bill for the exact same amount of work.

### 2. The Unbundling Trap (NCCI Edits)
Hospitals sometimes "unbundle" services that should be billed together as a single package. For example, if you have a surgical procedure, the follow-up care or small supplies (like bandages or simple sutures) should often be included in the primary surgical fee. Billing them separately is a violation of **NCCI (National Correct Coding Initiative)** standards.

![Analyzing the Audit](https://plus.unsplash.com/premium_photo-1726769176212-1ab1fd60f42a?q=80&w=1170&auto=format&fit=crop)

### 3. Frequency Limit Violations (MUE)
The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) sets **Medically Unlikely Edits (MUE)**. These are limits on how many times a specific service can be billed in a single day. If a provider bills you for four blood draws when the limit is one, you are likely overpaying for redundant services.
