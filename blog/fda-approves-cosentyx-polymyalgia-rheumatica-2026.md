# FDA Approves Cosentyx for Polymyalgia Rheumatica

On November 17, 2026, the FDA approved **Cosentyx (secukinumab)** for polymyalgia rheumatica (PMR) — the first and only anti-IL-17 biologic approved for the condition. The approval covers adults whose PMR has not responded well enough to corticosteroids, or who cannot tolerate tapering off them. For a disease that has been treated almost entirely with steroids for decades, it is the first new kind of treatment option patients have had — and it arrives with trial results showing remission rates double those of steroids alone.

## What Is Polymyalgia Rheumatica?

Polymyalgia rheumatica is a common inflammatory disease in adults aged 50 and older. It causes acute pain and stiffness in the shoulders, neck, and hips — the kind of stiffness that makes getting out of bed, getting dressed, or lifting your arms difficult. Around 966,000 people in the United States live with it.

For decades, the standard treatment has been corticosteroids, usually prednisone. Steroids usually work quickly, which is why they have stayed the standard for so long. The problem is what comes after: relapses affect up to 40% of patients in the first year, and many people end up on steroids for months or years. Long-term steroid use carries significant risks — osteoporosis, cardiovascular disease, and diabetes among them — and for older adults, those risks weigh heavily. As Prof. John Stone of Harvard Medical School and Massachusetts General Hospital, the global lead investigator of the REPLENISH trial, put it: "Patients with PMR are at increased risk of poor outcomes related to steroids, and the prospect of months or years of additional steroids is disheartening."

## What Is Cosentyx? A New Polymyalgia Rheumatica Treatment

Cosentyx is a fully human biologic that blocks interleukin-17A (IL-17A), a cytokine involved in the inflammation behind several immune-mediated diseases. It is given as an injection under the skin. The 150 mg dose is the one approved for PMR.

This is Cosentyx's 11th approved use in the United States — it is already used for psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis, hidradenitis suppurativa, and other conditions, with more than a decade of clinical experience behind it. It was already approved for PMR in the European Union, and regulatory review is ongoing in Japan and other countries. What is new here is not the drug itself, but the disease it can now treat: PMR is the first condition in this approval where the alternative to steroids has been, effectively, more steroids.

## The REPLENISH Trial: What the Evidence Showed

The approval rests on the **REPLENISH trial (NCT05767034)**, a global phase 3, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study conducted across 27 countries. Patients were assigned to one of three arms — Cosentyx 300 mg, Cosentyx 150 mg, or placebo — all combined with a 24-week steroid tapering regimen. The results were published in the *New England Journal of Medicine* and presented at the 2026 European Congress of Rheumatology (EULAR) on June 3, 2026.

**Key results at a glance:**

- **Sustained remission:** 40.5% of patients on Cosentyx 150 mg achieved sustained remission at Week 52, versus 20.5% on placebo — double the rate, after a 24-week steroid taper.
- **Steroid-sparing:** The trial also measured the total steroid dose patients needed over the year and how quickly they needed rescue treatment — both key secondary endpoints, reflecting the real goal of PMR care: getting patients off steroids and keeping them off.
- **Complete remission:** The proportion reaching complete sustained remission at Week 52 was also a key secondary endpoint.

"For too long, treatment options beyond glucocorticoids have been limited," said Prof. Stone. "In the Phase III REPLENISH trial, secukinumab doubled the likelihood of sustained remission while helping patients taper off steroids, which should be the goal of PMR treatment."

## Who Can Receive Cosentyx for PMR?

The approval covers adults with polymyalgia rheumatica who have had an inadequate response to corticosteroids, or who cannot tolerate tapering their steroid dose. In practice, that means people stuck on steroids — relapsing when the dose drops, or accumulating the side effects of months of treatment.

It is not a first treatment for newly diagnosed PMR. Steroids remain the first step: they work fast, and many people do well on a taper. Cosentyx is for the point where steroids are not enough, or where staying on them is doing more harm than good. A rheumatologist will make that call, usually after seeing how a patient responds to the initial steroid course.

One related point worth knowing: PMR can occur alongside giant cell arteritis, a related inflammation of the blood vessels. New headaches, scalp tenderness, jaw pain when chewing, or any change in vision need urgent medical attention — those are not ordinary PMR symptoms.

## Side Effects to Know About

The safety profile in the PMR trial was consistent with Cosentyx's established profile across its other uses. Serious adverse events occurred in 15.9% of patients on Cosentyx 150 mg and 14.2% on placebo — close, but not identical, and the differences matter.

The most notable differences were hypersensitivity reactions — including eczema, dermatitis, rash, and hives — in 14% of Cosentyx patients versus 9% on placebo, and urinary tract infections in 13% versus 7%. One patient on Cosentyx had a serious urinary tract infection. A fatal case of cryptococcal meningitis — a rare fungal infection of the membranes around the brain — occurred in a patient treated with Cosentyx.

That last point is the one to take seriously. Cosentyx works by dampening part of the immune system, and like other biologics it raises the risk of infections, including uncommon ones. Patients starting it should be screened for infections, including tuberculosis, and should report fever, persistent cough, or any unusual symptoms promptly rather than waiting for the next appointment.

## What This Means for Patients

**A real alternative to endless steroids.** For the first time, PMR has a non-steroid treatment with phase 3 evidence behind it — and the headline result, doubling sustained remission, is the outcome patients care about most: staying well without the drug that is quietly damaging their bones, blood sugar, and heart.

**Tapering becomes the goal, not the hope.** The trial was designed around a 24-week steroid taper, and the steroid-sparing endpoints matter as much as the remission numbers. If you are on long-term prednisone for PMR, this approval gives your rheumatologist something concrete to discuss at the next visit — not just a lower steroid dose, but a plan to get off steroids altogether.

**It is a biologic, with biologic trade-offs.** Cosentyx is an injection, it suppresses part of the immune system, and it carries infection risks that steroids do not — including rare but serious ones. It is also a branded biologic, the kind of drug that typically arrives with prior authorization, high list prices, and complex billing. Before starting, ask your insurer whether it is covered under the medical or pharmacy benefit, what your share will be, and what patient-assistance programmes Novartis offers. And when the bills arrive, check every line: administration charges, coding errors, and duplicate charges are common with biologic treatment. **Movement still matters.** Medication is only part of living with PMR. Gentle movement, physiotherapy, and staying active protect the shoulders and hips that PMR stiffens — and they matter just as much once the inflammation is controlled as they did while it was active.

## The Bottom Line

Cosentyx is the first new kind of treatment for polymyalgia rheumatica in decades, and the first biologic approved for it. In the REPLENISH trial it doubled sustained remission to 40.5% while patients tapered off steroids — the outcome that matters most to the nearly one million Americans living with the disease. The trade-offs are real: infection risk, including rare serious infections, and the cost and complexity of a biologic. But for people stuck on steroids that are not working well enough, or who cannot come off them, it is the first genuine alternative they have had.
