1. A 74-year-old male named John presents to the resuscitation bay with sudden-onset substernal chest pressure radiating to the left arm. Vitals are BP 120/76 mmHg, HR 88 bpm, RR 20 rpm, and SpO2 96% on room air. The initial ECG reveals ST-elevation of 2.5 mm in leads I, aVL, V5, and V6 with reciprocal ST-depression in leads V1 and V2. Troponin is 9.8 ng/mL. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?
   - A) Discharge the patient home with sublingual nitroglycerin and outpatient cardiology follow-up within 1 week
   - B) Initiate intravenous thrombolysis with tenecteplase as first-line therapy, as PCI is reserved for patients with recurrent symptoms
   - C) Immediate activation of the cardiac catheterization laboratory for primary percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) and administration of aspirin and a P2Y12 inhibitor
   - D) Obtain a chest CT angiogram to rule out aortic dissection prior to initiating any antiplatelet therapy
   - E) Administer oral metoprolol 50 mg immediately and repeat the ECG in 4 hours
   - **Answer:** C
