High-Yield Clinical Calculators & Educational Models

Mastering clinical equations is a core requirement for USMLE, MBBS, and board certifications. However, memorizing formulas without understanding their physiological derivations is counterproductive. Our student learning portal provides standard clinical calculators with detailed formula breakdowns, step-by-step mathematical reasoning, and diagnostic criteria guides to help students bridge the gap between classroom theory and clinical practice.

Primary Learning & Study Tools

Our students' library incorporates step-by-step learning widgets:

  • Formula Breakdown Models: BMI, BSA, and Holliday-Segar fluid calculations displayed with raw fractions and scaling steps.
  • Step-by-Step ABG Interpretation: Breaks down pH, $pCO_2$, and $HCO_3^-$ values to teach acid-base compensations (Winter's formula).

BEDSIDE FAQs

What is Winter's Formula and how is it used clinically?

Winter's formula predicts the expected respiratory compensation ($pCO_2$) in patients with metabolic acidosis:

Expected pCO₂ = (1.5 × HCO₃⁻) + 8 ± 2
If the measured $pCO_2$ matches the predicted value, appropriate respiratory compensation is taking place. If the measured value is higher, a secondary respiratory acidosis co-exists; if lower, a secondary respiratory alkalosis is present.

Why does QTc interval need to be calculated?

The raw QT interval varies dramatically with heart rate. A faster heart rate shortens the QT interval, while a slower heart rate prolongs it. Clinicians use the Bazett equation to calculate the QTc (corrected QT) to normalize the interval to a heart rate of 60 bpm. A prolonged QTc (> 470 ms in males, > 480 ms in females) raises the risk for life-threatening arrhythmias like Torsades de Pointes.
