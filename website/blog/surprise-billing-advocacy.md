# Avoiding "Surprise Billing" — Your Rights under the No Surprises Act

Medical billing is often called the "hidden tax" of the modern world. You go in for a routine procedure, check that the hospital is in-network, and three weeks later, you receive a $2,400 bill from an out-of-network anesthesiologist you never even met.

## What is Surprise Billing?
**Surprise billing** (or balance billing) occurs when a patient receives care from an out-of-network provider at an in-network facility, or in an emergency situation where the patient has no choice in where they are taken.

### The No Surprises Act (NSA)
As of January 1, 2022, the federal **No Surprises Act** protects patients from these exact scenarios. The law generally prohibits:
1.  **Surprise bills for emergency services**, even if they are out-of-network.
2.  **Surprise out-of-network charges** for services provided at an in-network facility.
3.  **Balance billing** without your written consent.

## How to Dispute a Charge
If you receive a bill that seems like a "surprise," here are the steps you should take:

1.  **Check the "Consent" Form**: Did you sign a "Surprise Billing Protection Form"? If not, the charge is likely illegal.
2.  **Contact the Billing Department**: Reference the No Surprises Act and ask them to re-process the claim through your insurer at the in-network rate.
3.  **File an Appeal**: If the hospital refuses, file an appeal with your insurance provider. They are legally required to handle the dispute.
