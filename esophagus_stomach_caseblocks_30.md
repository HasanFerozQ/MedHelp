## CASE 01 — Heartburn That Won’t Quit
Scenario: A 46-year-old man has 2 years of heartburn and regurgitation worse after meals and when supine. He has taken OTC PPIs intermittently. Over the last 2 months he reports progressive dysphagia to solids and 10 lb (4.5 kg) unintentional weight loss.

Q1. Which is the best next diagnostic step?  
A. Empiric high-dose PPI for 8 weeks  
B. Upper endoscopy (EGD)  
C. Barium swallow only  
D. CT abdomen/pelvis without contrast  
Answer: B

Q2. If GERD is confirmed and surgery is being considered, which pre-op test best evaluates esophageal peristalsis to help choose a partial vs complete fundoplication?  
A. Esophageal manometry  
B. 24-hour pH monitoring  
C. Gastric emptying study  
D. Plain chest X-ray  
Answer: A

Q3. Which lifestyle change is most likely to reduce nocturnal reflux symptoms?  
A. Increase fat intake at dinner  
B. Eat meals closer to bedtime  
C. Elevate the head of the bed and avoid late meals  
D. Drink peppermint tea after dinner  
Answer: C

Q4. Which complication most strongly increases risk of distal esophageal adenocarcinoma?  
A. Esophageal candidiasis  
B. Barrett esophagus  
C. Zenker diverticulum  
D. Mallory-Weiss tear  
Answer: B
