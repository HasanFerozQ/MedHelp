## CASE 01 — The Crampy Post-Op Belly
Scenario: A 56-year-old woman with prior hysterectomy presents with 24 hours of crampy pain, vomiting, distension, and no flatus.

Q1. Most likely diagnosis is:  
A. Postoperative ileus  
B. Small bowel obstruction from adhesions  
C. Acute pancreatitis  
D. Perforated peptic ulcer  
Answer: B

Q2. Best initial management includes:  
A. Oral laxatives and discharge  
B. NPO, IV fluids/electrolyte correction, NG decompression as needed  
C. Immediate colonoscopy  
D. High-dose NSAIDs  
Answer: B

Q3. Which finding most suggests strangulation/ischemia and need for urgent operative evaluation?  
A. Mild tachycardia alone  
B. WBC 11,000/µL  
C. Peritonitis or severe continuous pain with fever/leukocytosis and metabolic acidosis  
D. History of prior surgery  
Answer: C

Q4. Best imaging to evaluate severity/cause in a stable adult is:  
A. KUB only  
B. CT abdomen/pelvis with IV contrast  
C. HIDA scan  
D. ERCP  
Answer: B
